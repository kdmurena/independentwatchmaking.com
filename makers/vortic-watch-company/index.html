Vortic Watch Company makes wristwatches from restored antique American pocket watch movements in Fort Collins, Colorado. The company describes itself as one of the only wristwatch manufacturers in the United States and builds its watches by hand. Its two young founders, whom the website does not name, were developing metal 3D-printed prototypes for a university design project at Penn State when they realised that many antique pocket watch movements had been separated from their precious-metal cases. They decided to restore the movements, reverse-engineered antique parts to build wristwatch cases that house them much as the original pocket watch cases did, and later moved to Fort Collins to work on the business full time. The legal entity is Vortic Holding Company, LLC. The website gives no founding date, but the brand cites press coverage in The New York Times (2015) and The Boston Globe (2016), and says it has been a business member of the NAWCC since 2016.

The core line is the American Artisan Series, in which everything inside the watch is an antique and everything outside is new. The pocket watch movements are found, salvaged and restored by hand, and the cases are made in Colorado. Because suitable pocket watches are hard to find and restore, Vortic says it produces only a couple of hundred watches per year, and each is one of a kind. The current collection groups the watches by the movement maker: the Springfield series uses Illinois Watch Company movements from 1913 to 1927, the Lancaster uses Hamilton and the Canton uses Hampden. They are hand-wound with 17 or 19 jewels, in 10-size or 12-size movements, with about 36 hours of power reserve. Cases of 43 mm or 45 mm are made of Grade 5 titanium or stainless steel with a sapphire crystal and exhibition caseback, are water resistant to 1 ATM, and come with a leather strap and a one-year warranty. The brand says it builds as much as possible in the United States and is building up its in-house capabilities over time.

The Military Edition is released once a year on Veterans Day, 11 November, and is built from salvaged pocket watches made for the U.S. Army Air Corps in the Second World War. The brand calls the series 'The Watch That Won The War'. The first edition came out in 2019, and the later editions and specials have included watches worn on screen in the series Jack Ryan and watches built on movements made for the Royal Air Force, the Royal Navy and the U.S. Navy. The Eighth Edition is limited to 40 pieces and uses 16-size Hamilton 4992B 'Master Navigational Watch' movements from 1942 to 1945 with 22 jewels and a 24-hour GCT dial. It has a 49 mm Grade 5 titanium case with a hand-distressed black DLC finish, a domed sapphire front crystal and a flat sapphire caseback, and 5 ATM water resistance. It is priced at USD 11,000 and ships with two straps by Greg Stevens Design in Utah. Since 2019 Vortic has donated USD 500 from each Military Edition sold to the Veterans Watchmaker Initiative, and expects total donations to exceed USD 200,000 with this edition.

Vortic also offers a service to convert a customer's own American pocket watch into a wristwatch, using an American Artisan Series or Railroad Edition case. It limits itself to fewer than 50 conversions per year, typically 4 to 6 months each, and says that it is currently at capacity and not accepting new submissions. The company also offers watchmaking and warranty services, and is the official watch of the Red Rocks Amphitheatre in Morrison, Colorado. Its watches are on display at the National Watch and Clock Museum in Columbia, Pennsylvania, and the Charles River Museum of Industry in Waltham, Massachusetts. Prices for the available watches on the website range from USD 4,200 to USD 5,100 for the American Artisan Series and USD 11,000 for the Military Edition.
