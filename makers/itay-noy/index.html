Itay Noy

Israel
Founded2000
LocationOld Jaffa, Tel Aviv, Israel
Price$3,000–8,000
Volume~120–150/yr
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Born in Israel and largely self-taught in watchmaking, Itay Noy is one of the most original and criminally underrated independent watchmakers in the world. He studied jewelry and object design at the Bezalel Academy of Art and Design in Jerusalem (BFA) and industrial design at the Design Academy Eindhoven (M.Des.), with a student exchange at the Gerrit Rietveld Academy in Amsterdam. After army service, he worked as a watch salesman before turning to independent creation. Since 2000, he has released one new collection per year — every design an exercise in breaking out of received perceptions of time. His studio in the Old City of Jaffa, one of the world's most ancient ports, is where every watch is assembled to order by hand.

Noy is one of approximately two dozen true independent watchmakers in the world — making 120 pieces per year, all original designs, using high-quality movements integrated with in-house dynamic dials, modules, and complications which he creates himself. Price: typically $3,000–8,000, offering remarkable accessibility for true independent work. Nine prizes including the Andy Prize for the Arts, the Israeli Ministry of Culture Prize, and the America-Israel Cultural Foundation Prize. His work is in permanent collections at the Israel Museum, Tel Aviv Museum of Art, the Charles Bronfman Collection (New York), and the Droog Collection (Amsterdam). Lecturer at Bezalel Academy since 2005. Collections explore concepts of duality, memory, identity, cultural signs, logic vs. chaos, and the nature of time itself. 'I don't want to remain the only independent watch designer in Israel!'

The collections themselves are small philosophical machines. City Squares renders elusive cityscapes on the dial; Fractal breaks time into shattered dimensions; Duality displays simultaneous dichotomies; and Night Flight — developed with a fourth-generation Taiwanese lacquer artist — builds each unique dial from nighttime aerial photographs of world cities, mixing lacquer with 24k gold for a glimmering, three-dimensional depth like city lights seen from a plane, complete with an exhibition caseback shaped like an aircraft window, all on a hand-wound 6497-1 in a 44 mm black PVD case. The Color Wheel pair reimagined his Time Tone and Full Month concepts as rainbow-graded displays limited to 24 examples each — the Full Month running a micro-rotor movement in a case just over 6 mm tall — while the Time Tone's customised hand-wound calibre typifies his method: sourced Swiss bases transformed by in-house modules, dials, and finishing until they answer to no one's design language but his own. Everything is made or modified largely by Noy himself in the Jaffa studio, from cases and dials to movement work, and every watch is ordered directly from the maker with its number on the dial. Press coverage settles reliably on the same conclusions: that his whimsical complications force the wearer to see time in genuinely different ways, that the watchmaking content far exceeds the asking prices, and that even collectors who would never wear one should study them. A quarter-century into the one-collection-per-year discipline, Noy has quietly assembled one of the most sustained bodies of conceptual work in modern horology — an artist's catalogue raisonné that happens to tell the time.
