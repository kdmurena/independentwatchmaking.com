Alain Silberstein

France

Alain Silberstein is a French architect and designer rather than a watchmaker in the traditional sense, and he is catalogued here for the extent of his influence and the breadth of his collaborations with watchmakers over more than three decades. He trained in interior architecture and design in Paris and spent roughly fifteen years running an architecture and design practice with his wife, first in Paris and then in Besançon, France's watchmaking capital. He says he began designing watches after the Swatch appeared, first for Hong Kong makers of inexpensive watches and then for a luxury piece, and that around 1984–85 he started to learn about mechanical movements, which he valued for their durability.

He presented his first collection at the 1987 Basel fair, built around the Krono Bauhaus, and went on to make watches under his own name from Besançon. Sources date the founding of the brand differently, as 1987 or 1990, and his own site refers to a 40th anniversary next year, which points to 1987. His language comes from the Bauhaus and from Kandinsky: a red triangle, blue square and yellow circle, and hands in red for the hour, blue for the minute and yellow for the seconds. He moved from brass to steel and later titanium cases, partly because titanium takes black PVD well, and had to persuade manufacturers that his oversized pushers would not break. The catalogue grew into more than 480 creations, including the Krono Bauhaus and Krono 2 triple-calendar chronographs, the Cyclope wandering-hour watch, Arkitek, Bolido, the Marine divers, the single-hand Pikto, the Le Réveil alarm and Le Perpétuel perpetual calendars. His high-end pieces include a 1997 Krono Tourbillon limited to ten (reported to use the Lemania LWO 2387), the Tourbillon Volant and a Marine Tourbillon 'Black Sea' in black PVD titanium, plus cloisonné enamel dials such as the 'Santa Claus' pieces. Auction records include one Black Sea tourbillon at HK$882,000, and a Phillips lot describes a piece limited to ten made with Svend Andersen.

His own company closed in 2012, which sources describe as the end of the original brand. Since then he has worked chiefly as a designer for other houses, and the collaborations have made his name known to a new generation. His first, in 2009, was with Maximilian Büsser of MB&F on the Horological Machine No. 2.2 'Black Box', followed by the Legacy Machine No. 1 Alain Silberstein, dated to 2016 by The Rake (another source says 2011). In 2021 he designed a floor clock, the KB2 (Kontwaz Bauhaus 2), with the Besançon clockmaker Philippe Lebru and UTINAM. He describes collaboration as a matter of humility and respect for each partner's identity.

With Louis Erard he has made three projects. The first, the Excellence Régulateur (2019–20), won a 2021 Red Dot Award. Le Triptyque followed in 2021: Le Régulateur II, La Semaine, whose day window uses smiley faces, and the Le Chrono Monopoussoir, listed at CHF 3,500 to 4,500 with 178 pieces of each, and two of the three were nominated at the 2021 GPHG. In October 2023 came the khaki Triptyque, in which a tourbillon, Louis Erard's first, replaced the chronograph: a manually wound regulator tourbillon with 100 hours of power reserve in a 40 mm titanium case, sold only in a set limited to 78 at CHF 22,222, alongside khaki versions of the Régulateur and La Semaine at CHF 4,000 each in 178 pieces.

Through Grail Watch he made three more. The Ressence x Alain Silberstein 'Carpe Diem' (April 2022) is a 36-piece memento mori design, with a tulip hour indicator and a skull seconds hand, at CHF 22,500, in a 41.5 mm titanium case. The Bell & Ross BR 03 Black Ceramic Trilogy (sold from 9 January 2023) is his first ceramic-cased watch, in three versions with no logos: Klub 22 (200 pieces, US$4,400), Marine 22 (100, US$5,600) and Krono 22 (100, US$6,700), with the first fifty sold as a box set. The Angelus U11 Streamline Tourbillon (March 2023) revives the A100 manual-wind flying-tourbillon calibre from the 2015 U10 Tourbillon Lumière in a slimmer, streamlined titanium case with a Raymond Loewy influence, limited to eight pieces at CHF 68,000.

More recently came the Yema x Alain Silberstein Marine (October 2024), a ground-up design rather than a restyle of an existing watch: a 40 mm black DLC titanium diver with an internal rotating bezel and the CMM.20 micro-rotor calibre, limited to 500 at €3,900. In March 2025 MeisterSinger released two single-hand Kaenos editions, the Grand Date (€4,100) and the Open Date (€4,400), each limited to 225 with a booklet signed by Silberstein and Manfred Brassler. He was also The Rake's first artist in residence.

On his own brand, his official site says he is composing a new collection this year, powered exclusively by proprietary movements, and that the brand will mark its 40th anniversary next year. In a 2022 interview he spoke of a relaunch planned for 2024, beginning with a repair and certification service, and a catalogue raisonné of more than 480 creations. We could not verify that these plans were carried out or that any new watches have been released, and some blog posts quoting a 2027 date and prices are unsourced, so this listing does not repeat them.
