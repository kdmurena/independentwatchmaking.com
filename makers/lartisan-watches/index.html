L'Artisan was not founded from a business plan or an industry background. As its founder Arnaud puts it: it began with a sixteen-year-old in Belgium who fell in love with the mechanics, the design, and the emotion of a watch — and simply could not stop. In 2022, at seventeen, with no formal training and no industry connections, Arnaud decided to make one himself; that single decision is where L'Artisan begins. What followed was entirely self-taught: through trial, error, and a great deal of patience, he worked out assembly, case polishing, dial finishing, and design one piece at a time, funding the early work by producing custom corporate watches for local Belgian companies. In 2024 came the breakthrough that would define the brand: after six months of intense research and experimentation, Arnaud mastered the art of making genuine stone dials — rare, bold, and full of natural character, no two ever alike — and that became L'Artisan's signature. Word spread quickly, and by 2025 the brand was shipping to collectors and first-time buyers in more than thirty countries, taking on a flagship model alongside two notable collaborations: a custom pilot watch created with Southwest Airlines, and an exclusive Porsche-themed design for the Porsche Club of America. That September, Arnaud launched his most technically demanding piece yet: a dinosaur bone dial watch, each dial cut and polished by hand from fragments of genuinely 65-million-year-old fossilised material. Late 2025 opened new partnerships across the Middle East, extending the brand to a fresh audience of collectors drawn specifically to handmade, one-of-a-kind work.

2026 has been a year of deliberate elevation: since January, Arnaud has been developing an entirely new watch from the ground up — new case architecture, a new movement, a new dial concept — the most ambitious single project the brand has attempted, alongside a growing push into enamel dials for custom client commissions, bespoke mosaic mother-of-pearl patterns unique to each owner, and precious metal cases. As Arnaud puts it: 'Every watch I make starts as an obsession and ends as someone's most treasured object.' The brand today comprises two collections — the Chapter One Collection and L'Artisan Exclusive — alongside The Archive, a museum-style record of past sold-out pieces. Recent and past Exclusive references include the Heliotrope Arabic (limited to 50 pieces, €1,095), the Arabic Mosaic (€795), the Jurassic Triceratops Edition built around a dinosaur bone dial (€1,495), the Calligraphy piece created in collaboration with artist Jassim Alnasrallah (€995), a hand-painted Panda dial by TheDialArtist (€1,495), and the Middle-East Blue Enamel reference (€995) — nearly all of which have sold out shortly after release. Every watch remains a genuinely one-person operation: designed, assembled, and finished entirely by Arnaud himself, out of a small workshop in Menen, Belgium, under the registered name ADB Watches.

Early 2026 brought two milestones that mark the brand's growing confidence. The Jurassic project — a 38mm watch cased around authentic fossilised dinosaur bone and powered by the slim HZ5000A micro-rotor movement — moved from a limited workshop run onto Kickstarter, opening the one-person operation to public crowdfunding for the first time. Weeks later came the Heliotrope, a 50-piece limited run with a genuine bloodstone dial (no two alike) and Eastern Arabic numerals — a deliberate design choice, Arnaud has explained, as a personal gesture of gratitude toward the Middle Eastern collectors who embraced the brand early and helped it grow, priced around €1,120 with no second batch once sold out. On New Year's Day 2026, Arnaud marked the year by quietly launching a pièce unique — a one-of-one watch he has said he hopes will one day pass across someone's wrist carrying moments and memories he'll never fully know, capturing in a single sentence why he makes watches at all. Press recognition has followed the growth: features in Oracle Time (among its best stone-dial watches of 2026), Time Telling Magazine, Watch Circle Magazine, and Belgian financial daily De Tijd, plus a stand as exhibitor at the Monte Carlo Watch & Jewellery Convention. Now operating as Artisan d'Horlogerie from a single bench in Ghent, with pieces worn on collectors' wrists in more than forty countries, L'Artisan remains exactly what it started as: one pair of hands refusing to rush a single piece.
