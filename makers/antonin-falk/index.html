Antonin Falk

Antonin Falk is a young French watchmaker from Savoy who trained in Morteau, the town near the Swiss border that is close to the watchmaking centres of Le Locle and La Chaux-de-Fonds. In 2016 he was named Best Apprentice in France in the MAF Or National Horlogerie competition for a handcrafted Breguet-style hand and pipe. He studied at the Lycée Edgar Faure in Morteau, the same school that trained other names in this catalogue such as Tanguy Huret and Pierre Billon.

His first montre d'école, presented in 2019 when he was 19 and had just graduated, was made to earn his Brevet des Métiers d'Art. It shows jumping hours on a retrograde minute scale. The hours appear on an off-centre sub-dial like those on marine chronometers, while a retrograde minute hand sweeps across the dial and the hour jumps at the end of each sweep. A mechanism makes the hour jump in one direction only, and it draws on the lost-beat detent escapement used in marine chronometers. The watch is a tribute to marine chronometer makers such as Berthoud, Arnold, Leroy and Breguet, and the minute hand's counterweight is lead made by melting fishing weights.

He made most of the watch himself, including the case, hands and complication. The movement is a hand-wound ETA 6497 with a 68-hour power reserve, beating at 18,000 vph with 17 jewels, and finished with charbonnage (coal polishing). The silvered dial and the 45 mm by 12 mm gold-plated brass case carry the marine chronometer theme, with sapphire crystals on the front and the caseback. Falk used traditional tools such as a jig borer, a lathe and a milling machine. The cam and the rack spring were CNC-machined, the hand and the graduated sector were laser-cut, and all the other parts were made by hand. It is a unique piece and no price has been published.

His second montre d'école, shown in 2021, was made to complete his studies. The brief was to design and make an automatic watch using a hand-wound movement as the base, and Falk chose the Calibre T02 tourbillon from BCP Tourbillons of La Chaux-de-Fonds. He drew on Breguet's Perpétuelle of 1780, with its oscillating weight winding by shock, and on the 1950s Jaeger-LeCoultre Futurematic, which had no winding crown and used a hook to stop the winding mass from overwinding the mainspring.

Instead of the usual slipping mainspring, the watch has a large bumper, or hammer, that winds the movement. A nut moving up and down drives a lever that locks the hammer with a hook once the mainspring reaches a set tension. The barrel turns only within an optimal range, which shortens the power reserve to about 30 hours but gives the balance enough amplitude as soon as the watch starts. The watch cannot be wound by hand: the crown is built into the caseband and only sets the time. The design is minimalist, in a cushion-shaped case with an exhibition caseback that shows the automatic mechanism.

Falk's official website was not yet confirmed to be live when this listing was written, and the pages we found give no case dimensions, materials or price for the bumper-automatic watch. Further photographs and information appear on his Instagram account.
