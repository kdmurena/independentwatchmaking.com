Voxel is an independent watch brand founded by Thibault Luc, its creative director, and Bruno Herbet, its technical director, who describe it as French creative direction combined with Swiss mechanical engineering. The brand lists both founders in Paris, though Time+Tide places the design work in Lyon and the engineering in the Vallée de Joux, Switzerland. Luc is an entrepreneur who founded an earlier watch brand, Aight, in 2012 and led artistic collaborations including a contemporary art collection with the Centre Pompidou. Herbet is a microtechnology engineer trained in the Vallée de Joux with more than 15 years of movement development, prototyping and industrialisation work with Swiss watchmakers. By the brand's timeline the project was conceived in 2022, the 12/24 jumping-hours complication was developed in 2023 and 2024, the case was refined through prototyping in 2025, and the first edition was released in 2026.

The first watch, the Meridion VX01-E, was shown at Geneva Watch Days in 2026. Its feature is a proprietary 12/24 jumping-hours display, which Time+Tide says the brand calls the Reactor. At 1 pm the hour markers jump from a 12-hour to a 24-hour scale, and at 1 am they jump back, so the dial changes its numerals every twelve hours, and a small window at 11 o'clock shows a sun or moon. Fratello notes that the Gruen Airflight used a similar idea in the 1960s, with a hand-wound movement. The brand says the system took four years to develop in-house. It is built on a Swiss automatic Newton P092 (a modified Soprod calibre, according to Fratello, Oracle of Time and Monochrome) running at 4 Hz with a 44-hour power reserve and 23 jewels, with the module added on top.

The Meridion's decagonal, compass-like case is 41.4 mm wide, 12.2 mm thick including the domed anti-reflective sapphire crystal, and 45.5 mm lug to lug, with 20 mm lugs. It is made of 904L steel with a grade 5 titanium screw-in caseback that has two smoked-glass windows, and is water resistant to 120 m. Dials are finished in grained and sunburst patterns in three versions: Charcoal Black, Electric Blue and Silver Star. The watch comes on a tool-free interchangeable Alcantara strap in six colours or on an integrated 904L steel bracelet, and the brand supplies a travel case that holds the watch and up to four straps.

Edition 01 is limited to 300 numbered pieces, with 120 each in Charcoal Black and Electric Blue and 60 in Silver Star. Pre-orders opened on 1 October 2026 through the brand's online boutique, with delivery estimated for around February 2027 (Fratello says Q1 2027). The brand's site lists the Meridion at EUR 3,000 including EU taxes, while the press launch coverage gave EUR 2,500 on the Alcantara strap or EUR 2,850 on the steel bracelet excluding VAT, and the site separately lists the steel bracelet at EUR 540, so the pricing figures differ across sources. Time+Tide reports the watch is an official candidate in the 2026 GPHG Challenge category.

The brand offers a two-year worldwide warranty covering every movement, free insured shipping and 30-day returns, and says the watches are assembled and regulated in Switzerland. Its production process runs from concept through prototype and industrialisation to release, and it says no watch reaches the wrist before it meets its precision standards. The Monochrome article on the launch is marked as a sponsored post by the brand.
