L. Leroy is a revival of one of the great names of French and Swiss watchmaking. The house was founded in Paris in 1785 by Charles Leroy, the son of a watchmaker, and was soon appointed watchmaker to the king and later a supplier to the French imperial family. In 1835 it became watchmaker to the French Ministry of the Navy, supplying marine chronometers to the major European fleets until the late 1980s, and its clients over two centuries included Marie Antoinette, Queen Victoria, Napoleon III, Chopin, Louis Pasteur, Henri Matisse, Antoine de Saint-Exupéry and General de Gaulle. Its best-known creation, the Leroy 01 "La Lucie", was commissioned by the Lisbon collector António Augusto de Carvalho Monteiro and took seven years to develop: a gold pocket watch of 975 components and 27 complications that won first prize at the 1900 Paris Exposition Universelle and remained the world's most complicated watch until Patek Philippe's Calibre 89 in 1989. Louis Leroy also installed the first radio-telegraph time-signal system at the top of the Eiffel Tower in 1910, and the house sold an automatic-winding wristwatch as early as 1922.

After decades of decline the name was relaunched in 1999 under THA, the Sainte-Croix incubator founded by François-Paul Journe and Denis Flageollet, and in 2004 it was acquired by Miguel Rodríguez, owner of the Festina Group, who bought the historical materials and patrimony of the house and has built it up as a heritage maison. Its archives include close to 400,000 recorded sales covering some 200 years, along with drawings, plans, supplier orders, chronometry certificates and correspondence. L. Leroy also holds a private collection of more than 200 historic pieces, including grand-complication pocket watches with minute repeaters, perpetual calendars, tourbillons and chronographs, officer's table clocks, marine chronometers, an alarm clock in the form of a snuffbox commissioned by Marie Antoinette, a watch made for Queen Victoria in 1875 and an 1890 enamelled ladies' watch set with diamonds, all of which serve as the inspiration for new collections and as a restoration reference. Rodríguez is the sole owner, the brand is led by CEO and brand director Hugo Lesizza, and the maison is headquartered in Bienne with production in Geneva, after earlier years in Le Sentier in the Vallée de Joux.

The modern collection is the Osmior line, which began with a monopusher chronograph in 2014 and was reintroduced in earnest after a Minute Repeater Flying Tourbillon unique piece for Only Watch 2023. The Osmior Bal du Temps, shown at Geneva Watch Days 2025 for the house's 240th anniversary, uses the hand-wound L601SQ calibre, a 321-component movement with a minute repeater on two gongs, a one-minute flying tourbillon, 21,600 vph and a 90-hour power reserve, in a 43mm case of titanium, 18k red gold or platinum. The Elyor (2026), whose name is an anagram of Leroy that the watchmaker used to sign his work during the Terror, carries the L600, an automatic with a micro-rotor in tungsten alloy, a 78-part flying tourbillon at 6 o'clock and 60 hours of reserve in a 42mm case, with only 50 movements to be commissioned at US$82,800 in titanium, US$118,800 in red gold and US$138,000 in platinum. The 2026 Vox Temporis, inspired by a tact watch the house made in 1810 for the Prince of Hesse, combines a rotating dial turning beneath a fixed hand-engraved arrow hand with the L601SQR minute repeater and flying tourbillon in a 43mm case, limited to 15 pieces across three metals at CHF 265,000 to 320,000, and the house was nominated in the Artistic Crafts category of the 2026 Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève.

The brand states that its watches are entirely manufactured in Geneva, with an emphasis on high complications, métiers d'art and gem-setting, and it also offers restoration of vintage and antique watches for collectors and museums. The movements are developed exclusively for L. Leroy, but press reports (Monochrome and Time+Tide) name Régence Production SA, formerly Timeless SA, as their producer, and other coverage describes them simply as in-house. Production is tiny, in numbered limited series of 15 to 50 pieces, and the catalogue lists the house as an independent maison whose owner runs it separately from the rest of Festina Group, on the basis of its own leadership, Geneva production and heritage programme.
