Reuben Schoots is a self-taught independent watchmaker who works alone in a home workshop south of Canberra, Australia, and describes his aim as handmade Australian watches. He was born into no watchmaking tradition: as a boy he restored a 1961 Triumph TR4 with his father Pieter and was fascinated by a carriage clock at home, and as a young adult he ran several ventures, including a restaurant, a clothing business and a web design practice. His site calls him 'self-taught, independent, based in Australia.'

Illness led him to watchmaking. During travel in South America in 2015–2016 he contracted tropical viruses and a parasite, and spent most of two years recovering. In mid-2017 an engineer friend showed him a mechanical watch, and on New Year's Eve 2017 he decided to become a watchmaker. He read George Daniels' book Watch Making cover to cover, bought a lathe and set out to build Daniels' double-barrel tourbillon pocket watch by hand, a project of roughly 300 components that he later paused because it earns no income. With no watchmaking school, tradition or apprenticeship available to him in Australia, he turned to toolmaking and machining, with the engineer Lindsay Drabsch, who taught him machine and tool making for over a year, and the Canberra watchmaker Roger Little as mentors. With Drabsch he built a precision CNC mill, taught himself CAM and 3D design, and has made hundreds of his own tools.

His method combines hand skills with modern machining: he designs in CAD and 3D, machines on manual lathes and his CNC mill, finishes by hand, plates and ink-fills engravings himself, and assembles in a separate clean room. He draws on Daniels, Breguet and 19th- and 20th-century Glashütte pocket watches, as well as Canberra's rivers, seasonal colours and mountains, and Japanese design. His stated goal is to make every component of a watch in his own workshop. He believes that he and the Hacko team in Sydney are Australia's only professional watchmakers, and he hopes to see the country recognised in watchmaking.

Series One, his first wristwatch, was prototyped from August 2021 during Canberra's COVID lockdown and limited to six pieces, which sold out within about 40 minutes without an official release. It has a 41.5 mm stainless steel case and a hand-wound, time-only movement. He designed and made almost every component from raw material, including the German silver dial in four parts (dial plate, chapter ring, subsidiary seconds ring and nameplate, forged, engraved and ink-filled), blued hands heat-treated to a purplish blue in the manner of Daniels, and the barrel bridge, wheel train bridge, balance cock, click works and screws. Each dial carries a hand-scraped finish made with a tool modified from his grandfather's engineering workshop, so no two are alike. The price was A$15,000, and Time+Tide reported that one example appeared on the Loupe This auction platform in July 2023, which it believed to be the first Schoots watch to be auctioned.

Series Two is a ground-up redesign that keeps the look of Series One while bringing much more of the watch in-house, and is priced at A$57,500 excluding taxes (Time+Tide had reported A$52,500 earlier). It is a hand-wound time-only watch with small seconds, in a 316L stainless steel case 41.5 mm across and about 10 mm thick (9.8 mm without the crystals), 49 mm lug-to-lug, with a sapphire front and exhibition caseback. It is the first Schoots watch with a case he designed and made himself, after nine prototypes. The dial is flame-whitened silver, larger in proportion, finished with guilloché, circular and straight graining and a hand-flaked texture, with a 3D-sculpted arrowhead tip on the hour hand.

The Series Two movement beats at 18,000 vibrations per hour. It began from a base calibre of which only the wheel train and keyless works were retained, and the gear train and escapement are modified and hand-finished. The architecture is new, with 3D-sculpted multi-level nickel silver bridges and mainplate, a Daniels-style steel centre-wheel cock and a beryllium copper crown wheel cap. Plates and bridges are frosted and hand-finished, and each movement is numbered and signed. In-house parts include the mainplate, bridges and cocks, pallet fork and escape wheel cock, crown wheel, all screws, ratchet pawl and clickspring, balance wheel, hands, and the case, crown, dial and chapter ring. Schoots aimed to double his labour per watch against Series One and says it has gone well beyond that. Series Two was pre-sold, with no edition size stated.
