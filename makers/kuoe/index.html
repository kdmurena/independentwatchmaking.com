Kuoe is a Japanese watch brand founded in 2020 in Kyoto — the city that serves as the living archive of Japanese traditional culture, craft, and aesthetic sensibility — by designer and founder Uchimura, whose concept for the brand had been gestating for a full decade before it reached the market. The origin story begins in 2010, when Uchimura was a university student studying language in London and wandered by chance into a shop stocking antique watches from the 1940s through the 1970s. He was immediately and deeply drawn to the instruments in the window: hand-wound watches whose movements had been keeping time for decades, whose materials had accumulated warmth and patina, and whose designs possessed the quality he would spend the next ten years trying to understand and then recreate — a quality he describes simply as 'truly classic.' What he observed in that London shop was that genuinely classic design is not anchored to any era; it is loved and revered universally across generations and cultures because it speaks to something that does not change. He returned to Japan with that insight. After graduating university he joined a watch company in Kyoto, spending several years learning the business before using it as a platform to develop the sub-brand concept that would eventually become Kuoe. In 2020 he launched Kuoe independently, with the first model — the Old Smith 90-001 — released in April of that year. The brand is based at the Yoshioka Oike Building in central Kyoto, and ships worldwide via DHL with VAT and customs duties included in the product price for most destinations.

The name Kuoe does not translate directly into English; it is deliberately chosen for its sound and its distinctly Japanese phonetic character — a name that carries the aesthetic register of Kyoto itself without requiring explanation. The design philosophy it represents is the direct product of Uchimura's 2010 encounter: to make watches that look as if they were born in the mid-20th century but function and wear as contemporary instruments. Every watch Kuoe produces is a study in what the brand calls the timeless charm of historic Kyoto — a balance between deep respect for tradition and a recognition that the purpose of craft is to be used, worn, and lived with, not preserved behind glass. The collection is organised into three families — Old Smith, Royal Smith, and Holborn — plus the recent Sombrero addition, all distinguished by their explicit references to the mid-century dress and field watch vocabulary: wire lugs or integrated lug forms, domed crystals, restrained dials with applied indices or print numerals, and case shapes that recall the small-case golden era of the 1940s–60s. Bronze case variants are offered alongside steel. The Holborn 90-003 is the most design-distinctive model, referencing post-war British watch aesthetic in its dial layout.

The collection spans both quartz and automatic movements. Quartz models — the Old Smith 90-001 and Holborn 90-003 — use Japanese quartz movements and represent Kuoe's most accessible entry points, offering the vintage aesthetic without compromising on reliability for everyday use. Automatic models — Old Smith 90-002 (also in a bronze case variant), Royal Smith 90-006, Royal Smith 90-008, Old Smith 90-007, Royal Smith 90-010, Sombrero 90-011, and Royal Smith 90-012 — use Japanese automatic movements and expand the Old Smith's design language into a wider range of dial colours, case configurations, and case materials. The Sombrero 90-011 is the most recently introduced and most distinctive form: a cushion-influenced case with domed crystal particularly reminiscent of certain mid-century Swiss dress watches. Accessories including straps are also sold through the webshop. Kuoe distributes globally through its own online store and through a network of authorised retailers, with physical presence in Japan through selected stockists. The brand's mission — 'the appeal of classic design from Kyoto to the world' — is not a tagline but an accurate description of what the collection does: it makes a specifically Japanese version of universal mid-century aesthetic language accessible to collectors worldwide at approachable prices.
