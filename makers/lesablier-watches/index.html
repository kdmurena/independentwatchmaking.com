Lesablier began, quite literally, as one enthusiast — founder Akbar S — putting together salvaged watch parts on his own coffee table in 2017, driven by a burning desire to build his personal perfect watch. Born from frustration with what the brand calls the world of mass-produced, cookie-cutter watches, that early tinkering grew from drawings and conceptualisation into experimentation and eventually genuine implementation, evolving over the years into the small Washington, D.C.-based watch company it is today. In his own words, Akbar set out with three specific goals: to build watches incorporating his own personal take on the design languages, features, and small quirks he loved as a collector himself; to make each Lesablier instantly recognisable and filled with nuance and character rather than generic; and to build the brand as a genuine community, staying personally connected with everyone who buys one and supporting their own journey through the watch-collecting hobby. 'Watches are deeply personal to me,' he writes, 'and I am deeply honored to be doing this.'

Lesablier's model timeline traces a clear evolution in ambition and craft. The AVQ (Aviateur VQ), the brand's first watch, launched in 2017–2018 as a modern spin on the 24-hour-only pilot's watches of the 1960s, featuring a 24-hour hour hand, day-night indicator, and high-visibility tool-watch dial; only 500 were ever produced. The Sport Classic followed in 2019–2020, a love letter to the funky geometric integrated-bracelet sport watches of the 1970s and 1980s, and Lesablier's first foray into exotic dial materials — including the brand's signature 'Ghost' dial, a Grand Feu enamel dial, and its first-ever aventurine dial, each produced in runs of 50–100. The Chronosport (2021–2022) continued that vintage-sport theme in the form of a bi-compax integrated-bracelet chronograph, built around an all-new case and a slim meca-quartz movement, with aventurine and meteorite dial options among its 50-piece-per-colour runs. The LS-04 Travelgraph (2023–2025) is Lesablier's most technically advanced watch to date: a genuine automatic world-timer complication — a feature rarely found at this price point — paired with the brand's most intricately finished case and bracelet yet, in runs of 50 per colourway; a related Travelgraph Universalis reference has since sold out. Fratello Watches, reviewing the LS-04 hands-on, called it 'a very capable watch for a nice price,' noting the worldtimer complication, 100-metre water resistance, and the not-often-seen aventurine dial option.

Across its full range — the Chronosport (from $410), Sport Classic ($495), and Travelgraph ($875) — Lesablier continues to build in small, limited-production runs, prioritising distinctive design, exotic dial materials, and genuine mechanical ambition over mass-market volume. Every watch is sold direct through the brand's own online store, maintaining the close, personal relationship with its collector community that Akbar identified as core to the brand's mission from the very beginning.
