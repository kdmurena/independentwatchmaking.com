Parmigiani Fleurier

Parmigiani Fleurier was founded on May 29, 1996, at the Grand Salon Sandoz of the Beau-Rivage Palace in Lausanne, born from Michel Parmigiani's lifelong devotion to restoration and the mechanical arts. A 1995 Prix Gaïa laureate, Parmigiani trained as a master restorer, spending decades preserving exceptional horological masterpieces for the Sandoz Family Foundation before the Foundation backed him in building a manufacture of his own. 'Restoration taught me that creation begins with understanding what came before,' he has said. 'By reviving the past, you learn to build the future with coherence and gratitude.' The Maison launched with 52 references spanning elegant simplicity to haute complications, and today operates a fully integrated manufacture in Val de Travers where close to 350 artisans craft nearly every component in-house — cases, movements, and dials alike.

The house's early decades produced a string of genuine firsts: the 1996 Toric, Michel Parmigiani's debut design, with a knurled bezel and fluted details drawn from classical architecture and the Golden Ratio; the 1999 Ionica, the first wristwatch built around an eight-day power reserve movement (the PF110); the 2004 Bugatti Type 370, a world-first tubular movement assembled along a single axis in imitation of a car engine; the 2009 Fibonacci pocket watch, another meditation on the Golden Ratio; and the 2010 ultra-flat Tonda 1950, marking the manufacture's first automatic movement with a micro-rotor. Later highlights include the 2016 Chronor split-seconds chronograph built to mark the Maison's 20th anniversary, the 2017 Toric Hémisphère dual-timezone calibre, the 2019 Hijri Perpetual Calendar wristwatch — a world premiere — and 2024's L'Armoriale pocket watch, built around a 19th-century minute-repeater chronograph movement Michel Parmigiani had personally restored decades earlier.

The Maison's modern chapter began in 2021 with the arrival of CEO Guido Terreni, who found what he has called an 'under-expressed' brand — one with all the historical ingredients but lacking a unifying creative direction. The pandemic-era production halt gave him room to work: rather than launching tactically, he spent the downtime on what he describes as introspection, and within seven months of his appointment the team had designed and released the Tonda PF, seven references across four movements. 'It was an alignment of the stars,' Terreni has said. 'Once we presented it, the company changed face.' The collection's guiding idea was what Terreni calls 'Private Luxury' — a deliberate contrast to 'quiet luxury' as a fashion trend, built instead on discretion as a genuine, durable value: the brand's logo was removed from the dial entirely, replaced by a small 'PF' badge at 12 o'clock, aimed at owners who buy for their own satisfaction rather than for recognition.

The response was immediate and, by industry standards, unusually strong: the Tonda PF GMT Rattrapante, presented at Geneva Watch Days in 2022, drew roughly 1,000 deposits against an initial production run of just 140 movements. Terreni matched that demand with equally deliberate restraint on the supply side, cutting two-thirds of the Maison's retail partners in 2022 and rebuilding distribution around roughly 100 carefully chosen retailers worldwide, supported by a digital warranty system that lets the company track dealer sell-through in real time and withhold new stock where prior seasons remain unsold — a discipline Terreni credits with strengthening the brand's secondary-market values. 'Our customer doesn't want to be seen,' he has said. 'He wants to be understood.'

That same restraint shapes what Terreni calls the Tonda PF's 'invisible complications' — mechanisms that remain hidden within a calm, conventional-looking dial until the owner activates them. The GMT Rattrapante and Minute Rattrapante established the idea; the 2026 Chronographe Mystérieux, unveiled for the Maison's 30th anniversary and built around the new in-house PF053 calibre, pushes it furthest yet. At rest, its five hands sit perfectly superimposed and the dial reads as an ordinary time-only watch; one press sets the chronograph running, a second stops it, and a third returns the display to invisibility. 'Creativity has to breathe and has to have the time to penetrate and to be understood,' Terreni has said of the multi-year arc connecting these releases. 'You cannot go faster than the music.'

Toric, the Maison's very first design, returned to the line-up in 2024 and became the vehicle for its most technically accomplished recent release, the Toric Quantième Perpétuel. Its solid-gold dial is finished using grené main, a technique Michel Parmigiani brought over from his own restoration practice: a paste of silver powder, cream of tartar, distilled water, and sea salt, abraded into the gold with a stiff boar-hair brush until it takes on a fine matte grain, then selectively colored by physical vapor deposition rather than lacquer, so the texture stays visible beneath the tint. Inside is the manually wound Calibre PF733, cased in solid platinum or 18k rose gold at just 5.15mm thick with a 60-hour reserve, finished with Côtes de Fleurier and hand-bevelled bridges. Offered as two 50-piece limited editions — 'Morning Blue' in platinum and 'Golden Hour' in rose gold, priced at CHF 92,000 and CHF 85,000 respectively — it deliberately wears a pin buckle rather than a deployant, since, as Terreni puts it, 'when you put all that craft in the movement, you can't look at it through the blade of a deployant.'

Alongside its wristwatches, the Maison has built a dedicated calendar collection under the Tonda PF banner, describing the calendar complication as 'the mirror of civilizations.' It comprises the Hijri Perpetual Calendar, based on the tabular Islamic calendar developed by Muslim scholars in the 8th century and dated from the Prophet Muhammad's migration from Mecca to Medina in 622 CE; the Xiali Complete Calendar, reconciling the Chinese lunar-solar system by inserting an intercalary 13th month roughly every three years while also dividing the solar year into 24 traditional agricultural terms; and the Gregorian Annual Calendar, built around the in-house PF339 calibre with a retrograde date display and precision moonphase, available in both rose gold and steel with platinum accents.

For its 30th anniversary in 2026, the Maison built two further trilogies. In platinum, the Tonda PF GMT Rattrapante, Minute Rattrapante, and Chronographe Mystérieux were each recast in 950 platinum and limited to 30 pieces, unified under what the Maison calls the idea of the invisible complication. In parallel, the Toric Anniversary Collection reworked the Petite Seconde, Quantième Perpétuel, and Chronographe Rattrapante with hand-hammered gold dials and hand-finished gold movements, each also limited to 30 pieces — including a fully integrated, 5Hz split-seconds chronograph movement built from 285 components. The anniversary year also introduced a new Tonda PF Chronograph in a more compact 40mm rose-gold case, extending the collection's everyday-elegance side alongside its exhibition pieces.
