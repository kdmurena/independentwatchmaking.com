Canton Clock by Wei Guang Wen

China
Founded2002
LocationGuangzhou (Liwan District), China
PricePOA
Volume~5/yr
Official Site ↗

Canton Clock, or Guangzhong, refers to the chiming clocks produced in Guangzhou during the reigns of the Kangxi, Yongzheng, Qianlong, and Jiaqing emperors; by the mid-Qianlong period they had reached a level of craftsmanship exceptional enough to be presented as tribute to the imperial court itself. Wei Guang Wen is recognized as the sole inheritor of this national intangible cultural heritage, and has been passionately dedicated to reviving it since 2002. As he has explained, Canton Clock's art and technology reached their zenith centuries ago, when these exquisite timepieces were typically collected by emperors and rarely seen by the public; today the surviving examples are found almost exclusively in the Forbidden City or in museums, leaving the tradition largely unfamiliar even within China. Wei identifies three defining characteristics of the form: an appearance that blends Chinese and Western elements, combining Western Baroque style with distinctive Lingnan folk motifs such as treasure bottles and golden pineapples alongside the grandeur of Chinese palace aesthetics; internal mechanisms of real complexity, demanding exceptional craftsmanship and precision in assembly; and an artistic value expressed through intricate exterior decoration and inlay work, reflecting the mastery of Lingnan folk craftsmanship at its height.

Wei's own creations embody all three. At the 42nd Hong Kong Clock & Watch Fair, he exhibited a Canton Clock standing over 40cm tall, more than 20cm wide, and about 20cm thick — a copper body adorned with gold-plated hollow carvings and inscriptions, its patterns culminating in a round tent at the top beneath which a viewing box houses a gilded Buddha statue, flanked by a purple-robed monk striking a bell on one side and a yellow-robed monk striking a wooden fish on the other, the round clock face below drawing directly on palace Canton Clocks from the late Qing dynasty. He acknowledges candidly that the craftsmanship behind such pieces has been nearly lost since the tradition's decline, with only a handful of artisans continuing to produce them, largely out of personal passion rather than commercial demand — a fragility that drives his broader mission to gather historical documents, reconnect with the tradition's remaining masters and craftsmen, and systematically rediscover and pass on techniques that might otherwise disappear entirely within a generation.

In 2018 Wei established the Canton Clock History and Culture Institute on the Intangible Cultural Heritage Street of Yong Qing Fang in Guangzhou's Liwan District, its antique clocks in mahogany and brass adorned with enamel, gilt, inlay, and ivory carving giving visitors a genuine sensory journey into the tradition's past; in 2022 the Institute was recognized as part of the eighth batch of municipal intangible cultural heritage protection units. Beyond his own workshop, Wei holds the titles of Director of the Watch and Jewelry Culture Professional Committee of the GD-HK-MAC Greater Bay Area Economic and Cultural Promotion Association, Deputy Director of the Collection Research Committee of the China Horologe Association, and chairman of Time Lord, a nationwide franchise promoting Switzerland's REUGE and Germany's HERMLE across China. He actively seeks collaborations with watch brands to create cultural derivative products celebrating Canton Clock's significance — the work, ultimately, of a man positioning himself not merely as a craftsman but as the last active guardian of an imperial art form.
