Neo Kung

Neo Kung is a Chinese independent watchmaker whose debut in 2024 was one of the most striking arrivals in Asian independent horology in years — a former Chief Product Officer at both Sea-Gull and Shanghai Watch, two of China's most storied and historically significant movement manufacturers, who left the institutional world after years at its summit to build something entirely personal. His career as CPO gave him an unusually comprehensive understanding of movement manufacturing at industrial scale — the mechanics, the tolerances, the supply chains, and the distance between mass production and genuine craft — and it was that intimate familiarity with the industry's limitations and possibilities that prompted his decision to set out alone. Before creating his first watch, Neo Kung undertook a systematic pilgrimage to the epicentres of haute horlogerie: Geneva, the Vallée de Joux, La Chaux-de-Fonds, and Neuchâtel, visiting the ateliers of grand masters to observe their standards of finishing, movement architecture, and case construction firsthand, and to set his own benchmarks against the finest work being produced anywhere.

He describes himself on his website with characteristic directness: 'a one-man independent watchmaker, aiming to preserve and promote haute horology watchmaking in traditional ways.' His skill set extends beyond movement work into goldsmithing, hand-pushed engraving, and enamelling — all executed by hand and without delegation. Even the watch straps are finished personally: he dyes them himself using traditional plant-based, coating-free methods, stitches them by hand, and has stated that this time-consuming process reflects his commitment to ensuring that every element of the watch meets the same standard as every other. L'Atelier de Neo Kung is, in the fullest sense, a one-man atelier.

The Orienta, presented in 2024, is Neo Kung's debut watch and what he has described as the first haute horlogerie chronograph made in China — a historically resonant claim that connects deliberately to the monumental 60-year anniversary of China's first chronograph, itself developed in the 1960s using the Venus 175 column-wheel architecture as its technical foundation. The Venus 175 was acquired by the Tianjin Seagull Watch Group in the 1960s and became the basis for the Seagull ST1901 — the movement at the heart of the iconic Seagull 1963 Air Force chronograph, arguably China's most beloved mechanical watch. Cal. NK-01, the movement inside the Orienta, draws its architectural inspiration directly from this lineage, described by Neo Kung as connected to 'l'arbre de la vie' (the tree of life) — its bridges, arms, and balance wheel designed to symbolise life's resilience and splendour, infusing a poetic dimension into the movement's physical form. The calibre was developed in collaboration with both Swiss and Chinese partners and runs at 21,600 A/h (3Hz) with a 42-hour power reserve; all finishing is done by Neo Kung himself using traditional methods.

The watch's visual identity draws from the same 1960s Chinese chronograph heritage that its movement references: a bicompax layout with concentric grooves, hand-applied silvering, gold applied numerals and shadow flange evoking warmth, four squared indices whose form echoes the corner towers of the Forbidden City's imperial enclosure — a detail whose cultural reference is precise and intentional — and hands that retain the unique tail variations of the original 1960s design, updated with double red accents for contemporary readability. The signature at 6 o'clock reads 'Heritage Forward' — the philosophy in two words. The case required 21 design iterations before Neo Kung was satisfied: fully polished Pt950 platinum at 39.8mm × 10.6mm, classical pump pushers at 2 and 4 o'clock, water resistant to 60 metres. Wearability was the product of extensive trials — the final proportions not the starting point but the outcome of a rigorous refinement process. The strap is hand plant-dyed alligator, CITES-certified, in what Neo Kung calls a 'unique, warm, and radiating Soleil palette.' Limited to 12 pieces at CHF 35,800.

The Orienta was peer-reviewed at Watches and Wonders Geneva 2024, where it received praise from horological masters from across the independent world — an unusual reception for a first watch from an Asian independent with no prior brand history. It was subsequently nominated for the GPHG Chronograph category in 2024, competing against entries from Audemars Piguet, Vacheron Constantin, and the established Swiss independents — a nomination that confirmed the Orienta's technical and artistic standing as a serious piece of watchmaking, not a luxury curiosity. Monochrome Watches published one of the first detailed analyses of the NK-01 movement and noted both the clear Venus 175 heritage and the quality of the hand finishing, while acknowledging that further detail on the movement's production would be welcomed. The Independent Watcher described Neo Kung as having 'quickly become a standout figure in independent watchmaking.' His broader cultural ambition is stated without ambiguity: to demonstrate that haute horlogerie is not a Swiss or European monopoly, that a Chinese maker working alone, finishing every surface by his own hand, can produce work that belongs in the same conversation as the finest small ateliers in Geneva or the Vallée de Joux. In this he joins a growing and increasingly internationally recognised cohort of Chinese independent watchmakers — Lin Yong Hua, Qian Guobiao, Tan Zehua, Logan Kuan Rao, Qin Gan, and others — who together are writing the most important new chapter in the history of the craft.
