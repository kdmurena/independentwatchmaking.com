Haim Watch Company is a Chicago-based independent microbrand founded in 2020 by M. Zakir Miah — whose name, spelled backwards, gives the brand its name. Miah is a passionate watch connoisseur who built the brand from a collector's perspective rather than a manufacturing background: as passionate watch enthusiasts themselves, the founding team developed their concept by asking the question 'What defines the perfect watch?' — and pursuing that answer across four collections. The brand's philosophy balances accessibility with ambition, believing that a finely crafted timepiece should adorn every wrist whether the owner is a seasoned collector or new to horology, while also offering exclusive and limited models that carry the appeal of true rarity. Every Haim Watch Co. timepiece undergoes final assembly and quality control personally performed by the team in Chicago, with every watch individually tested for 72 hours in four positions before leaving the workshop. A portion of profits is donated to organisations committed to eradicating hunger, starting in Chicago. The brand has been featured in Hodinkee, Worn & Wound, Gear Patrol, The Calibrated Wrist, The Time Bum, and Watch Clicker — a press roster that reflects genuine collector community recognition rather than paid placement.

The collection is organised across four categories. The Time Only line is anchored by the Legacy Automatic — the brand's foundational model and the watch that first brought Haim to wide attention, a classically proportioned automatic dress watch drawing inspiration from bygone eras while infusing the brand's own aesthetic. Worn & Wound reviewed it as a well-executed debut that delivered considerably more than its price point suggested. The Complications category was added for timepieces with intricate features beyond pure time display; its debut model is the Viajero — Spanish for 'traveller' — a world timer that became the brand's most discussed watch. The Viajero was featured on Hodinkee, reviewed by Gear Patrol, The Time Bum, and The Calibrated Wrist, and is the watch shown in this listing's image in its Global Citizen configuration. A worldtimer at an accessible price point is a rare proposition in the independent American microbrand space, and the Viajero filled that gap with a compelling aesthetic rooted in exploration and travel. The Chronographs category offers classical split-function timing in a variety of dial configurations. The Sports & Adventure category began with the Descent — described by the brand as 'our inaugural sports watch, with a unique twist' — a tool watch designed for active wear with a higher water resistance specification and a more robust case profile than the dress-oriented Legacy.

Haim positions itself explicitly within the broader collecting community rather than above it — the website's language of 'joining the family' is deliberate, and the brand maintains direct communication with buyers via email, Instagram, and social media as a standing invitation rather than a marketing operation. The long-term aspiration, stated on the website, is to design and create in-house mechanical movements — a goal that places Haim in a longer developmental arc typical of the most ambitious American microbrands. The Viajero's Hodinkee coverage in particular marked a step-change in the brand's visibility, establishing it as a name within the international collector conversation rather than only the domestic American microbrand community.
