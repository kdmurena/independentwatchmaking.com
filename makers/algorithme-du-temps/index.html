Algorithme du Temps is a new independent brand founded by Nadim Elgarhy, who holds a degree in computer engineering and algorithms and researches how people perceive time. Its first watch, Rêveries des Secondes, Étude Nº1, was released on 18 November 2026. Elgarhy designed it himself, and the design is patent pending. The company's location is not stated on its website, its press page or the press coverage we found, so this listing does not give one. The brand name sets the algorithm, which is definite and logical, against time, which we try to control and cannot. Monochrome also reports that he founded a study office, the Bureau Privé des Études Expérimentales, which asks how faithfully a watch can reflect lived time.

Elgarhy sums up the idea behind the watch in a video posted to the brand's Instagram account: 'Time doesn't flow, it dreams.' It starts from the way we live time: research on time perception shows that time seems to fly when we are absorbed, drag while we wait and stretch in memory, and a glance at a clock can make its seconds hand seem to stop, an illusion known as chronostasis. Every watch marks each second identically, whereas Rêveries des Secondes gives the seconds the shape of felt time while keeping the minute measured. The brand's research page cites Henri Bergson's Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience (1889), published in English as Time and Free Will (1910) in F. L. Pogson's translation, and Bergson's contrast between measured clock time and lived duration is the philosophical thread running through the project. The page also lists further reading by Augustine, Droit-Volet and Meck, Gable and Poole, Witowska, Schmidt and Wittmann, and Stetson, Fiesta and Eagleman. Elgarhy has published two papers, dated 5 November 2024: 'The Future of Time: Psychological, Perceptual, and Philosophical Dimensions and Their Potential Applications in Advanced Horology' and 'Consciousness as a Fundamental Dimension: A Modern Theoretical Framework'.

The centre seconds hand never runs evenly. Its speed varies continuously from minus 1 times (backwards at the pace of an ordinary seconds hand) to plus 3 times normal speed, so it slows, stops for an instant, runs backwards and then races forward. It runs backwards 19 per cent of the time and changes direction 46 times in five minutes, and nothing about it is random. At every full minute it crosses twelve exactly, after running forward for at least two seconds, and the pattern repeats only every five minutes, longer than anyone watches a seconds hand. Each watch has its own pair of cams, so no two hands wander the same way. Monochrome places the idea among earlier unusual seconds displays, including the Swiss railway clock of Hans Hilfiker, John C. Taylor's 2008 Corpus Clock and the 2020 Thila by Dubois Depraz.

The mechanism is a differential on the seconds: the hand shows the base movement's seconds plus an offset set by the cams. A carrier is steered by an anchor resting on two non-circular cams with periods of 50 seconds and 42.86 seconds, which turn 6 and 7 times in each five-minute cycle. Their profiles are computed so that the offset is zero at every full minute. The anchor and both cams are visible through a round 10 mm aperture at six o'clock, which shows the rhythm being made. The hours and minutes are driven 1:1 from the base movement and shown on two recessed sub-dials at 10:30 and 1:30, leaving the centre to the seconds hand, which is blued steel with a tail ending in a hollow ring that frames the sub-dial centres. There is no date.

The base calibre is an automatic Sellita SW200-1 without date, beating at 28,800 vph (4 Hz) with a 38-hour power reserve, and the escapement and going train are untouched. The display mechanism sits on the dial side of the movement, and Monochrome describes the SW200-1 as a clone of the ETA 2824, so this is a modified base movement, not an in-house calibre. The case is 904L steel, 38.0 mm by 42.1 mm and 10.53 mm thick, with a domed sapphire crystal, a crown centred on the flank, and a closed, mirror-polished case back acid-etched with the name, the reference and the number of the piece. Monochrome describes a grained dial base with a seconds-track flange and a black alligator strap on a steel pin buckle. Water resistance is not stated.

Rêveries des Secondes is a limited, numbered series of 40 pieces. Monochrome reports an expected price just under CHF 17,000 excluding taxes, with the specifications giving approximately CHF 16,800, and says both figures are provisional until the prototype is finished and production partners are chosen. No delivery date has been given. Collectors, journalists and members of the trade can register their interest on the brand's website, and a private viewing of the prototype can be arranged through the press address once it is ready.
