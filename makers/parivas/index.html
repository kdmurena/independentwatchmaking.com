Parivas is a Los Angeles watch house built on a single premise: that metal additive manufacturing, or 3D printing, can free watch design from the limits of machining. The name comes from a Latin phrase that Parivas translates as 'created from dust,' a reference to the fine metal powder from which each case begins. The founders describe the brand as having started in 2020, and present themselves as engineers and designers rather than traditional watchmakers. Their pitch is that coming from outside watchmaking is an advantage.

The team is led by Mickey Brown, co-founder and CEO, who has an aerospace engineering background including additive manufacturing development programs and led the first watch through about six years of development. The brand's own page names four co-founders: Brown; Jared Woods, Chief Creative Officer; Justin Chang, Chief Operating Officer; and Brian Cohen, Chief Financial Officer, a former defense-industry contracts manager. Press coverage typically names only Brown, Woods and Chang. Parivas says its lattice structures were first developed for fighter-jet components, and that the Exo.1 grew from that work.

The debut watch, the Exo.1 of the Genesis Collection, was announced in May 2026 and was designed in 2020 but could not be built until additive technology caught up. Its 42 mm case is printed as one monolithic piece, with the bezel, body, lugs and dial features fused into a continuous open lattice of roughly 1 mm beams, which Parivas says removes the joins and tolerances of a conventional multi-part case. The hour markers are printed hollow to hold tritium tubes, so they appear to float within the lattice. The Parivas design process starts from a single repeating unit cell and works inward toward the movement, the reverse of conventional watch design.

The case is 316L stainless steel, built from powder in layers of about 30 microns and then sintered at high temperature. Parivas calls the signature surface Solar Dusted™: a wave-like, fingerprint-like texture produced by its proprietary sintering process, which the team says emerged by accident and which varies between runs, so no two watches look alike. The company has not disclosed its printer hardware; one trade report describes the process as metal binder jetting, while others say powder-bed printing. Each watch is cleaned, finished and assembled by hand in Los Angeles by the engineers who designed it.

The Exo.1 is 42 mm across and 10 mm thick, with a 49 mm lug-to-lug distance, 20 mm lugs, a weight of about 107 g, 6 bar of water resistance and an anti-reflective sapphire crystal. Inside is the Parivas Caliber P1001S, a customised Swiss automatic skeleton movement based on the Sellita SW300-1SA, with rhodium plating and soleillage decoration, 25 jewels, 28,800 vibrations per hour, a 56-hour power reserve, Incabloc shock protection and 4,800 A/m antimagnetism. Each watch is certified to the Parivas Chronometer standard, an in-house programme developed with the Horological Society of New York that the company says exceeds ISO 3159. This is a manufacturer's claim that the sources do not independently verify. Light blue is the colour shown for sale, on bespoke straps.

Batch 01 is limited to 30 pieces, priced at $7,500, and the company says there will only ever be one Batch 01. A waitlist opened to the public on 18 May 2026, with delivery expected in the first quarter of 2027 and sales limited to buyers in the United States. Parivas says three more Genesis models are in development on the same movement family, priced from the Exo.1's level up to the mid-five-figure range, and that two further collections with their own lattice architectures are at concept stage and await advances in 3D printing.
