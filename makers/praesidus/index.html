Praesidus is an American watch brand founded around a single origin story: its first watch was a near-exact recreation of the A-11, the U.S. Army's standard-issue field watch of the Second World War, built specifically to honour paratrooper Tom Rice, who lost his own A-11 in the sea off the coast of Normandy when he jumped into France on D-Day. That founding act — reconstructing a piece of lost military history down to its original 1940s specification, rather than designing something new from scratch — set the template for everything Praesidus has built since: a brand whose primary creative language is historical fidelity to a specific American military watchmaking heritage, rather than novel design invention. The brand takes its name from the Latin word for 'defender' or 'guardian,' and its identity is built around honouring the men and women who served, often collaborating directly with veterans and their families to develop historically accurate editions tied to specific individuals, units, and campaigns.

The collection has since expanded considerably beyond the original A-11 into an extensive catalogue organised around distinct historical and thematic lines: the A-11 Type 44 (the flagship recreation, with White and Patina dial variants); the U.S. Army and Iwo Jima collections, evoking specific campaigns of the Pacific and European theatres; the D-Day Commemoration series, including annual anniversary editions such as the D-Day 82nd, built with parts sourced from an actual WWII Willys MB jeep; named Veteran Editions dedicated to Tom Rice, Vince Speranza, and the 'A-2 Bud & Diz' commission, developed in direct consultation with veterans or their families to ensure historical accuracy; the Dirty Dozen 45, referencing the legendary set of WWII military watches issued to British forces; the Service Watch line, a simpler everyday field watch; the Rec Spec and Rec Spec EX, a more modern reinterpretation with contemporary materials; the Jungle Field watch, themed around Pacific and jungle warfare service; the PAC-76 and H-75 Chronographs, extending the brand's military-watch language into chronograph complications; the A-5 UDT Diver, referencing Underwater Demolition Team dive watches; the Pathfinder 44 Bastogne Edition, tied to the Battle of the Bulge; the Spec 2 Ameriquartz, built around US-manufactured quartz movements; and the Victory Day Dress Watch, a more formal departure from the brand's core field-watch identity.

Several current references, marketed under the banner 'US-Built Movement,' use movements manufactured in the United States rather than sourced from Switzerland or Asia, extending the brand's American-manufacturing identity beyond the case and dial into the mechanism itself. Praesidus also undertakes bespoke Unit Commissions for individual military units, and operates a Veterans & First Responders discount programme and a Pass The Torch initiative connecting the brand's community with continued support for those it was built to honour.

Praesidus was founded in 2019 in New York by the father-and-son team of Remi and Oscar Chabrat — two Frenchmen building distinctly American military history, now based in Phoenix — and the brand commits five percent of every sale to partner veteran organisations as a standing policy rather than a marketing gesture. Its material storytelling has only deepened with time: the C-47 A-11 D-Day used dials cut from the actual aluminium skin of a C-47 Skytrain transport aircraft flown during the Normandy invasion, while the 2026 A-11 LMUV, released for D-Day's 82nd anniversary, recovered steel from the hood of a genuine WWII Willys MB jeep and turned it into dials for a limited run priced at $595. The 80th anniversary of V-Day in 2025 prompted the brand's first real departure from field-watch territory, the Victory Day Collection of proper dress watches — a surprise even to longtime reviewers, who noted founder Oscar Chabrat had managed something genuinely new without breaking the brand's historical-fidelity rule. Collaboration has also become a growing part of the model roster, including a 250-piece A-11 Type 44 built with French outlet The Watch Observer and US Air Force historian Grégory Pons to honour the 533rd Bomb Squadron, while fan demand has pushed practical updates too, such as a larger 38mm Jungle Field Automatic answering years of requests for a bigger case on the brand's best-selling model. Few American microbrands anywhere have built as consistent a bridge between accessible pricing, genuine military history, and literal battlefield materials as Praesidus has in barely six years.
