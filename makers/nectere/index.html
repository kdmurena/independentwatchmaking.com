Nectere Watches is a Hong Kong-based independent brand built around a single unifying creative premise: Order From Chaos, the name of its debut collection, in which each watch is conceived as a wearable meditation on the moment structure and beauty emerge from apparent disorder. Rather than drawing from a single design tradition, the collection takes its four references from distinct natural phenomena, each explored through a deliberately complex, multi-tiered dial construction that manipulates depth, texture, and light to visualise its theme in mechanical form. The debut lineup comprises The Forest, The Mist, and The Dune (each HK$5,600), alongside the collection's flagship and most conceptually ambitious reference, The Eclipse (HK$6,900) — with The Forest, The Mist, and The Dune already sold through their initial run, reflecting strong early demand for a genuinely new independent voice in the Hong Kong watch scene.

The Eclipse is Nectere's centrepiece: inspired by the total solar eclipse — the instant of perfect celestial alignment in which absolute order momentarily emerges from a chaotic universe — the watch is conceived less as a conventional timepiece than as a wearable art object capturing the precise moment of totality. Limited to 100 individually numbered pieces, its dial is a complex three-tiered black construction: a pure sunburst finish radiates outward from the centre before being abruptly interrupted by an asymmetrical sub-register detailed with fine concentric micro-ridges, the whole composition anchored by a sharply striated outer dial of precise linear grooving — engineered specifically to manipulate depth and texture in a way the brand describes as 'calculated chaos.' The 38mm × 40.5mm stainless steel PVD gold case is 8.5mm thick, fitted with a domed sapphire crystal cut with sharp, precise edges that create a captivating visual distortion at extreme viewing angles, acting as an optical lens that magnifies the dial's three-dimensional architecture.

The movement is the Seagull ST-17 ultra-thin manual-wind calibre, visible through a semi-openworked caseback with a K1 crystal open-heart window — a porthole onto what the brand calls 'the kinetic chaos that you power by hand.' The watch ships on a PVD gold mesh bracelet engineered for extreme flexibility and comfort, paired with a complimentary 18mm genuine black leather strap and a quick-release mechanism that lets the wearer swap between the two in seconds — shifting the watch's entire character, in the brand's words, 'from a brilliant corona to the deepest shadow.' Water resistance is rated to 3 ATM (30 metres), and every case is individually engraved with its unique number. Nectere formally launched The Eclipse to coincide with the next total solar eclipse, 12 August 2026 — a release date chosen with the same intentionality that defines the rest of the watch's concept.

Nectere's origin is disarmingly casual for a brand this conceptually deliberate. Co-founders James Wong and Tony Yip, childhood friends who had collected everything from trainers to cameras to furniture together over the years, were having dinner when Wong noticed an oyster shell on the table and became fixated on the randomness of the lines etched across its surface — the spark for what became chaos theory as a design language (they couldn't call the watch 'Oyster,' Wong has noted, since the term already means something specific in the watch industry). The name Nectere itself derives from the Latin verb meaning 'to connect' or 'to bind.' Wong brings a background in creative direction, print manufacturing, and modified vehicles; Yip brings over a decade inside the watch industry itself, across both traditional Swiss and German brands and modern trading platforms. The debut Order From Chaos collection launched via Kickstarter on 18 April 2025, priced from USD 475 during the campaign, and raised more than thirteen times its modest goal — over HK$690,000 against a HK$52,000 target — with first deliveries reaching backers in September 2025 despite complications from shifting US tariffs. Entering a design conversation already energised by Anoma and Berneron's asymmetrical cases, Nectere carved its own niche with a four-step, pebble-like case whose flowing, irregular curves were engineered specifically to embody the unpredictability of the butterfly effect — proof that a genuinely new independent voice can still emerge from a dinner-table idea and a well-run crowdfunding campaign.
