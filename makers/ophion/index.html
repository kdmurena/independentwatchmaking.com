Ophion

Spain
Foundedc.2013
LocationSpain
Price€1,890–3,620
Volume~50/yr, reservation-based
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Ophion was created with a simple goal: to offer the look and feel of haute horlogerie — proper guilloché, hand-finished movements, domed dials and crystals — at a genuinely accessible price, rather than the five- and six-figure sums that kind of finishing usually demands. The name comes from Greek mythology: in some tellings, Ophion was the first Titan, who together with his wife created the universe and fathered all the gods, including Cronos, god of time — a lineage the brand liked as a namesake for a watchmaker. Rather than work from a single fixed design DNA, each Ophion model sets out to revive a specific historical period or forgotten complication, reinterpreted for the present. Production is intentionally small, run in limited batches so the brand can control quality at every stage of manufacture, with reservations reopened for new batches once a series sells out. Components are sourced from specialists across several countries — cases, dials, and assembly from Germany, hands and movements from Switzerland, and straps and packaging from Spain — chosen specifically for their ability to execute complex, mostly curved and domed geometry that breaks from flat, easy-to-machine industry norms.

The collection currently spans four references. The OPH 960 looks back to 1960s watch design — domed dial, domed crystal, domed hands — translated into a 42mm 316L steel case with a hand-wound, 13¼-line double-barrel movement delivering a five-day power reserve from 26 jewels, finished with perlage, beveled and angled edges, blued heat-treated screws, and straight Colimaçon striping on the main and balance bridges. The OPH 786 takes its cues from pocket watches of the 1780s, built around a real CNC-engraved guilloché dial — chosen specifically to avoid the shallow, less reflective look of cheaper stamped guilloché — layered with a hollow-cut hour-and-minute ring sandwiched over the guilloché base, and powered by a Technotime/Soprod-based movement redesigned with symmetrical bridges and a hand-hammered finish evoking the mercury-gilding texture of 18th-century movements. The OPH 786 Vélos is a substantially reworked evolution of the 786 in an Art Deco idiom, with new teardrop lugs individually welded onto the case, a bold concave 'Vélos' (Greek for 'arrow') hour hand, double-sided anti-reflective sapphire crystals, and dials offered in CNC guilloché (blue, salmon, or silver) or radial brushed (blue or anthracite) finishes — produced in collaboration with Voutilainen-Cattin S.A. for the case and Estima AG for the hands, both in Switzerland, and CADOR GmbH in Germany for the dial.

The OPH 411 Vesper departs furthest from the pocket-watch lineage, drawing instead on the neoclassical architectural fantasies of Étienne-Louis Boullée — particularly his unbuilt 1784 cenotaph for Isaac Newton, a 150-metre sphere meant to evoke day and night through light — translated into a multilayered, almost entirely curved dial of repeating 3D micro-indices and raised hour markers meant to evoke a night sky; the watch takes its name from the Latin for 'evening star.'

The brand is the work of Miguel Morales Ribas, a young Madrid-trained architect whose route into watchmaking ran through admiration for F.P. Journe, Laurent Ferrier, and Kari Voutilainen rather than any formal horological apprenticeship — an outsider's eye that shows in Ophion's unusually candid transparency about its own supply chain, naming its component partners by name rather than hiding behind a generic 'Swiss Made' label. That transparency has won it a loyal following among enthusiast press since the OPH 960's 2015 debut, with reviewers repeatedly noting how closely the case work echoes Voutilainen and Urban Jürgensen's teardrop-lug silhouettes without ever pretending to be anything other than an homage built at a fraction of the price. Ribas has also proven willing to iterate on customer feedback directly — swapping hand styles between production runs at individual collectors' request — and to collaborate with retailers on exclusive limited runs, including a 60-piece collaboration with UK retailer The Limited Edition and watch platform The Horophile. From a single architect's conviction that haute-horlogerie finishing shouldn't require a haute-horlogerie budget, Ophion has grown into one of the most closely watched value propositions in the reservation-based independent scene — proof that a brand need not be Swiss, nor decades old, to earn a place alongside the watchmakers that inspired it.
