Mühle-Glashütte is the only watch manufacturer in Glashütte still owned by a long-established local family — the Mühle family, who have lived in the Glashütte region for more than 700 years and worked in its watchmaking industry across six uninterrupted generations. The company's origins trace to 1869, when Robert Mühle (born George Albert Mühle, 1841–1921) established his precision mechanical workshop in Glashütte after an apprenticeship with the watch manufacturer Moritz Großmann. Robert Mühle became the sole manufacturer of measuring instruments for Glashütte's watch industry and for the German School of Watchmaking, winning the Gold Medal of the City of Dresden in 1896 for his innovations. In 1905 his three sons joined and the firm became R. Mühle & Sohn, later expanding into speedometers, rev counters, and car clocks from 1918, supplying BMW, DKW, Wanderer, Triumph, and others. The company survived both the Great Depression — when many Glashütte manufacturers were forced to close — and the Second World War, but was expropriated twice: once by the Soviet administration in 1945 (when 80% of its equipment was dismantled and sent to Jena), and again in 1972, when Hans-Jürgen Mühle's rebuilt company 'Ing. Hans Mühle' was nationalised as VEB Feinmechanik Glashütte and ultimately absorbed into VEB Glashütter Uhrenbetriebe (GUB). Hans-Jürgen Mühle served as GUB's Sales Manager for marine chronometers and marine time systems, building a global network of shipyards and yacht manufacturers — a network that would become the foundation for the family's return.

German reunification opened the door. In 1994, Hans-Jürgen Mühle (born 1941) re-established the family company under the name 'Mühle-Glashütte GmbH nautische Instrumente und Feinmechanik', starting with two employees on 1st April 1994. The company began by producing marine time systems and nautical chronometers — areas of unmet demand after GUB ceased production — and in 1996, responding to a shipyard's request, launched its first wristwatches: the Herren-Sporttaucher-Uhr (men's sport diving watch) and the Marinefliegeruhr-I (marine pilot's watch). Thilo Mühle (born 1968), the fifth generation, joined in 2000 to lead product development and became sole Managing Director in 2007. Under his leadership the manufactory developed its own three-quarter plate movement (the MU 9408 chronograph, 2008), its first fully in-house hand-wound calibre (MU 9411, 2011), and the Robert Mühle RMK series of manufacture calibres (RMK 01 and 02, unveiled on the company's 145th anniversary in 2014; RMK 03 in 2016; the Robert Mühle Mondphase limited to 150 pieces in 2019). In 2018, the sixth generation entered: Thilo's son Dustin (born 1991) and daughter Fanny (born 1993) joined the company, with Dustin heading production and Fanny responsible for sales and customer care. Today 65 employees work across all areas of the manufactory, including watchmaking, service, sales, marketing, administration, and an in-house watchmaker apprenticeship.

Mühle-Glashütte's philosophy is expressed in three nautical virtues that govern every watch it produces: precision, reliability, and good legibility. Each movement — whether fully developed in-house or based on a Swiss ébauche from Sellita modified and finished to Mühle's standards — is fitted with the woodpecker neck regulation, a proprietary fine regulation developed by the company in 2003 as a further evolution of Glashütte's renowned swan neck regulation. The woodpecker neck's distinctive shape prevents the regulator arm from moving upwards under shock, making it significantly more resilient than classical spring-loaded regulators. All movements are regulated in six positions and tested to a house standard of 0 to +8 seconds per day — intentionally slightly fast, consistent with the company's commitment that no Mühle wearer should ever be late. Every watch undergoes a final inspection of nearly a full week covering accuracy, water resistance, and visual quality. The in-house Robert Mühle manufactory line calibres (RMK 01 through 03) feature the classic Glashütte three-quarter plate with exposed crown wheel and ratchet wheel, Glashütte sunburst decoration, blued screws, and the woodpecker neck, all produced to one-thousandth-of-a-millimetre tolerances on CNC machining centres. The current collection spans eight lines priced from approximately €1,200 to over €4,300: Sportivo (sports watches including the S.A.R. Rescue-Timer — the official wristwatch of the German Maritime Search and Rescue Service DGzRS for nearly 20 years), Tool Watches (S.A.R. mission and diving variants), 29er, Teutonia, Lunova, Panova, and Terrasport.
