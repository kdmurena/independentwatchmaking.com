Voutilainen

Voutilainen sits in the very highest tier of independent watchmaking — in the peer group shared with Roger W. Smith, Philippe Dufour, and F.P. Journe — and is the work of a single Finnish master watchmaker, Kari Voutilainen, born in Finland in 1962. He trained at the renowned watchmaking school in Tapiola before moving to Switzerland, where he completed a WOSTEP complicated-watchmaking course in 1989 and spent nearly a decade at Parmigiani Mesure et Art du Temps restoring some of history's rarest and most complicated antique timepieces — including, by his own account, a Breguet tourbillon pocket watch with a natural escapement that would later inspire his own movement designs. In parallel, spending over 2,000 hours of his personal time at night, he handcrafted his first tourbillon pocket watch entirely by hand. He subsequently led WOSTEP's complicated-watchmaking department before founding his own workshop in Môtiers in 2002, in Switzerland's tranquil Val-de-Travers.

The early milestones came quickly. In 2005, at Baselworld, he introduced the world's first decimal repeater — sounding hours, ten-minute intervals, and minutes — a complication of enormous historical significance. In 2007 his Observatoire model won the GPHG Men's Watch Prize, his first major international award; the watch remains among the most admired he has ever made, and demand so quickly outgrew its planned production run that roughly 50 examples were made before it was succeeded. In 2011 came the landmark Vingt-8, built around his first fully in-house movement, the Calibre 28. The Calibre 28 is distinguished by a patented double direct natural escapement using two large escape wheels of 13.6mm — approximately 30% more efficient than the standard Swiss lever — German silver plates and bridges, an 18k rose gold wheel train, and a free-sprung balance with Breguet overcoil and Grossmann internal curve, all finished entirely by hand. The Vingt-8 family expanded across the years into GMT, World Timer, retrograde, and sports executions, along with experimental variants including the reversed-architecture Vingt-8 ISO and Vingt-8 Inverse; the Vingt-8 ceased accepting new orders in December 2025. His three-word philosophy — precision, longevity, and innovation rooted in classical watchmaking — governs every decision. Design and development of movements, cases, dials, and hands are carried out entirely in-house; traditional materials — steel, German silver, and gold — are machined on CNC and automatic lathes to strict tolerances, then hand-finished with anglage, perlage, and Côtes de Genève. Each movement undergoes a first assembly for adjustment, is fully disassembled for hand-decoration, then finally reassembled by master watchmakers.

The dial work at Voutilainen is as celebrated as the movements. Hand-engraved, enamelled, and guilloché dials — many produced through the Comblémine dial manufacture (Dialtech SA, acquired in 2014 and renamed) and Brodbeck Guillochage — have earned the workshop more GPHG Artistic Crafts and Men's Watch prizes than almost any other independent: the Hisui enamel dial (2014), the GMR (2015), the 28ti and Starry Night Vine (2019), the 28SC (2020), the JI-KU (2022), the World Timer (2023), the KV20i Reversed (2024), and the lacquered 28 GML SOUYOU — created in collaboration with Japanese Urushi lacquer artist Tatsuo Kitamura — which won the Artistic Crafts prize at the 2025 GPHG. The workshop has also acquired case manufacturer Voutilainen & Cattin and several guilloché companies to preserve traditional micromechanical artistry and maintain total creative control across the entire production chain.

In 2021 Kari Voutilainen acquired the historic watchmaking house Urban Jürgensen — where he had trained early in his career and for which he had designed its iconic Breguet-style hands and dials — taking the helm as CEO alongside a group of investors; Angélique Singele was subsequently appointed as CEO of Voutilainen. Beyond his own line, the workshop supplies dials and hands for Urban Jürgensen, and Kari mentors AHCI candidate Shane Tulloch. Notable collaborations include Louis Vuitton and a project with auction house Phillips to revive the historic Zenith Calibre 135-O. Output remains deliberately scarce — fewer than 60 watches per year, with waiting lists that have stretched beyond a decade and at times led to a complete pause on new orders.
