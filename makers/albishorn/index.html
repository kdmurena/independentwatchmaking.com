Albishorn is an independent watch brand based in La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland, built around a single idea it calls Imaginary Vintage®: original mechanical watches that look as though they belong to the golden age of watchmaking but never actually existed. Each design is presented as a 'missing link', an imagined piece that could have sat alongside the classics of its era while keeping a character of its own.

The brand is led by Sébastien Chaulmontet, the owner, who sets the product vision. He is an inventor with ten patents and the author of a book and articles on the history of the chronograph. Since 2017 he has been Head of Innovation and Marketing at Sellita Watch Co SA and Manufacture AMT, after leading innovation at La Joux-Perret from 2008 to 2017 and overseeing product development for Arnold & Son and Angelus from 2010 to 2017, including the revival of both brands. Fabien Collioud is Head of Design, responsible for product design and visual identity. An engineer with a Bachelor of Science in Product Design, he has handled movement design and marketing tools at Sellita since 2017, is lead designer at Atiles Design Studio, and previously designed at La Joux-Perret (2012–2017), where he worked on movement aesthetics and many Arnold & Son watches and helped set the aesthetic codes of the Angelus revival.

Albishorn says its watches use proprietary mechanical movements with original dial layouts and displays, many with patented complications, and a focus on slim chronographs that echo vintage pieces. Most models use one of three chronograph calibres: the self-winding monopusher ALB01 A, made in La Chaux-de-Fonds (6.60 mm high, 25 jewels, 28,800 vibrations per hour, about 64 hours), which carries a patented retrograde 10-minute regatta countdown at 7 o'clock that stops at zero while the chronograph keeps running; the manual-wind ALB02 M (5.70 mm, 21 jewels, about 65 hours, COSC-certified), with a patented indicator at 12 o'clock showing reset (black), running (red) and stopped (white); and the manual-wind ALB03 M (also COSC-certified, about 65 hours). All are single-pusher chronographs with stop-seconds. The brand promises premium materials and Swiss quality standards.

The imaginary history is laid out as a timeline of invented models, each tied to a real moment. The Maxigraph (1939) is a regatta chronograph for a racer at the first Bol d'Or on Lake Geneva. The Type 10 (1948) is presented as an imagined French Air Force order and the notional ancestor of the real Type 20 chronograph. The Thundergraph (1950) is made for high-altitude Himalayan climbing, in the years leading to the 1953 Everest ascent. The Marinagraph (1958) is a new genre combining a skin-diver's watch with a regatta chronograph, with a patented regatta display. The Spatiograph (1965) is an imagined chronograph for space exploration. Albishorn gave the Type 10 a planned release date of October 2024 and the Marinagraph one of 2025.

The watches are grouped in three collections, each model limited to 99 pieces and mostly with a 39 mm 316L steel case, a sapphire crystal and 100 m of water resistance. The Air collection, tied to aviation and space, starts with the Type X-Graph (CHF 4,250), a prototype-style Type 10 with a translucent anthracite polycarbonate dial that imitates metal, a gunmetal PVD bezel and a large red anodised pusher. The three Type 10 versions, Officer (cream dial), Sihlwald (smoky green) and Classic (black), are CHF 3,950 each: a 12 mm-thick case with a rotating DLC-coated steel bezel and the ALB02 M, delivered on two straps.

The Water collection, inspired by the sea and water sports, comprises the Marinagraph Classic Fumé, Classic, Classic Racing and Paraíba Racing at CHF 3,950 each: a 13 mm-thick case with the ALB01 A and the patented regatta countdown. The Fumé has a white fumé lacquer dial, the Racing versions a bezel with a tide-indication insert tracking a 24-hour cycle, and the Paraíba a tourmaline-green dial. The Maxigraph, made with Massena LAB and priced at USD 5,495, has a silver sector dial and a caseback drawing inspired by the yacht Ylliam IV; the page showed it as sold out.

The Earth collection, inspired by mountaineering, expedition and polar exploration, includes the Thundergraph Khumbu (mint-green opaline dial) and Himalaya (petrol sunburst), CHF 3,650 each, with a 'California ghost' dial of gold-applied Roman numerals, the ALB03 M, a conical knurled 42.7 mm bezel and an oversized crown, pusher and bezel meant to be used with thick gloves; the 99 pieces are to be made over about three years. The Voisin Aérosport Ice Blue (CHF 3,450) is a slimmer time-only watch, a 38 mm case 9.6 mm thick and 50 m water-resistant, with a hand-wound COSC chronometer movement based on a Manufacture AMT ébauche and a three-layer sector dial with a snailed running-seconds sector and blued hands. It is presented as an imaginary 1930s commission inspired by aviation pioneer and carmaker Gabriel Voisin and his C28 Aérosport, but not as a replica of the car, with deliveries scheduled to start at the end of November 2026. A Voisin Aérosport Classic is also listed, but its page could not be read for this listing.
