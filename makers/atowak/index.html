ATOWAK was founded in Hong Kong in 2019 by Johnny Deng, whose team had already spent more than a decade as an OEM manufacturer helping microbrands build unique mechanical watches before putting their own name on a dial. That accumulated technical expertise shows: the brand specializes in wandering-hour complications wrapped in deliberately sci-fi case designs, a combination that has made it one of the more visible names among the new wave of affordable Chinese independents. The debut Ettore series (2020) introduced the house's four-arm wandering-hour display at an accessible price point, its design drawn from supercar lines and speedometer aesthetics; the Ettore Lite variant pushed the concept further with a dial and hands 3D-printed from copper and aviation-grade aluminum for a uniform, enduring finish. The 2022 Cobra followed with a screwless, tenon-and-mortise three-dimensional hour-wheel system built from a carbon-fiber lever and aluminum axis, its central tourbillon-like display framed as a 'gravitational field.' The brand's own name is explained as a Caesar-cipher shift of the word 'unique.'

The 2023 Spaceship pushed the format further still: a 42.5mm bullhead case (crown at 12 o'clock) with an irregularly shaped curved crystal, built around a modified Miyota 9039 automatic fitted with ATOWAK's own wandering-hours module — a 13-component mechanism with a miniature three-piece gear train driving a Starwheel-style rotating hour disc, complemented by a three-armed running-seconds wheel and a rippled matte dial, limited to 300 pieces. Both the Cobra and Spaceship won German iF Design Awards, with Cobra also named MUSE Design of the Year and Spaceship taking a MUSE Platinum Award. The Tarantula brought a patented satellite wandering-hour wheel to a three-layer, hand-polished 316L steel case (a 20-step polishing process per watch), its cobweb-like dial layers glowing pale blue under Swiss Super-LumiNova, limited to 500 pieces per colorway.

Recent collections have pushed into genuinely higher horology. The Manta-X introduced what ATOWAK calls the world's first 180-degree hexa-prism wandering-hour system, powered by the in-house AK-07BA calibre (a modified Sellita base with a 38-hour reserve) and styled around the brand's own idea of 'horological fluid dynamics.' At Geneva's Time to Watches exhibition, ATOWAK unveiled its most ambitious line yet, the CORE collection: an in-house central tourbillon, described by the brand as a symbol of the Earth's own pulsating energy, offered at a genuinely disruptive sub-£2,200 price point — a direct challenge to the assumption that a central tourbillon must carry a Swiss-scale price tag. Alongside Cosmofleet, Cobra Venom, and Windows Pro, these collections continue the same playbook of modified Japanese base movements paired with elaborate proprietary display modules, sold direct-to-consumer across dozens of countries with select limited editions now appearing on the secondary market.
