Founded in 2015 by Armand Billard — an industrial designer who sold his design agency to immerse himself in watchmaking, a craft entirely unknown to him at the time — Sartory-Billard is built on a single conviction: bespoke is not an option, it is the foundation. A watch at Sartory-Billard begins with an intention: sometimes a material, sometimes a memory, sometimes simply an idea. 'Form follows intent.' Design precedes decoration; proportion comes before detail; coherence overrides indulgence. Collectors are invited to explore guilloché patterns, precious stones, engraved surfaces, rare materials, unusual textures, personalised hands, and engravings on the caseback. Watches are often created to celebrate milestones — a birthday, the birth of a child, a promotion. Bespoke commissions also extend to small collector circles or watch clubs: very small series, sometimes only a handful, with subtle shared symbols. Armand personally designs, assembles, and finishes every piece in his Moselle workshop. The SB05 is produced in partnership with Comblémine (Voutilainen's parts manufacture) for dials and La Joux-Perret for movements. The SB10 Jumping Hour uses a sapphire disc that rotates 360° over 60 minutes, with the hour revealed at 6 o'clock in large aperture; typography specially created by designer Simon Schmidt; the surrounding ring and numerals turn blue at night. Straps by Scarlett (French bespoke leather specialist).

GPHG history: SB05 nominated 2022; SB07 lapis lazuli/meteorite 2023; SB06-24HM 'La Nuit' tourbillon 2024 (in-house calibre SBTV01, 24-hour complication, celestial dial). Exhibited at Watches & Wonders 2026. 6–9 months production per piece. 'A watch that begins with a conversation. A true Master of Bespoke.'

2025 brought a deliberate counterpoint to a decade of pure bespoke work: the SB04-E, a 39.5mm platform redesigned from the ground up as a semi-permanent, retail-ready collection rather than a one-off commission, priced from around €3,600 — Billard's way of making his colour-and-texture expertise available to collectors who don't want to commission a fully custom piece. Limited-run Editions built on that same case have pushed the dial craft further still: the Winter 2026 embosses individual snowflake geometry into a physical 3D relief before hand-painting a gradient from ice blue to snow white, capped at 26 pieces, while a Chinese New Year Year of the Fire Horse edition was engraved in the United States by artist Ron Elkins before finishing passed through Switzerland. For the brand's tenth anniversary in 2026, Sartory-Billard introduced an entirely new typology, the SB10 jumping hour, derived from the experimental SB08 sapphire-architecture project: its exposed American copper cabochon dial was CNC-engraved by Ron Elkins in the US, hand-patinated in Scotland by Chris Alexander of The Dial Artist, and finally assembled in France by Billard himself, with time read entirely off two sapphire discs whose turquoise-glowing minute track was designed specifically to solve the legibility problem that plagues most jumping-hour watches. Between the empty-canvas freedom of full bespoke commissions and an increasingly ambitious permanent collection, Armand Billard has spent a decade proving that a designer with no formal watchmaking training can still build one of French independent horology's most distinctive houses.
