Jean Daniel Nicolas is the singular watchmaking enterprise of Daniel Roth — one of the founding fathers of modern independent watchmaking, a man whose career touched Breguet, Audemars Piguet, and his own eponymous brand before arriving at the purest possible form: one watch, made entirely by hand, produced at the rate of two or three pieces per year. Roth's biography is a master class in the transmission of watchmaking genius across generations. His grandfather and great-grandfather were watchmakers in the La Chaux-de-Fonds region of Switzerland; his grandfather relocated the family to Nice, in the south of France, where he opened a clock and watch repair business. As a boy, Roth played in the shop and eventually enrolled in a three-year watchmaking programme in Nice. His professional ascent then followed the most consequential path available in mid-20th-century horology: he moved to the Vallée de Joux — the valley in the Swiss Jura then, as now, considered the centre of the world for complicated watchmaking — and secured a position at Audemars Piguet. After seven years he encountered an advertisement that changed his life: 'watchmaker wanted… for Breguet.' He left Audemars Piguet for a year's supplementary training in Le Sentier, acquiring the academic and technical foundations of complication watchmaking, then joined Breguet — then under the ownership of the Chaumet brothers and in the earliest stages of its historic revival.

Over the fourteen years that followed, Roth stamped his imprint on both the watches and the company, playing a central role in reviving Breguet and its movement supplier Lemania, and is credited with introducing what is now considered 'the Breguet look' — a design vocabulary of engine-turned dials, blued Breguet hands, and coin-edge cases that became the brand's international visual identity. He also helped create the Lemania tourbillon movement, one of the first wristwatch tourbillons ever realised. When Breguet was sold to Investcorp in the late 1980s, he departed and in 1989 launched Daniel Roth: one of the very first independent watchmaker brands in the modern era, paving the way for the generation that followed — including F.P. Journe, who went independent shortly after. The brand debuted with a completely original double-ellipse case shape, a focus on complications, and an immediate recognition from collectors and the press that here was a watchmaker of exceptional standing operating under his own name. The Hour Glass in Singapore acquired a majority stake and later sold the brand to Bulgari in 2000, which subsequently absorbed it entirely into the Bulgari branded collection.

At 55 years old, with his namesake brand gone, Daniel Roth embarked on his second career as an independent: creating the Jean Daniel Nicolas Two-Minute Tourbillon from a workshop in Rome. The brand's name is a family encryption: Jean is his son's name; Nicolas is his wife's name; and the full name Daniel Roth is contained within the initials J-D-N alongside his own. It was with his son Jean that Roth returned to independent production — the two of them working together on each watch, beginning in 2001, as a family atelier of the most intimate possible scale. The sole timepiece produced by Jean Daniel Nicolas is the Two-Minute Tourbillon, available in two case variations: a round case and a form case shaped like a figure-8, the double-ellipse silhouette echoing Daniel Roth's own original case design from his independent brand's debut. Both house the same hand-wound base calibre: twin barrels providing a 60-hour power reserve, driving a tourbillon regulator beating at a deliberately slow 18,000 A/h (2.5Hz), the carriage completing one revolution every two minutes — rather than the standard one minute — a choice that reduces the mechanical stress on the tourbillon carriage and cage components, extending longevity.

The silvered dial is hand-engraved with guilloché and dressed with blued steel hands — details that carry the direct imprint of Roth's Breguet years. The bat-shaped tourbillon bridge is the watch's most striking decorative element: the upper surface is black polished, all edges are bevelled and polished by hand, and the vertical flanks are brushed — an example, as SJX described it in their 2013 hands-on, of 'how steel should be finished.' The tourbillon carriage itself features rounded arms, equally well finished. The movement is German silver with a broad bridge featuring hand-engraved text in the characteristic slightly uneven lines that confirm the human hand's involvement; Geneva stripes are applied with an unusual broad U-shaped wave pattern that differs from any standard machine-applied finish; and the anglage on the bridge tapers to a sharp point around the ratchet wheels — a rare and demanding detail that SJX singled out as 'a wonderful detail' in their review.

The Jean Daniel Nicolas Two-Minute Tourbillon is priced at CHF 180,000 in pink gold and CHF 200,000 in platinum. It is available only through direct contact with the atelier and produced in quantities so limited — two or three pieces per year — that it occupies a category beyond mere rarity, into the realm of true intimacy: each watch is an object of personal communication between its maker and its owner. Roth has never sought celebrity. His peer F.P. Journe, who regards him as a founding father of the generation, described the trajectory simply: Roth was a pioneer who went independent before it was a recognised path, paving the way for every watchmaker who followed. SJX's review concludes with characteristic precision: 'Neither widely known or available, Roth's tourbillon is a beautiful and charming timepiece.' The Daniel Roth brand (now Bulgari) and Jean Daniel Nicolas are entirely separate entities; the historical Daniel Roth watches — the double-ellipse cases, the eight-day tourbillon, the Westminster Grande Sonnerie Carillon with four gongs, the instantaneous perpetual calendar — are highly sought by collectors through specialist auction houses and private dealers. Those who have followed Roth's full career consider Jean Daniel Nicolas the most personal and uncompromising chapter of all.
