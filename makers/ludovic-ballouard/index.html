Ludovic Ballouard

Switzerland
Founded2009
LocationAvusy, outside Geneva, Switzerland
PriceCHF 75,000–200,000
Volume~20/yr
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Born in 1971 to a French father and Dutch mother, Ludovic Ballouard grew up in a remote coastal area of Brittany among agriculture and fishing — a childhood far from watchmaking. He worked at Franck Muller, then received a call from a friend at F.P. Journe with a vacancy — initially staying only six months but ultimately spending seven years there. In his final three years at Journe, he was responsible for assembling the extraordinarily complex Sonnerie Souveraine. Before his departure, François-Paul Journe gave him the same toolbox Journe had used when he launched his own brand in 1999 — a symbolic gesture from one watchmaker to another.

Ballouard's watches are deeply philosophical. Personal tragedy — the loss of his first wife Eveline, the unexpected arrival of Flavia, the birth of his son Gabriel — left an indelible mark on the man and the watchmaker, driving him towards the central idea behind both his creations: the most important moment is the present. Debut: Upside Down (December 2009) — all 11 numerals are inverted, only the current hour flips right-side-up; there is no hour hand; the mechanism uses instantaneous switching. Priced from CHF 75,000 in red gold. Half Time (January 2012) — two counter-rotating discs show only the current hour as a complete numeral at 12 o'clock; all others appear as scrambled halves; minutes shown on a retrograde arc at 6 o'clock; the discs spin frantically and synchronize instantly at each hour change. In 2013 Harry Winston selected Ballouard to create the Opus XIII. A new chronograph project is currently in patent stage.

Ballouard explains the Upside Down's inverted numerals with characteristic directness: the hours not yet lived and the hours already gone are shown upside down deliberately, a standing reminder to hold no regrets about the past and no anxiety about the future — only the present hour rights itself. Half Time carries the same idea into more demanding mechanics: its jumping-hour movement runs over 300 components and 53 jewels inside a 25mm module, the counter-rotating disks needing to align exactly at each hour change, all visible through a hand-engraved red-gold case around an off-centre crown. Working from a small atelier in the countryside town of Avusy outside Geneva, Ballouard produces around a dozen watches a year, occasionally in striking materials such as a Henbury Edition with a genuine meteorite dial, and increasingly through fully bespoke commissions — one London workshop, for instance, worked with him to refine a Half Time's dial hierarchy and create his first-ever fully hand-painted dial bridge for a client. A collaboration with retailer CLÉ produced a Saudi Collectors Edition of the Upside Down, and secondary-market results have held remarkably firm, with recent Upside Downs and Half Times trading between roughly $70,000 and $100,000 at auction and through dealers. The long-gestating chronograph project remains in development — patented but still unreleased at the time of writing — a reminder that for a watchmaker whose entire philosophy is built around living in the present, the future arrives exactly when it's ready to.
