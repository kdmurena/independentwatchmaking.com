Baltic is a passion born from transmission. Founder Etienne Malec's watchmaking story began in 2005, when he discovered his late father's watch collection — his father was a photographer, collector, and true enthusiast who had minutely kept a diary of every watch that crossed his path in life, recording prices paid, prices for selling, and what he traded each piece for. Immersed in that diary and collection for over a decade, Etienne found both his passion and his mission; the name Baltic refers to his father's roots on the northern coast of Poland. 'This adventure is the best tribute I could pay to my father,' he has said, 'from transmission to passion, from passion to creation.' Through connections made frequenting a Paris restaurant popular with watch collectors and dealers, Etienne sourced a manufacturer for his first prototype — a chronograph drawing heavily on the Longines 13ZN step-case — then spent six months preparing a Kickstarter campaign, photographing the prototype himself with his own passion for photography brought to bear.

Baltic launched on Kickstarter on 7 April 2017 with two models, the Bicompax chronograph and the time-only HMS 001, seeking €65,000 and closing with €514,806 pledged — nearly eight times the goal, and 1,200 watches pre-sold before a single one had shipped. Etienne has described the anxiety that followed as more intense than the campaign itself: the fear of becoming a one-hit wonder once hundreds of backers had put real money behind a promise. The follow-through answered that fear emphatically — a limited-edition panda chronograph sold out its 200-piece run in a single hour in June 2018, and the Aquascaphe dive watch, a harder prototype to perfect, followed that November using the same pre-order playbook the Kickstarter had proven. By 2021, the MR01 took the wider enthusiast world by storm: the first batch sold out and delivered, secondary-market prices shot up despite promised future batches, and Baltic found itself invited to participate in Only Watch, the biennial charity auction — a remarkable credential for a brand barely five years removed from its own crowdfunding debut.

Baltic's ambition has stayed constant throughout: timeless watches of the highest quality at fair prices, sold direct-to-consumer to remove traditional industry margins and stay genuinely accessible, with no retail structure at all — customer relationships built instead through daily contact on social media. Components are produced in Hong Kong, assembled and adjusted at the Besançon atelier, with straps made in France and accessories in Italy. The catalogue has grown into four collections — Dive, Classic, Chronograph, and Archives, the last dedicated to celebrating Baltic's own now-substantial heritage — while maintaining a 4.8-star rating across more than 5,400 reviews, backed by a 24-month warranty and free exchanges and returns. What began as one collector's son honoring his father's diary has become, in under a decade, one of the most consistently successful direct-to-consumer watch brands to emerge from the Kickstarter era.
