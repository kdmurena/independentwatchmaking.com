Kurono Tokyo

Japan
Founded2018
LocationTokyo, Japan
Price$2,000–5,000
Volume~200/yr, limited drops
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Founded in 2018 (launched domestically as Chrono Tokyo before its 2019 international debut) by Hajime Asaoka — Tokyo's most celebrated independent watchmaker and one of only two Japanese members of the AHCI, whose own-name atelier pieces (Tsunami, Project-T Tourbillon, scratch-built Chronograph) sell for $25,000 to $400,000+ in single-digit annual quantities with multi-year waitlists. Kurono Tokyo exists to bring Asaoka's design language to a far wider audience: every dial, case, and hand is personally designed by him, cased and assembled in Japan using Miyota base movements, at a fraction of the price of his personal work. Sales happen exclusively through online lotteries on the Kurono website — no physical retail or dealer network anywhere in the world outside the brand's own Aoyama (Tokyo) and Shanghai salons — with each small-batch release, typically 150–300 pieces per colourway, oversubscribed ten to fifty times over within 48–72 hours, and secondary-market premiums of two to five times retail routine within days of shipping.

The catalogue unfolds as a rotating sequence of Japanese-named dial colourways, each chosen for cultural resonance rather than decorative convenience. The debut Chronograph 1 (Bunkyō Tokyo, June 2019, named for the district housing Asaoka's studio) carried the hiragana 'クロノ' on its dial and was nominated for the GPHG in 2020. The Classic three-hand line that followed from 2020 has rotated through Mokume (a wood-grain-effect Japanese lacquer dial), Toki — named for the crested ibis, a bird whose rising-sun-hued plumage is a Japanese symbol of happiness and fortune, its coral-to-vermillion sunburst deliberately engineered by Asaoka to read as distinct from the Western 'salmon' dial trend — alongside Kogetsu, Asagi, and Karakuri. The 2021 青磁:Seiji, a 500-piece celadon-blue-green lacquer release whose colour Asaoka hand-mixed himself in his atelier through countless trial samples, launched amid controversy after an initial women-only sales structure drew public criticism and was hastily revised. The 2022 Calendrier Type 1 added a serrated Art Deco bezel and month/day windows; the GMT 1 became the brand's first world-timer; and 2023 brought the Bunkyō Tourbillon, limited to 50 pieces at $50,000 — a direct bridge to Asaoka's high-end atelier work. By 2024 Kurono had shipped roughly 5,000 watches worldwide with zero physical retail presence.

The Reiwa carries special weight in the catalogue. First released in December 2019 in a 50-piece limited edition, it took its name from Japan's imperial era that began that same year upon Emperor Naruhito's accession — Reiwa translating to 'Beautiful Harmony,' embodied in a complex two-part dial pairing a metallic-sheened periphery with a serene solid-coloured centre. For Kurono's fifth anniversary in 2024, Asaoka revived the concept one final time as the 2024 Anniversary Reiwa, inspired by the Japanese lake Ruri-Numa (瑠璃沼, 'lapis marsh') and executed as a single cylindrical dial plate curving steeply toward its edges, blending a lapis-green metallic rim with a mirror-black lacquer centre — a piece so punishing to manufacture that only around five dials in every hundred produced met Asaoka's quality standard; the Reiwa line was permanently retired after this capstone edition sold out.

This level of difficulty defines Kurono's 'Grand' series, its artisan-tier masterpieces distinguished by a signature caseback bearing Asaoka's kanji calligraphy signature and personal hanko stamp — including the Grand:Akane (茨, madder red), the line's first artisan-level lacquer dial, and the Grand:Hagane (鉛, steel), whose dark-amber urushi pattern was designed to evoke the tobiyaki spots of hitatsura-style sword hamon, referencing the ultra-pure tamahagane steel of traditional Japanese swordsmithing. All Kurono urushi dials are hand-built in Kyoto by a master lacquer craftsman using natural lacquer harvested from rare trees that takes months to cure, demands a flawless dust-free surface, and carries a correspondingly high rejection rate — while Asaoka personally hand-mixes and tests the colour of most Kurono dials himself under varied lighting until each meets his standard. That obsessiveness has carried Kurono to three GPHG nominations (2020, 2022, 2023) and auction appearances at Phillips, Christie's, and Sotheby's alongside Asaoka's own atelier pieces — remarkable pedigree for what began as the accessible arm of a master's practice, and is now considered one of the most credible gateways into independent watchmaking in the world.
