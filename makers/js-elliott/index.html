Jin-Seo 'Elliott' Kim, of South Korean heritage and based in Illinois, has been fascinated by intricate mechanisms since around age 8 — and turned that fascination into one of independent watchmaking's most unusual debuts. In February 2024, at just 17 years old, he launched his first watch, the Theory One, designing the case, dial, and overall concept himself around a Sellita base movement; all 40 pieces sold out almost immediately, drawing attention well beyond the usual circle of teenage hobbies.

Later that year he followed up with the Theory Two, a 39mm, 10.9mm-thick steel watch with distinctive open-worked lugs, a blue radiant grooved dial, and a hand-made grey nubuck strap with a steel pin buckle. It runs on a Sellita SW261-1 — the small-seconds version of the SW200 automatic, finished to a higher grade with blued screws, perlage, and rhodium plating, delivering a 41-hour power reserve at 4Hz with 50m water resistance. Unlike the Theory One, the Theory Two was offered as an ongoing model rather than a hard limited edition. Reviewers have repeatedly highlighted both the compact, wearable proportions — a 38mm case, 8mm thickness, and 43mm lug-to-lug on the debut piece — and Kim's apparent instinct for what collectors want, citing it as a rare case of a teenager out-designing watches costing many times more. Widely cited as one of the youngest independent watchmakers in the world, he continues to build and design under his own name from Illinois.

Kim's path into the trade is unusually well-documented for someone so young: his fascination began at eight watching a chronograph pocket watch being repaired, by fifteen he was restoring and repairing vintage watches himself, and the Theory One design work started that same year, using 3D-printed prototypes to refine the case before working with component manufacturers to produce it, all finished and assembled in the United States. He has described his approach in interviews as trying to be a mechanical artist — drawn, as he put it, to things that work without batteries or electronics, without artifice. The Theory Two's case finishing adds a technique rarely seen at this price point: alongside brushing and polishing, sections are media-blasted, a process firing fine particles at high pressure to leave a matte, textured surface that also erases any manufacturing imperfections. Trade coverage has followed him consistently since the Theory One's debut sold out — Monochrome, aBlogtoWatch, and others have returned for each release, and J.S. Elliott now maintains a dedicated press page cataloguing the attention. What continues to strike observers is not merely the age but the judgment: reviewers have repeatedly noted that Kim's proportions, detailing, and dial design read as more considered than watches costing many multiples of his prices, a rare case of restraint and taste arriving well ahead of formal training — a watchmaker still in his teens when this catalogue was written, building, in Illinois, a genuinely independent brand from first principles.
