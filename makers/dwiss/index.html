Dwiss is a Swiss watch brand founded in 2011 in Lugano, with its address on Via Nassa, by the industrial designer Rafael Simoes Miranda. Miranda has designed watches since 2006, creating hundreds of timepieces for other brands, has taught watch design in Milan, and says he has won more than 30 international design awards including Red Dot and iF Design Awards. The brand's mission is to show time differently, under the line that any watch can tell time but Dwiss tells it differently. Each watch begins as a design concept that is then realised through Swiss engineering, and the brand says it has full creative control to explore new forms and materials. It sells limited editions, says it does not manufacture in volume, and retires each model for good once it sells out.

Dwiss's history is a run of unusual time displays and materials. In 2012 it launched what it calls the world's first niobium watch, made with the Brazilian mining company CBMM, and the C2 Niobium was limited to 41 pieces to match niobium's atomic number. Mysterious Hours arrived with the C2 in 2013 and 2014, alongside limited precious-metal editions including 18k gold. The M1 of 2011 and M2 were early automatics with a disc hour display, and the 2015 and 2016 crowdfunding campaigns, including the M2 and R1, were highlights. In 2017 and 2018 it unveiled the RS1 and RC1 tourbillons, which it describes as the first Swiss Made tourbillons from a microbrand, at CHF 14,900 each and limited to 10 pieces per model, and the RW1 wandering-hours watch. The brand also launched a Watch Design Club in 2019 and 2020, marked its tenth anniversary in 2021 with commemorative M3W and R2 editions, and ran an NFT project in 2022 and 2023.

The three current collections are the S1, the A1 and the M3S. The S1 Wandering Hours, released for the brand's 15th anniversary in 2026 and called its most ambitious watch, is inspired by the Moon's orbit: two satellite discs carry the hours across the upper dial arc, and the current hour points to the minute scale, with seconds on a central disc. It uses a modified La Joux-Perret G100 Soigné automatic with 24 jewels and a 68-hour power reserve, in a 40 mm sandblasted 316L steel cushion case 13.8 mm thick, with a 44 mm lug-to-lug, a double-domed sapphire crystal, 100 m of water resistance and an FKM rubber strap. It is limited to 50 pieces per colour and costs CHF 2,200 in blue, salmon, green or black, or CHF 2,300 in silver or orange. The brand says the S1 is in the ADI Design Museum in Italy.

The A1 PowerLume is a more conventional time-and-date automatic, with an eight-sided 40 mm by 10.8 mm steel case, a 45.5 mm lug-to-lug, 100 m of water resistance, an integrated two-link bracelet, a tapestry-finish dial with Super-LumiNova C1 X1 BL blocks and the same La Joux-Perret G100 Soigné with its 68-hour reserve. It won a 2026 iF Design Award and costs CHF 1,600 in blue, black, green or white, with an edition of 500 numbered pieces according to Time+Tide, though the brand's page states no size. Time+Tide adds that a one-of-one white Ghost version, serial 001, was auctioned on launch day, that the movement is adjusted to within about +7/-7 seconds per day, and that its article was made in partnership with the brand. The M3S Signature Hours is the brand's best-selling Mysterious Signature Hours display, with the hours on a rotating disc under a multi-layer dial, a Sellita SW200-1 automatic with a 42-hour reserve, a 42 mm by 11.7 mm case and 200 m of water resistance. It is limited to 500 pieces, at CHF 1,600 on rubber or CHF 1,800 on a bracelet, and the brand says it has won seven design awards.

Dwiss Atelier is a bespoke service that customises its existing designs or creates unique watches, with logos on the dial, custom colours, engraving and special packaging, for corporate gifts, clubs, anniversaries and small limited editions. Small changes need a minimum order of 15 watches, and the page gives conflicting lead times. Archived models include the M3W, M3, RT#80 Racing Team, R2 Floating Hours, R1 Automatic, R1 Mechanical and the classic precious-metal collection, using movements from ETA, Peseux and Sellita. The brand offers free worldwide shipping, 15-day returns and a 24-month international warranty, and says it has won 39 design awards between 2011 and 2026. Several archive pages carry inconsistent specifications, such as the R2 page repeating RT#80 movement details, so the listing does not state those figures.
