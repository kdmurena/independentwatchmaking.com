Micromilspec is a Norwegian maker of Swiss Made titanium and steel tool watches, with its design studio in Oslo. Its founder and CEO is Henrik Rye, and the company says it was established in May 2019 and signed its first contract four days later, for 330 custom watches for the Royal Norwegian Air Force's Search and Rescue Squadron (330 Squadron), whose previous military watch dated from 1972. The brand designs and builds watches for military units and select organisations such as first responders, in close collaboration with the clients, and says it has now made more than 40 custom projects. Named clients include 335 Squadron, the Asymmetric Warfare Group, 1. Frigate Squadron, the Oslo Fire Brigade, the Garrison of Sør-Varanger, French Special Forces, the Armoured Battalion, Paratroopers Norway and Sweden, His Majesty The King's Guard and the Telemark Battalion. The brand describes itself as a full-service company that handles customer support, financing, design, sampling, production, distribution and aftercare, and its custom watches are made by appointment only. Its website does not say where the watches are assembled beyond calling them Swiss Made, and it gives no details on production volumes.

The first design to reach the public was the Field Testing Unit (FTU), which grew out of a field test program that began in 2019, in which Micromilspec gave 30 timepieces to Norwegian Armed Forces personnel to evaluate performance and accuracy. The FTU's bezel, crown and caseback are deliberately unmarked so that owners can track wear and scratches, and it uses a Sellita SW200-1 with a Micromilspec rotor in a 42 mm stainless steel case. The Milgraph chronograph followed at Geneva Watch Days in 2024, honouring the brand's first 40 custom military projects, and was nominated for Best Microbrand of 2024 by Oracle Time. It has a left-hand crown and a QuadGrip bezel with four deep indents for use with gloves, in microblasted Grade 5 titanium, 42 mm by 15 mm, with a La Joux-Perret L121 chronograph with GMT, a column wheel, a 60-hour power reserve, 26 jewels and 200 m of water resistance. The 2026 Milgraph is the third edition of the annual collection, limited to 150 pieces, and the T5 variant is limited to 35.

In 2025 the brand added a Pilot collection, which traces back to one of its first commissions, a pilot's watch for the Royal Norwegian Air Force. The Dualtimer is a 42 mm by 12 mm stainless steel GMT with a Sellita SW330-2, a 56-hour power reserve, 200 m of water resistance and bi-colour Super-LumiNova, in black, white and the coloured Perfect Blue, Perfect Green and Perfect Sand versions. The Worldtimer is a 42 mm Grade 5 titanium chronograph with a La Joux-Perret L122, a ceramic bezel listing 24 cities and a 60-hour power reserve, in black or white with single or triple date. The Fieldmaster, announced on 2 September 2026 and shown at Geneva Watch Days, is a 40 mm by 11.2 mm titanium field watch with no rotating bezel. It uses the HORAGE K3, an automatic with a silicon escapement and hairspring and a power reserve of over 100 hours, in microblasted titanium and Gunpowder DLC finishes, limited to 75 pieces each, with first deliveries in May 2027.

The brand also releases civilian editions of its custom military watches and a series of collaborations. Project Sabotage, a white-dial Milgraph reinterpreted by the artist James Thompson (Black Badger) and limited to 75 pieces, was followed in 2026 by the Broken Hour, a Dualtimer with deliberately damaged hands and date window, sold only during a single 24-hour window and accompanied by a 12-page comic in the Time Wars story. The Field Testing Unit line has added the Compass and Micro GMT, each limited to 25 pieces. The Declassified Editions include the Hercules, originally made for 335 Squadron, the US Space Force and the Smokejumper, and past releases include the Voyageur, made by members of the 132nd Naval Security Unit and limited to 25 pieces, and the Monogram, a personalised dial offered for a limited period each year.

All the models are made to order with a five-year international warranty, and the watches are sold on the brand's own website and through authorised retailers in Europe and North America. The collections page shows prices from EUR 1,530 for the Field Testing Unit MicroGMT to EUR 4,250 for the Milgraph White, though many individual model pages show lower prices and most are currently listed as sold out. The brand's pages also give conflicting edition sizes and water-resistance figures for some models, which the listing avoids stating.
