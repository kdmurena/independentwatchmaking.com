Kiwame Tokyo is a Japanese microbrand founded in Asakusa — the historic district of Tokyo that has preserved traditional craftwork, artisan workshops, and heritage culture within one of the world's most modern cities for centuries. The brand is the creation of Masami Watanabe, a veteran of the Japanese watch trade who spent over thirty years introducing overseas luxury timepieces to the Japanese market, working with numerous renowned brands, and in recent years focusing on officially introducing respected international microbrands to Japan and creating Japan-exclusive collaborations between global makers and domestic collectors. Through that long immersion in independent watchmaking from around the world — observing how brands in Europe and America were shaped by their local culture and their founders' vision, often reviving vintage values through a modern lens — Watanabe arrived at a question that became his founding motivation: why does Japan, a global watchmaking powerhouse in both industrial scale and finishing tradition, have so few microbrands of its own? That question, rather than any commercial calculation, is what Kiwame Tokyo exists to answer.

The brand's name comes from the Japanese word 極め (kiwame) — to pursue something to its utmost, to refine without compromise, to take a thing as far as it can go. The word is also used in Japanese artisan culture to describe mastery: the state of having reached the ultimate expression of a craft. Watanabe's philosophy, stated with deliberate directness on the brand's website, is to pursue 'honest watchmaking' — not simply to make affordable Japanese watches, but to strip away all excess, hidden costs, and artificial luxury, and to deliver watches of genuine integrity at a transparent and fair price. The balance between that directness and the unmistakably Japanese aesthetic sensibility that permeates the watches — minimalist without being empty, precise without being cold, revealing more the closer you look — is the product of Watanabe's decades of exposure to what fine watchmaking actually requires versus what is merely performance. The Asakusa location is not incidental: it is precisely because Asakusa lives in the delicate balance between old and new, between authenticity and refinement, that it was chosen as home to the workshop. Every watch name is drawn from classical Japanese vocabulary and colour tradition, grounding the brand in a cultural register that is specific and deliberate.

The collection comprises three case families — IWAO, MUNE, and KUBO — each with two or three named colour variants drawn from traditional Japanese colour nomenclature. The IWAO (巌, meaning crag or massive rock) is the flagship form, available in GINKAI (銀灰, silver-grey) and SUMI (墨, ink-black) at JPY 92,400, both now sold out. The MUNE (棟, meaning ridge or rooftop) follows with USUKI (浅黄, pale yellow) and KUROTSUKI (黒月, black moon) at the same price, also sold out. The KUBO (窪, meaning hollow or concave) is the newest family, at JPY 84,700, in three variants: USUKI (浅黄, pale yellow), TETSUKON (鉄紺, iron navy), and SAKURA (桜, cherry blossom — the pink dial visible in the provided image); all three are currently sold out. The design language across all three families is unified by the same philosophy: light and shadow, proportion and texture, restraint that rewards sustained attention. Movements are Swiss automatic. A physical salon is available for visitors to the Asakusa workshop by appointment.
