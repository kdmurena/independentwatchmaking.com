Auret is a Colombian independent watch house, founded by the oncologist and watch collector Astor Aguirre Santamaría and described by the brand as 'conceived in Colombia, with a Swiss heart' (its motto is 'Swiss Made. Colombian Soul.'). Aguirre says his work with cancer patients gave him a sense of time as a privilege, both in waiting and hoping and in the moments shared with loved ones, and that Auret grew from his wish to turn that outlook into objects that accompany people and last. The brand line is 'We don't measure time, we honour it.' The sources name no Colombian city and no founding year; the first collection was announced in November 2026.

The name draws on the idea of aura, a quiet presence that stays. The number twelve, which the brand calls Aeternum, is its founding code: a completed cycle that allows a new beginning, standing for origin, balance and expansion. Its outline appears in the R of the logotype, and its geometry shapes the twelve-sided bezel of the first watch. Auret says its house rests on five forces: presence, intention, expansion, humanity and legacy. Colombia is said to contribute origin, humanity and an emotional view of time, and Switzerland the watchmaking tradition, precision and discipline.

Mederi, from the Latin for 'to heal', is Auret's first collection and comes in three versions that differ only in dial colour: Origin (black, 'time that builds'), Horizon (blue, 'time that inspires') and Permanence (burgundy, 'time that transcends'). All three are marked as a Founding edition and cost USD 1,990 (EUR 1,750) before taxes and import duties, which the buyer pays. They ship insured from Auret's atelier in Switzerland from November 2026, in a walnut case. No edition size is stated.

The 316L steel case is 40 mm across, 10.5 mm thick and 46 mm lug-to-lug, with a twelve-sided polished bezel, a brushed and polished mid-case, a screw-down crown, sapphire crystals front and back and 100 m of water resistance. There is no integrated bracelet; each watch comes with two quick-release straps matched to the dial, and a custom steel buckle bearing the Auret symbol. The dial has a concentric wave guilloché in a fumé gradient, brightest at the centre and darkest at the edge, with faceted applied hour markers and small seconds at 6 o'clock. Time+Tide, which reviewed the watch, praised the dial, proportions and movement and criticised the lack of a bracelet and a price it considered high for a debut brand.

Inside is the automatic Sellita SW260 in D4 grade, running at 28,800 vibrations per hour with a 41-hour power reserve, showing hours, minutes and small seconds. The finishing includes rhodium-treated and nickel-plated parts, heat-blued screws, Côtes de Genève on the bridges, perlage on the mainplate and snailed barrels, visible through the sapphire caseback, under a custom brass rotor shaped like the flower of the Colombian coffee plant. The watches carry a transferable two-year international movement warranty and a 14-day return period for unworn watches in full packaging, with servicing recommended every five to seven years. Two of the press sources, Monochrome and Time+Tide, say the coverage was sponsored or made in partnership with Auret.

Auret also plans The Auret Circle, an editorial project in which twelve people from different disciplines each answer twelve questions about time, ten shared by everyone and two personal. From January 2027 one conversation is to be published each month, forming a first volume of twelve chapters that the brand intends to compile into a printed book. Sign-up for the conversations is free.
