NOMOS Glashütte is an owner-operated, independent German manufacture founded in 1990 by Roland Schwertner, based in Glashütte — a small town in the Ore Mountains of rural Saxony that has been the ticking heart of German watchmaking for over 175 years. Glashütte's watchmaking history began on 7 December 1845, when Dresden watchmaker Ferdinand Adolph Lange, backed by an economic grant from the Saxon King Friedrich August II, brought the craft to a town whose silver and copper mining industry had run out of ore. Lange trained local youth in the trade, organised production along the Swiss model of divided specialist labour, and founded a watchmaking school that drew students from across Europe, including Switzerland — the beginning of what the town's own history calls 'the myth of Glashütte.' Watchmaking survived two world wars and the GDR-era centralisation of the industry under the state-owned Glashütter Uhrenbetriebe, and the accumulated knowledge and skill of that history remains the foundation of the craft practised there today. Since 2022, German law has granted watches from Glashütte a protected designation of origin — extremely rare for a manufactured product — under which the name 'Glashütte' may only appear on a dial if at least 50 percent of the value of its caliber was created in the town; NOMOS calibers are made almost entirely there.

NOMOS operates from five buildings across the town: the 'Bahnhof' (former train station) housing senior management, product development, and logistics; a dedicated production hall in the Schlottwitz district, built in 2017 to modern sustainability standards with a local larch-wood façade, partial self-generated electricity, and heat recovery from its own machinery; a historic chronometry building — once a marine chronometer workshop, doubled in size in 2014 — where calibers are assembled, regulated, and encased; a converted 19th-century Catholic church that now hosts the annual Forum NOMOS Glashütte industry gathering; and the NOMOS flagship store, the Kaufhaus, housed in the very building where Ferdinand Adolph Lange opened his first Glashütte workshop in 1845. NOMOS's senior management received Germany's Federal Cross of Merit in 2024 for their contribution to the town's international reputation and their commitment to openness and diversity.

Every mechanical movement inside a NOMOS watch is a caliber from Deutsche Uhrenwerke NOMOS Glashütte (DUW) — developed in-house and manufactured in the company's own workshops, none bought in from outside movement suppliers. The automatic range includes the DUW 3001, DUW 6101, DUW 3202 (with a distinctive gold globe on its winding rotor, signalling its worldtime function), DUW 5001, DUW 5101, and DUW 5201, all built around a highly efficient bidirectional-winding rotor. The hand-wound range includes the original Alpha alongside the DUW 4001, DUW 4601, DUW 4101, DUW 4301, and DUW 4401 — movements the brand frames around the particular pleasure of the winding ritual itself. At the top of the range sit the Atelier calibers, DUW 1001 and the tonneau-shaped DUW 2002, reserved exclusively for the gold watches Lambda and Lux: movements made almost entirely by hand, with twin mainspring barrels giving an 84-hour power reserve, swan-neck fine adjustment, a screw balance, gold chatons hand-polished in balsa wood, hand-bevelled edges, and a balance cock hand-engraved with the phrase 'Mit Liebe in Glashütte gefertigt' — made with love in Glashütte. Across the whole range, NOMOS movements carry the hallmarks of Glashütte finishing: the three-quarter plate spanning the mainspring barrel and gear train, Glashütte sunburst decoration on the ratchet and crown wheels, and the specially shaped Glashütte stopwork for smooth, controlled winding. NOMOS also developed its own proprietary escapement, the NOMOS swing system, whose fitting demands a watchmaker's steady hand and years of experience.

NOMOS's design language traces directly to the Deutscher Werkbund, the design network founded in 1907 to bring quality and aesthetics to industrial production, whose ideas gave rise to the Bauhaus school in 1919 — a shared vision of art and craftsmanship united in reduced, functional forms manufacturable to the highest standard, 'good design for many.' NOMOS is itself a member of the Werkbund today. The brand's original 1992 collection of six hand-wound stainless steel watches — Tangente, Ludwig, Orion, and Tetra, in round and square cases with Arabic numerals, Roman numerals, or indexes — established a design vocabulary that remains almost unchanged across the current range. Tangente, inspired by a 1930s Glashütte model, is Germany's best-known wristwatch: a straight-lined case with angled lugs, a silver-plated dial, narrow serif typography, and blued steel hands. Every model family — cylindrical Tangente, lenticular Orion, fine-bezelled Ludwig, conical Minimatik, the sportier Club, and the gold Lambda and Lux — carries its own case shape, crown, lug treatment, typography, and hand design, extending to distinctive NOMOS 'thread hands,' Glashütte azurage grooving on sub-seconds dials, and complications including date, power reserve, and GMT or full worldtime displays integrated onto the dial with the same restraint applied to everything else. NOMOS watches have won more than 175 design and watchmaking awards, including the Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève for the Tangente neomatik 41 Update.
