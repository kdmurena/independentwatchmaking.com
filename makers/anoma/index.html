Anoma is a watch brand founded in 2024 by Matteo Violet-Vianello, a Paris-born, London-based creative director who is not a watchmaker or designer by trade. Press describes the brand as London-based, with its watches designed in London and made in Switzerland. The brand's own pages do not state its headquarters, which is why the listing relies on press for the London base. Violet-Vianello grew up taking apart vintage watches from a drawer at his family home in Paris, worked at Sotheby's, then spent four years as one of the first employees at the rare-watch platform A Collected Man, where he sourced rare watches and worked with independent watchmakers. He is a Geneva Watchmaking Grand Prix (GPHG) Academy member. The name is short for anomaly, and the brand describes its products as wearable sculptures drawing on art, architecture and nature.

The first watch, the A1, was announced in 2024. Its rounded triangular, lugless case was inspired by a 1950s free-form table by Charlotte Perriand that the team saw in a Parisian antique gallery, together with river stones and the modernist sculptures of Constantin Brancusi. The 316L steel case is 39 mm wide and 9.45 mm thick, with the bottom 3 mm curving inward, and wears closer to 37 mm. It has a hidden crown, a sapphire crystal with anti-reflective coating, 50 m of water resistance and an 18 mm Italian leather strap, and uses a Swiss automatic Sellita SW100, a thin calibre of the sort often found in women's watches. The A1 has been issued in several versions. Slate, a vertically brushed grey dial, came in 2025 at GBP 1,800 excluding taxes and was not numbered. The Optical, with an op-art dial of 50 hand-engraved offset triangles, is in copper and silver. Abyss, in three lacquer layers of blues, and Stone, with a blasted dial, followed in first runs of 300 pieces.

The A1 Prehistoric, announced in July 2026, takes the same form and adds a hand-chiselled case and buckle, inspired by the hand axes and arrowheads that Brancusi collected, which founder Violet-Vianello saw at an exhibition at the Centre Pompidou. The French engraver Steven Brunel, who works in Mornand-en-Forez, spends about five hours on each case, and the series of 100 took almost a year. The dial is a brass base with roughly 600 hand-cut sunburst lines in anthracite, with no printed markers or logo. It is limited to 100 pieces for 2026 and costs GBP 3,480 including taxes, about GBP 2,900 before them.

The A2, announced in November 2026, is a more sculptural second watch, an oval, Möbius-inspired one-piece steel case that spirals clockwise, topped by a 4 mm faceted sapphire crystal that twists the opposite way. The case measures 41.5 mm by 35.7 mm and 9.8 mm thick, and wears like a 38 mm round watch, with 50 m of water resistance. The dial is vertically brushed teal-blue lacquer with domed, mirror-polished hands four times thicker than usual, inspired by Brancusi's Bird in Space. It uses a hand-wound La Joux-Perret D100 (Fratello gives D101, and its figures for the beat rate conflict), and the price is GBP 2,880 including taxes. Fratello says it is not a limited edition, but part of the core collection, with first-batch deliveries expected in April 2027.

Anoma's site also hosts an Anomalies page of testimonials from collectors, including John Goldberger. Watches carry a two-year warranty, and the brand ships from within the USA for American customers, with tariffs and duties included. The press coverage that the brand shows includes The New York Times and GQ, and Hodinkee has covered the A1 Optical. The movements are bought-in Sellita and La Joux-Perret calibres, and the brand does not describe itself as an in-house maker.
