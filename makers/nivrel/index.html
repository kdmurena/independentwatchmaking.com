Nivrel

Germany

Nivrel is a family-run watch brand in Saarbrücken, the capital of the Saarland in southwestern Germany, operated by Gerd Hofer GmbH. It describes its watches as 'the German watch with a Swiss heart': they are designed, finished and assembled in Germany, and every mechanical movement is a Swiss ébauche that Nivrel refines and decorates. The brand's slogan, 'Coeur de la Sarre', expresses solidarity with the Saarland and the Saar-Lor-Lux region. The company says it is 'not an in-house movement manufacturer' and rejects the idea that an in-house movement is automatically better. In a press release dated 14 September 2026, the Hofer family announced that Nivrel will close by July 2027, calling it a planned decision rather than the result of a crisis. The last watches remain on sale through the brand's e-boutique and the family jewellery store Juwelier Kraemer in Saarbrücken, a farewell celebration is planned, and the family says all obligations will be honoured and that customers will be told how servicing will continue.

The brand was introduced in 1936 by Marvin, the Swiss manufacture that Marc and Emmanuel Didisheim founded in St-Imier in 1850. Nivrel made men's chronographs and calendar watches and small women's watches, and both Marvin and Nivrel disappeared from the market after the quartz crisis. The Kraemer family opened its jewellery and watch store in Saarbrücken on 4 October 1891, and Gerd Hofer, the founder's great-grandson, built a career in the Swiss watch trade, including as Milus's sales agent for Germany, Austria and the Netherlands. In 1993 Hofer and his wife Gitta bought the Nivrel brand, moved production to Saarbrücken and introduced the first automatic watches, using parts of original Kelek movements and other movements. Hofer died suddenly in 2008, according to the press release, and his daughter Dr. Anja Hofer took over management with her mother Gitta and her husband Guido Grohmann.

Case and dial design take place in Saarbrücken, while Nivrel buys cases, small parts and dials from German suppliers. Final construction, fine finishing and functional refinement of parts, assembly, quality control and service are done in-house, and Nivrel describes most parts as German-made. Movements are engraved with the brand logo, and depending on the edition the finishing includes polished and engraved bridges, flame-blued screws, gold-plated wheels, engraved balance cocks and skeletonised movements. Cases are 316L stainless steel or solid 14 or 18 carat gold with sapphire crystals that are anti-reflective on both sides, and many leather straps use CITES-certified Louisiana alligator. The brand says it avoids costs such as heavy advertising, celebrity ambassadors and several trade layers, and offers engraving, skeletonising and other custom requests within its technical capabilities.

The collection is organised in editions. Édition Héritage marks the 1993 restart and has a straight case, a Clous de Paris guilloché dial and blued steel hands, in models such as the Héritage Automatique (40 mm, ETA 2824-2), chronographs, an alarm and a skeletonised watch. The Héritage Minier is a pocket watch honouring the Saarland coal-mining tradition, with a sterling silver case and a hammer-and-chisel engraving, limited to 200 pieces. Édition Replique revives historical Nivrel models, starting with the 1994 reference N 515.001, a homage to a 1940s Nivrel chronograph with a Lemania 1873 hand-wound movement, limited to 100 pieces; it also includes the Replique Manuelle with an ETA Unitas 6498-1, the Jubilé III made for the 80th anniversary in 2016, and Répétition Classique repeaters. Édition Plongeur is a line of dive watches that includes the Deep Ocean, the Wild Sea chronograph and the Red Voyager, limited to 100 pieces, with a 316L case rated to 20 bar and an ETA 2824-2.

Édition Pièce Unique is the brand's flagship line, the watches that matter most to Gerd Hofer, a former goldsmith who combined fine mechanics with goldsmith's work. It is made in the family goldsmith workshop with engravers, skeletonisers and miniature painters. The line is mostly repeaters with themes such as the dragon, horse, scorpion, polo and golf, and uses engraved or skeletonised gold plates, fully engraved and skeletonised movements, miniature paintings on sapphire and engraved gemstones such as agate and rock crystal. The brand also takes part in the 'Made in Germany' label of the German association of the jewellery and watch industry. Prices listed on the website range from about EUR 503 to EUR 24,202 excluding VAT.
