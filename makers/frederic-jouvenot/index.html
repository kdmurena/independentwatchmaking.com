Frédéric Jouvenot is a Swiss master watchmaker whose entire career has been a study in mechanisms that tell time without telling it the usual way. Born in Switzerland in 1977, he earned his federal certificate in micromechanics and watchmaking in 1998, a technical diploma in micro-technology in 2001, and a commercial diploma in 2005 — the complete toolkit of an engineer as much as an artisan. In 2006 he co-founded Concepto Ltd. in La Chaux-de-Fonds, today a serious manufacture of fine movements and complications employing over 100 people and supplying calibers to numerous brands (among them the atelier of fellow independent Frank Jutzi). Two years later, in 2008, he launched his eponymous house with the ACE — the Automatic Chronograph Evolution — a disruptive design that moved the winding mechanism onto the dial side, in full view. His talent for reinvention soon reached beyond his own name: in 2012 Dior commissioned him to develop the celebrated 'Dior Inversé' caliber for the Grand Bal collection, two versions of which were pre-selected at the 2014 GPHG and became some of the house's best-sellers; in 2017 he created and developed the jumping-hours movement for Corum's new Admiral Cup. In 2019 he developed the Gloria collection for Asian collectors, with unique pieces hand-engraved by master engraver Jean-Bernard Michel in La Chaux-de-Fonds.

His own collections chase a single obsessive idea: a watch face that reads like a sundial, with no hands, no numerals, and no discs — the first time display of its kind in watchmaking history. The mechanism, a patented '24-hour jumping cage,' comprises roughly 100 components inside a cage barely 13mm across, built from twelve independently pivoting micro sunbeams. At the stroke of noon every beam turns gold, representing the sun at its zenith; as the hours pass they flip to black one by one, clockwise, darkening the face toward midnight, then reverse course and turn gold again as day returns — a full mechanical sunrise-to-sunset performed on the wrist every 24 hours. In 2011 the resulting Solar Deity collection, and its flagship Helios model, won the Superwatch Award at the Geneva Time Exhibition; the Surya, in tsavorite, was selected at the 2014 GPHG. Recent Solar Deity pieces like the Helios Aventurines wrap this micro-mechanical sun in a black-and-pink-gold-toned aventurine dial, a titanium case, and rhodium-plated Côtes de Genève bridges, limited to 18 pieces.

The Moon Deity collection extends the concept to night and day together. Named for Nânna/Sîn, the Mesopotamian moon deity and mythological father of the sun god Shamash, the flagship Sîn model displays a rotating moon-and-sun face that marks the passage of a full 24-hour day-night cycle through the same patented jumping mechanism — in titanium or steel, a manually wound movement with 42 jewels, a screw-adjusted balance at 28,800vph, and a 50-hour reserve, each material limited to 18 pieces. Every Frederic Jouvenot piece, Solar or Moon Deity alike, is unique and built strictly to order, with a standard three-month delivery — the deliberate pace of an atelier where a movement co-founder turned complications inventor has spent two decades teaching watches to show the sun and moon's own passage across the sky, rather than simply counting the hours.
