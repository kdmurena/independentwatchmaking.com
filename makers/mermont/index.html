Mermont is a Geneva-based watch project born from a philosophy rather than a commercial ambition — a love letter to sincere watchmaking, as its founders describe it. The name is a contraction of the French words 'MERveilleuse MONTre' — marvellous watch — and it is anchored to a single, quietly radical idea: that the most beautiful moments in life are not measured but lived, and that a watch should invite its wearer into presence rather than imposing urgency. The brand takes its guiding inspiration from Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's famous definition of perfection: 'Il semble que la perfection soit atteinte non quand il n'y a rien à ajouter, mais quand il n'y a rien à enlever' — perfection is achieved not when there is nothing left to add, but when there is nothing left to remove. That principle governs every decision in Mermont's inaugural watch, which is called La Parfaite — the perfect one — and which carries it to a logical, almost provocative conclusion: a single hand.

La Parfaite has one hand — the 'solo hand' — displaying only the hour, with no minutes and no seconds. This is not a limitation but a philosophical position: by removing the minute and second hands, Mermont deliberately slows the apparent passage of time on the wrist, giving the wearer a different relationship with their day. The brand describes this as 'regulated to the highest standard with a deliberately approximate display' — the movement is finely regulated for accuracy, but the display offers only the hour, inviting the wearer to inhabit the moment rather than monitor it. The solo hand itself is a masterpiece of individual craft: it is black polished, heat-treated steel, of unusual shape and volume — visually substantial, commanding the dial with presence — and it is hand-polished by Nicolas Delaloye himself using traditional techniques. The dial is equally considered: finished in a rarely seen 'colimaçon' (French for snail or spiral) sunburst pattern — a more complex variant of the standard sunburst in which the radial brushing follows a spiral path outward from the centre rather than a simple radial line — creating spectacular, ever-changing interactions with light across the dial surface. The blue hue is achieved à l'ancienne using coloured pigments mixed with Zapon varnish, a traditional lacquer technique, giving the colour a depth and transparency that modern coating processes cannot replicate. The 38mm × 9.2mm case is platinum (Pt950) with a stamped hallmark, paired with a tantalum caseback — the same material combination Nicolas Delaloye uses in his own Delaloye Genève Renaissance collection. The single sapphire crystal carries an internal anti-reflective coating, and a 20mm × 16mm textile strap completes the presentation.

The movement is the La Joux-Perret D101 in 'finition soignée' grade, hand-regulated by Delaloye to his personal standard, with a 50-hour power reserve and 30 metres water resistance. Every Mermont watch is finished, assembled, regulated, and quality-controlled by Nicolas Delaloye himself — the same maître horloger who trained at the Geneva Watchmaking School in 1987, worked at Patek Philippe, spent eight years heading the restoration department at the Patek Philippe Museum, and created his own acclaimed Delaloye Genève brand whose tantalum-cased Renaissance watches are regarded as among the most carefully finished independent watches produced in Geneva today. The Mermont project is the meeting of that pedigree with a distinct creative vision — a collaboration between watchmaker and brand founder that places Delaloye's hands and conscience at the centre of every piece produced. Assembled in Geneva in limited quantities, Mermont speaks to enthusiasts who, in the brand's own words, 'appreciate quiet horological substance over hype and spectacle.'
