Anonimo

Anonimo was founded in Florence in 1997 and built its early reputation in professional diving, especially among members of the C.N.S. (Cooperativa Nazionale Sommozzatori), Italy's national divers' cooperative. Its dive watches use a screw-in crown locking system and fully water-resistant pushbuttons, and the brand says they were rated waterproof beyond 1,000 metres, with a model called the Professionale tested successfully at 2,000 metres. Working with experts and professionals, the company developed patented inventions that led to several product lines, including the Militare, known for its winding crown guard at 12 or 6 o'clock.

In 2003 the military engineer Dino Zei joined permanently and developed the Nautilo line, paying close attention to design and materials such as bronze; Anonimo says it was the first watch brand to put a diving watch in that alloy on the market. In 2013 the headquarters moved to Switzerland and the company became compliant with the Swiss Made label. The range was later narrowed to the Militare and Nautilo lines, and the Epurato collection was added in 2018 with a more refined, smart look. The brand describes itself as combining Italian design with Swiss technology under the banner of 'the joy of living,' and the pages consulted name no owner, and no Swiss town for the headquarters.

The design signatures are cushion-shaped cases inspired by Italian elegance and a triangle of indexes at 4, 8 and 12 o'clock that refers to the 'A' of Anonimo. The leather straps are handmade in Italy. All watches use Swiss automatic movements from Sellita: the SW200-1 in the Nautilo and Epurato, and the slim SW300-1 in the Militare Chrono, paired with a Dubois-Dépraz 2035-M bi-compax chronograph module that the brand says was developed exclusively for Anonimo.

The Militare is the flagship line, originally built for professional use. It has a 43.5 mm case 14.5 mm high, 120 m of water resistance and a crown guard at 12 o'clock, where a patented mechanism between the crown horns provides the water resistance, and a 38 to 42-hour power reserve depending on the model. It comes as a three-hand watch with a date at 9 o'clock or as a chronograph with a 30-minute counter at 9 and small seconds at 3. The Militare Automatic Vintage is CHF 2,590, the Chrono and Chrono Vintage start at CHF 3,890, and the Chrono DLC is CHF 3,990; the bronze-case Automatic, the Oxidized Bronze Chrono and the limited Alpini Chrono Camouflage editions are sold out.

The Nautilo is a contemporary version of the first professional diver's watches, with a 42 mm case, a crown at 4 o'clock, a distinctive graduated unidirectional bezel and a screw-down bezel and open caseback that give it 200 m of water resistance. The 42 mm and 42 mm Vintage versions start at CHF 2,290, and a steel bracelet for them is CHF 300. The Nendaz Freeride special edition is sold out.

The Epurato, the 'urban touch' of the brand, is a slim 42 mm watch for women and men with a fluted bezel of alternating satin and polished surfaces, a crown integrated at 3 o'clock and a 38-hour power reserve, and a movement with a circular-grained finish and a Côtes de Genève oscillating weight. The Steel is CHF 2,290, the DLC CHF 2,490 and the Bronze CHF 2,990. The Montreux Comedy Festival 30th Anniversary edition is CHF 2,290, and the Les Rencontres Jean-Marc Quarin special edition is sold out. Alongside the watches the brand sells spare straps in antelope leather, calfskin and felt.
