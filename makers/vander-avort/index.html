Vander Avort is an independent Belgian luxury watch brand from Leuven, where the company keeps a boutique at Margarethaplein 7-8. It describes itself as a family atelier that has made heirloom-quality pieces in 18-karat gold since 1985, which is the year in its name, and it began making watches under its own name in 2019. Its first and so far only watch is the Lovon, a limited edition of 35 pieces in 18-karat gold, made in Switzerland and finished in Belgium.

The brand was started by two childhood friends from Leuven who are both second-generation entrepreneurs. Maxime Vander Avort is a jeweller, goldsmith and gemologist who became interested in watchmaking after designing a watch from scratch for a friend, and who now devotes his design work to watches. Mathieu Van Eycken grew up around steel and construction, leads a family business that the brand says spans three generations, and oversees operations and complex material projects. In 2019 they set out to make a watch they would wear themselves, one that honoured their heritage and would outlast trends rather than follow them. They named it Lovon after Leuven, and the brand says it took six years to develop.

The Lovon has a 40.9 mm case in 18-karat 4N rose gold or palladium-alloyed white gold. The design has no bezel and its lugs are screwed on rather than soldered, and the brand says the case draws inspiration from a ring. It is offered in seven colour variations of five pieces each, each watch is individually numbered, and it is worn on an alligator strap made in Oostende. The parts come from across Switzerland: the dial from Geneva, the hands from Fleurier, the case from the Vallée de Joux, and the movement from Le Locle, where it is assembled.

The movement is the automatic Caliber VA01, whose development was directed by the master watchmaker Jean-François Mojon, known for complex contemporary mechanisms. It shows hours, minutes, central seconds and the date, beats at 4 Hz (28,800 vibrations per hour) with about 60 hours of power reserve, measures 31 mm by 4.15 mm, and has 207 components and 35 jewels. The bridges carry Côtes de Genève, the bevels are hand-polished and the parts are rhodium-plated, and the one component the brand singles out as its own is an exclusive tungsten rotor. The brand backs the movement with a five-year warranty. The site does not say whether the base calibre, escapement or other parts are made in-house or by a supplier, so this listing does not claim an in-house calibre.

Each case is hand-engraved in Belgium by the master engraver Bram Ramon of Tongeren, working under a microscope in the tradition of gravure d'art. The motif is taken from the Gothic windows of Leuven's Town Hall and St. Peter's Church, a discreet tribute to the brand's home town. Prices are not published and viewings are by appointment at the Leuven boutique or at a location of the client's choice.
