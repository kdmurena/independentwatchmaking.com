Sergey Chutov

LocationMoscow, Russia / Yerevan, Armenia
PricePOA
Volume3 pieces per limited edition
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Sergey Chutov is an independent watchmaker with one of the more unusual career-change stories in contemporary horology: through 2018 he worked as a civil judge in Moscow, growing increasingly frustrated with the legal system until he made the decision to walk away from law entirely and pursue a lifelong dream of becoming a watchmaker. He spent the next four years learning the craft from the ground up and refining his skills, before releasing his first model, the Fortress — a watch whose dial design draws directly from architectural fortification, with hour and minute indicators shaped to resemble the observation windows used by tower guards keeping watch over the walls below. Chutov has cited the example of other small independent watchmakers who struck out to build watches entirely on their own terms as the inspiration that gave him the confidence to devote himself fully to the new craft, leaving behind a stable legal career for the uncertainty and discipline of the bench. He now divides his time and operations between Moscow and Yerevan, Armenia.

The Fortress concept has since expanded into a full family of limited-edition variations, each exploring a different material, colour, and mechanical personality while retaining the tower-window dial architecture that defines the line. Fortress I introduced the concept with a striking blue textured dial set within a strong bronze surround, leaf-style hands, and a large diamond-style crown. Fortress II traded the blue for a more discreet, beautifully textured grey dial, while swapping the leaf hands for skeletonised dauphine-style hands and a more compact straight push-pull crown — a sportier, more understated read on the same architecture, with a sub-seconds display integrated cleanly into the tower-window motif. Fortress III, limited to just 3 pieces, pushed the finishing further: a dial made of pure silver, a laser-treated movement finished in rose gold, and skeletonised wheels visible through the dial apertures, with special attention paid to how the time-indicating lines connect harmoniously across the dial face. Chutov has stated his intention to complete two further limited Fortress models, each in a run of 3 pieces with different colours and movements, before moving on to an entirely new design language — treating the Fortress not as an indefinitely extended product line but as a bounded creative statement.

Fortress IV took inspiration from the sea: a Coral-dial model presented in a newly polished case fitted with a sapphire crystal carrying five layers of anti-reflective coating. The upper portion of the dial is crafted from German silver and attached to a copper base via micro-welding, finished in matte varnish, paired with stainless steel sword-style hands and a Unitas 6498-2 Top Grade movement. Fortress V followed strong positive response to the original Fortress I's blue dial, reinterpreting the colour as 'Royal Blue' — a shade achieved by layering several coats of paint from light to dark blue, a technique chosen specifically because it causes the dial to shift visibly between lighter and darker tones as it moves through sunlight. Fortress V also carries green luminous hands and houses the Unitas 6497-2 Top Grade Cal. db9 (Black) movement, and its top section is likewise finished in German silver, here with a glossy rather than matte varnish. Beyond the core Fortress series, Chutov's catalogue includes further limited editions exploring different aesthetic directions: the Azure Anniversary model, the Kaleidoscope, Silver Fortress, Eagle, Golden Fortress, and Phantom — each a distinct, small-batch expression of Chutov's architectural, hand-finished approach to watchmaking. Sergey Chutov can be contacted directly via WhatsApp, Viber, or telephone, with operations spanning both Moscow and Yerevan.
