Atelier Wen is a Sino-French independent watch brand founded in 2018 by Robin Tallendier and Wilfried Buiron — two French friends who met while studying in Beijing. Robin had built a career at the intersection of horology and China, advising the China Horologe Association and working with the French Chamber of Watchmaking in Shanghai and Christie's in London; Wilfried had studied at Peking University and Tsinghua University. Their name combines the French 'atelier' (workshop) with the Chinese character 文 (Wén, meaning culture and refinement) — 'culture workshop.' The brand is headquartered at 21/F, CMA Building, Connaught Road Central, Hong Kong. Their foundational mission is both specific and bold: to prove that Chinese craftsmanship in watchmaking can stand alongside — and in some cases surpass — anything produced in Europe, and to do so by engaging the finest artisans in China at every stage. This is not a branding exercise but a genuine industrial and cultural project, executed at increasing levels of ambition and price with each new collection. The debut Porcelain Odyssey, launched on Kickstarter in 2018 and priced around USD 700, met its funding goal in under 30 minutes. Every subsequent release has pushed further in materials, craft, and price.

The Perception, launched in 2022 and priced around USD 3,200, was the watch that changed the brand's standing internationally. An integrated bracelet sports watch in 904L stainless steel, it introduced hand-guilloché dials produced by the workshop of Master Cheng Yucai — China's first certified guilloché master craftsman, whose Écaille de Poisson (fish scale) pattern became the brand's most recognisable motif and drew comparison to the finest Swiss guilloché work at prices many times higher. The Perception V2 refined the case and bracelet proportions; the Perception V3 (2025–2026) upgraded the movement from a Chinese Dandong Peacock automatic to a French-made Pequignet calibre — a partnership that began with the Ancestra and continued through the brand's lineup. The Millésime is the limited annual special edition within the Perception family: the Millésime 2024 featured a titanium case with a bold purple guilloché dial; the Millésime 2025 introduced a natural pietersite stone dial — a metamorphic stone whose swirling blue, black, grey, and copper patterns evoke classical Chinese mountain-water landscape paintings and shift dynamically as the wrist moves.

A 25-piece tantalum collaboration with Grail Watch — the Perception 'Hóng' (鸿) — sold out instantly at USD 10,000. The Ancestra (蛟, Jiāo) is a formal dress watch in 38mm, named for the Jiāo — the ocean-dwelling serpentine dragon of Chinese mythology whose home in the depths of the sea among the dragon kings provides the narrative frame for the dial design. The Jiāo's dial is produced by the Beijing-based workshop of Kong Lingjun: a 925-silver base that is first hand-hammered using the French martèle technique (creating an organic, tactile texture), then covered in multiple layers of Grand Feu enamel fired to produce a fumé gradient from pale silver at the centre to deep cobalt at the periphery — the texture evoking the depths of the sea, the gradient suggesting the abyss. Hour markers are diamond baguettes set to resemble white stick indices rather than jewellery; hands are a new three-plane leaf silhouette design, available with either Western Arabic or traditional Chinese numerals. The Ancestra is powered by a customised Pequignet movement.

The Inflection is Atelier Wen's permanent flagship and most technically extreme creation — priced at USD 29,800, it represents a leap from accessible independent to genuine haute horology that few young brands have ever attempted. Its defining innovation is the first-ever serialised production of a bracelet watch made entirely and throughout from 99.9% pure tantalum — case and every individual bracelet link. Tantalum has long held near-mythical status in watchmaking for its dense, cool blue-grey tone and enigmatic polished sheen, but it is notoriously difficult to machine and considered almost impossible to achieve fine surface finishing on, particularly polishing, especially on curved surfaces. The Inflection contains no flat surfaces anywhere: every face of the case and bracelet is either convex or concave, an interplay of positive and negative space that made the finishing challenge exponentially more difficult than it would be on any conventional watch. The process took three years of development. Prototypes first appeared at Watches & Wonders 2024; the collection launched in November 2025. The Grand Feu enamel dials are by Kong Lingjun's Beijing workshop, with Western Arabic numeral hour markers calligraphed by Lee Yuen-Rapati in a style inspired by Chinese bamboo leaf calligraphy; the chapter ring motif carries further Chinese visual reference. The movement is a customised Girard-Perregaux 03300 automatic calibre, with bridges and oscillating weight decorated by Alfred Chan and Sidoine Lescauville with motifs of wind from historical Chinese paintings. Atelier Wen has been featured in Hodinkee, Revolution Watch, Gear Patrol, Worn & Wound, and the New York Times. A 5-year warranty is offered on all models.
