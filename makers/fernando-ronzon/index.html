F. Ronzon Timepieces is the work of independent Canadian watchmaker Fernando Ronzon, who creates unique one-of-one, hand-made jumping hour watches — each piece entirely individual, with no two alike. His best-known work, the F. Ronzon Jump Hour, debuted at Dubai Watch Week alongside releases from Greubel Forsey, Armin Strom, Ressence, Charles Frodsham, H. Moser, and Genus — notable company for a one-person operation. The Jump Hour features a 40mm stainless steel case with a Hesalite front crystal and sapphire caseback, built around a vintage 1970s jumping-hour movement reworked with hand-finished bridges and dials, often in striking blued steel.

Ronzon sells directly to collectors via Instagram and Facebook — no boutiques, no distributors — offering worldwide shipping, PayPal and cryptocurrency payment, and a lifetime warranty on every piece. With around 11,000 Instagram followers and a small, devoted collector base, he represents the purest form of grassroots independent watchmaking: one person, one bench, one watch at a time, each a genuine 1/1 creation.

The catalogue has quietly broadened beyond the signature piece. Alongside the hand-wound Jump Hour, Ronzon builds a '3D Jumping Hour' on an automatic base — still 40 mm in brushed-and-polished steel, with an exhibition caseback, date indication, and layered dial architecture that gives the digital hour display literal depth — each one, like everything from his bench, serialised simply '1/1.' His pieces have begun circulating beyond the direct-sale channel too, surfacing with specialist dealers and on collector marketplaces, an informal secondary market that speaks to demand outstripping a production rate of eight to ten watches a year. The working method is disarmingly transparent: Ronzon documents projects in progress straight from the workbench on Instagram and Threads — a work-in-progress post, then 'fresh off the workbench,' then off to its new owner — so his followers effectively watch each commission come into being. It is watchmaking at its most unmediated: no website, no press releases, no retail markup, just a direct message, a build, and a lifetime warranty from the maker's own hand. In a Canadian independent scene still counting its artisans on two hands, Ronzon's DM-to-delivery model may be the closest thing modern collecting offers to commissioning a watch the way it was done two centuries ago — one patron, one maker, one watch.
