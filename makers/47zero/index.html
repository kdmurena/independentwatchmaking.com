47ZERO is a small French brand founded in January 2023 by the Irish watchmaker and designer Cormac Hanley and his wife, Gráinne, in Chavanod, near Annecy in the French Alps. Its watches pair Swiss automatic movements with hand-finished, story-led dials, and its slogan, LIFE>TIME, contrasts counting down the years with living one's dreams. Prices run from about €1,040 to €2,000 before tax.

Hanley grew up in Ireland and worked in advertising and photography before moving to France in 2007 with his wife and their three children. In 2017, at the age of 47, he suffered a major stroke, and during rehabilitation he found watchmaking as a way to keep creating. He retrained as a watchmaker in Annecy, about thirty minutes from Geneva, and in January 2023 he and Gráinne founded 47ZERO, starting in a business incubator in Greater Annecy with support from local professional networks and the Haute-Savoie Chamber of Commerce and Industry. The brand describes its core values as quality, positivity and uniqueness, and says each watch carries a positive message.

Every 47ZERO watch is designed and assembled in Annecy, with the dial as the centre of the work. Each dial is hand-finished, so no two are identical, using manual colouring, CNC engraving, laser marking, manual polishing and hand finishing on materials that include brass, aluminium and steel, and is fixed with hand-polished brass screws, which the brand treats as a signature of the watchmaker-designer. Cases are 316L stainless steel, 11 mm thin and slightly curved, with a polished bezel and flanks and brushed lug faces. Most watches come in 36 mm and 39 mm, with 42 mm available on special order, and they have sapphire crystals, 20 mm lugs and a transparent case back. The movements are Swiss automatics sourced near Geneva: the Sellita SW200-1 in most models and the STP 1-11 in the Dinner Date-at-Eight and Carré Courbé, both with 26 jewels, a 28,800 vibrations per hour beat, stop-seconds and a 41-hour power reserve. The leather straps are made with a French family firm, and watches carry a 24-month warranty excluding straps and bracelets.

The collection is built around names and stories. Odd-Hours treats the dial as a puzzle: at first a random pattern, which becomes orderly from a fresh angle. It comes in 36 mm and 39 mm cases at €1,041.67, in a 38 mm Double-Dome version with a high-dome sapphire at €1,450, and in the OH•MY (Mysterious-Yonder) tonneau case, 32.2 mm wide and 45.5 mm tall, at €1,650. Dinner Date-at-Eight puts the date at eight o'clock, a reminder to value the end of the day, and the Carré Courbé, in 36 mm, builds its curves entirely from straight lines. Le Boulevardier takes its name from the cocktail devised in 1927 by Harry McElhone at Harry's New York Bar in Paris, with bicolour, silver, Double-Dome and skeleton versions, the last at €1,350. La Date Dorée, at €1,250, finishes its date indicator in 24-carat gold leaf, and Sentient is presented as a watch meant to hold meaning, memory and emotion. La Contingence, at €2,000, carries an individually numbered one-gram ingot of 24-carat Swiss gold in its dial.

There are also editions. Blanc de Blancs, a tonneau watch with a layered dial of grained metals and a bubble motif, was made from only seven cases, each with a numbered case back and the name engraved on the rotor; the brand says all have found homes. The Maisy's Boulevardier edition, at €1,125, is a collaboration with the Irish whiskey brand Maisy McLoughlin's, with each watch numbered on the back, and a Roulette edition of La Date Dorée has also been released. The brand lists no production figures, and prices are quoted in euros excluding tax, with taxes included for orders in France and the EU. This listing draws on the brand's own website and has not been checked against independent reviews; the brand describes itself as having had three press features, including one in Irish Tatler.
