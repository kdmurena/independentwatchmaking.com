Mickaël Gaspar is a 29-year-old French independent watchmaker who lives and works in Les Brenets, in the Swiss Jura near Le Locle. His great-grandfather was a watchmaker who spent forty years at Lip, and Gaspar says he wanted to be a watchmaker from childhood and never had a plan B. He founded his own atelier in 2026 and unveiled his first wristwatch, the Souscription, that November.

He trained at the Lycée Edgar Faure in Morteau, the watchmaking school behind several well-known independents, and won a silver medal in the Meilleurs Apprentis de France competition. He was one of three apprentices chosen from fifty to join Breitling, then spent four years at Sellita, before moving to MH Horlogerie in Les Brenets, where he became a partner and head of the workshop. There he developed and repaired grand complications for other brands, including flying tourbillons, fusée-and-chain movements, minute repeaters and natural escapements.

Gaspar is the first partner of Ateliers Associés, a Swiss incubator for independent watchmakers founded by William Massena, creator of the Massena LAB studio and a former head of TimeZone and Antiquorum, and Louis Westphalen-Lemaitre, a former Hodinkee contributor who worked at Breitling and oversaw the relaunches of Daniel Roth and Gérald Genta at LVMH. The incubator handles imagery, press, sourcing, production, after-sales, pricing and collector relations so that the watchmaker can concentrate on the bench. It plans to back up to four new watchmakers a year in exchange for minority stakes, with the watchmaker keeping control of the company, its name, designs and calibres. It is funded by its founders and a group of private investors and collectors, and a dedicated vehicle for prototypes and first series is being set up for 2027. Gaspar and Ateliers Associés review allocation requests together.

The Souscription began as Gaspar's school watch, made between the ages of 17 and 19: a retrograde date with a visible movement. About ten years later he rebuilt the concept and finished it under his own name, at the urging of his classmates. The name recalls the way Breguet financed his watches by subscription two centuries ago. The movement is open on the dial side under a domed sapphire crystal, so the balance, pallet lever and retrograde date mechanism can be seen from the front, with the bridges and gear train visible through the caseback. The date hand advances one step a day and jumps back to the first at the end of the month. The brand site gives 12 November 2026 as the public reveal, at IAMWATCH Singapore.

The hand-wound calibre has 26 jewels, beats at 18,000 vibrations per hour and has a 46-hour power reserve. It starts from an undisclosed, modified Swiss base plate and gear train (SJX names it Cal. 234), but Gaspar reworked most parts: staffs and pinions are turned on a traditional lathe, some wheels are cut in Les Brenets, the balance is made and poised in his atelier, and the Breguet overcoil is formed and pinned by hand. The retrograde date is his own design from his student days. Functions are hours, minutes, small seconds and a retrograde date with instant return, set through the crown. Bridges are hand-sandblasted and bevelled with mirror-polished edges, nine components are black-polished, and each watch takes about 250 hours of machining and finishing.

The 316L stainless steel case is 39.9 mm across and 13 mm thick, brushed with a polished bevel on the case band, with a domed box sapphire crystal on the front, a flat sapphire back and 30 m of water resistance (3 bar, tested on every watch). The dial is hand-sandblasted and rose-gold plated, with Super-LumiNova on the numerals, chapter ring and hands, and the hands are black-polished and curved by hand. The watch comes on a hand-stitched calf leather strap with a steel pin buckle, in a wooden box with a marquetry scene of the Doubs Valley. The Souscription is limited to 20 pieces, numbered on the dial, offered by subscription at CHF 69,000 excluding taxes, with no delivery date stated in the sources.
