Rönkkö Watches is the work of Antti Rönkkö, an independent watchmaker based in Espoo, Finland, who operates through the company Rönkkö Watches Oy. The brand's website says it has operated since 2012. Rönkkö began making watches in order to design and build his own timepieces, because he felt modern mechanics did not meet his standards of sophistication and perfection. The brand's tagline is 'Handmade in Finnish Silence'. It presents the idea of Finnish silence, a pause for thought, as the state from which design, art and innovation arise, and cites the Finnish proverb 'Hiljaa hyvä tulee', which means that greatness comes from silence.

His work draws on Finnish mythology, particularly the Kalevala, on Finnish design and on older horological traditions. The central story is the Sampo, a magical artifact forged by the smith Ilmarinen and later lost at sea, which Rönkkö says he aims to recreate in the form of watches meant to last a lifetime and pass through generations. He follows the methods of older watchmaking masters, combines traditional techniques with his own ideas, and aims for shapes and angles that, in his words, modern machinery cannot achieve. The brand says that production and finishing are slow and rely on skilled hands, that the watches are numbered limited editions handcrafted by him, and that he plans to pass his knowledge on to a future apprentice.

The collection has four timepieces, all in 41.9 mm by 10.15 mm cases in hand-finished 316L stainless steel with satin surfaces and polished edges. The case design is based on the talon of Kokko, the fire eagle of the Kalevala, and is redesigned from the dragon-foot case of his earlier Steel Labyrinth. Each has a sapphire crystal and exhibition caseback with anti-reflective coating, a 316L steel crown with the Rönkkö logo, water resistance of 3 bar (30 m), diamond-cut stiletto hands and diamond-cut indexes. The movement is a Swiss-made Soprod A10, 25.6 mm (11.5 lignes), beating at 4 Hz (28,800 vph) with a 42-hour power reserve and 25 jewels with Incabloc shock protection. It winds automatically in both directions through a hand-finished rotor shaped like Ukonvasara, the hammer of the sky god Ukko. The watches have a hand-finished rotating dial with the labyrinth pattern of the Jatulis' Garden.

Jatulis' Garden, the simplest, shows only hours and minutes. Its dial pattern is based on the stone mazes, called jatulintarhat, that the myth says the solitude-loving giants built to keep people away, and a steel ball placed in the labyrinth is visible through the caseback. Jatulis' Moon, 'The Lunar Labyrinth', Väinämöinen and Son of Tuoni add an in-house moon-phase complication, on a modified Soprod A10, with a 29.5-day cycle. It comes as a single full moon (a bright stainless steel moon face), a single new moon (a black stainless steel moon face) or a dual full and new moon. Väinämöinen, 'The Wise and Ancient', is a tribute to the shaman-hero of the Kalevala. Son of Tuoni, the 'Underworld Watchman', is inspired by Tuonen Poika, the guardian of the river of Tuonela, and has a hardened, black IP-coated steel case, crown and buckle. The Moon and Väinämöinen models have a 316L steel buckle and a handmade matte black alligator leather strap.

According to the brand's website, each model is limited to 20 pieces per variation. The prices listed on the brand's 'Luxury Timepieces' page, excluding VAT, are EUR 12,700 for Väinämöinen, EUR 14,900 for Jatulis' Moon, EUR 6,950 for Jatulis' Garden and EUR 12,700 for Son of Tuoni. The individual model pages give no prices and direct buyers to the 'Contact & Order Info' page. The website also lists Ukonvasara under 'Mechanics', and describes it as a Soprod A10 with a hammer-shaped rotor, without giving a price, a case or a dial.
