Born in Suresnes on the outskirts of Paris in 1963, Vianney Halter's father was a French National Railways train driver who brought home old machines and mechanical parts — early exposure to locomotives, steam engines, and control instruments that shaped a lifelong obsession. At 14 he took the train to Paris alone and enrolled at the École Horlogère de Paris; after graduating in 1980 he spent ten years restoring antique horological items. He became an independent watchmaker in 1984.

In 1994 he founded Manufacture Janvier, developing complications for Audemars Piguet, Breguet, Jaquet Droz, and Harry Winston, and working alongside François-Paul Journe and Denis Flageollet. He also contributed the base movement for the Cabestan Winch Tourbillon. In 1998 he presented the Antiqua Perpetual Calendar at Basel — immediately described as 'a relic from the future', with four porthole displays inspired by Jules Verne and science fiction — which won him AHCI membership. In 2003 he created the Harry Winston Opus 3 (ten overlapping digital discs, jump hours, twin gear trains) with Max Büsser.

GPHG Best Watchmaker 2011. Complete collection — CURRENT: La Resonance (two oscillators coupled by acoustic resonance, inspired by gravitational waves from colliding black holes); Deep Space Resonance (2021, triple-axis tourbillon with twin balance wheels in acoustic resonance, 371-part tourbillon assembly, 42 polished pillars, max 1 piece every 3 years, CHF 1.1M); Deep Space Tourbillon (2013, triple-axis under sapphire dome, 40s/6min/30min rotation, GPHG Design & Innovation 2013 and Gaïa Prize 2016); Reality of Time (new); Art Deco Metropolis (2024, tribute to the roaring twenties); Liberty (12 pieces, star fortification dial, sapphire hands); Classic Janvier (time-only in traditional architecture); Grand Voyageur. ARCHIVES: Retrospective; Antiqua; Trio. Behrens KWH (18 pieces, 870+ components) and KWH tantalum/ceramic (2026) are his most recent collaborations. 'My goal when doing watches is not to tell time or shaping a trend. I create watches because I have great fun doing so.' Fewer than 500 watches ever made.
