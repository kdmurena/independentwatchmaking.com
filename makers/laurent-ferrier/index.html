GPHG Winner

Laurent Ferrier

Switzerland
Founded2009
LocationGeneva, Switzerland
PriceCHF 30,000–200,000
Volume~300/yr
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Born in Geneva in 1946 into a watchmaking family — son and grandson of master watchmakers. He graduated from the École d'Horlogerie de Genève in 1968 as best in his class, his school piece pocket watch already embodying the design language that defines the modern brand. Patek Philippe's talent scouts noticed; he joined Patek's prototyping department where he participated in the creation of Gérald Genta's iconic Nautilus. He spent 37 years at Patek Philippe, ascending to head the product development department, before founding his own brand in 2009 at around 60 years old — nearing retirement, choosing ambition instead. His son Christian, an expert in mechanical watch design, joined him, along with longtime friend François Servanin (who had finished 3rd at Le Mans with Laurent in 1979 — the day they decided to one day create a watch together).

Philosophy: 'Simplicity, precision, and pure, uncluttered beauty.' Behind the modest appearance lies a world of complex mechanics. Signature elements: the 'Assegai' hands (named after the Zulu javelin, designed for their linear attenuation like a Constantin Brâncuși sculpture); in-house micro-rotor movements; double direct-impulse Breguet-inspired natural escapement with two escape wheels; Observatoire de Besançon chronometer certification. Three GPHG wins: 2010 Men's Watch Prize (Galet Classic Tourbillon Double Spiral — the youngest-ever maison to win on debut); 2015 Horological Revelation Prize (Galet Square); 2018 Men's Complication Prize (Galet Annual Calendar School Piece — named after his 1968 graduation pocket watch). Collections: Classic (Tourbillon, Annual Calendar, Micro-Rotor, School Piece); Square (contemporary cushion case, 2015); Grand Sport; Sport. École (School Piece) line pays homage to the 1968 Montre d'École. Also collaborated with URWERK (2017). 85% in-house; ~300 pieces annually.

The brand's newest chapter has been the Sport line, born from Ferrier and Servanin's own memory of driving at dawn on the 24-hour track: the Grand Sport Tourbillon translates racing-car curves into a titanium case with an integrated bracelet, its double-balance-spring tourbillon visible through the caseback like the workings of an engine, and has since expanded into the Sport Auto and, unveiled at Watches and Wonders 2026, the Sport Traveller — the collection's first dual-time complication, built on a newly developed automatic movement inside a 42mm grade-5 titanium case with a sculpted tonneau-shaped mid-case evolved from the Square line. The 15th anniversary in 2025 prompted a season of reflection: the seventh Série Atelier revisited the original Classic Tourbillon in teal grand feu enamel over a white-gold base with rhodium-plated components (a first for the house), the Classic Auto joined the permanent collection in a new horizon-blue dial, and the Classic Traveller Globe Night Blue returned with a fresh enamel rendering of Ferrier's 2016 depiction of the Earth at night. The house has also opened its austere design language to outside collaborators — a Classic Micro-Rotor reimagined with French artist Hervé Di Rosa, and a 12-piece Grand Sport Tourbillon Sincere Platinum Jubilee Edition marking a longtime retail partner's 70th anniversary — proof that even a maison built on restraint can flex playfully without losing its identity. Now in its second decade, Laurent Ferrier has grown from one ex-Patek executive's retirement project into a settled, multi-generational manufacture, still defined by the same quiet, uncluttered elegance he set down in his 1968 school piece.
