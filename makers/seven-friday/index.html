Seven Friday (written SEVENFRIDAY by the brand) is a Zurich-based lifestyle watch brand known for large, industrial-looking automatic watches with multilayer dials, built around the idea of living every day as if it were Friday. Its motto is "Show Me Don’t Tell Me": emotion over reason, action over words and creativity over unquestioned standards. It was founded in 2012 by Dan Niederer, who remains founder and CEO, and sells watches, straps, caps, bags, eyewear and apparel through its own website and about twenty brand stores it calls Spaces. Its watches are listed at roughly USD 898 to 1,905.

According to a 2021 interview with Time and Watches, Niederer is Swiss-born, holds a master's degree in law, began in the IT industry and then restructured offices for a Swiss trading house that represented watch brands in Asia and the Pacific. He lived for about ten years in Sydney, Singapore, Tokyo and Bangkok before returning to Switzerland in 2010, where he first invested in a watch design studio and then launched Seven Friday in 2012. The interview names Arnaud Duval as a co-founder who leads a design team described as entirely in-house, and gives the brand's stated values as sincerity, positivity, inclusivity and respect. The company is SEVENFRIDAY AG, Staffelstrasse 10, 8045 Zurich. The watches are designed in Zurich, and SJX reported in 2015 and 2016 that they are made in China, with most external components made in Hong Kong and China, as the case back states. The brand works with street artists, musicians, DJs and food and drink specialists, and its Spaces, in cities including Jakarta, Shanghai, Kuwait City, Kuala Lumpur and Singapore, are meant as places where like-minded people can experience its culture.

The watches are sold in lettered series. The P-Series, which launched the brand, is playful: the P1C/06 "Bubble Gum", at USD 1,815, has a white ceramic bezel, animation ring and crown, a four-layer mauve, papaya and pink dial and a Miyota automatic, with 10 ATM water resistance. The M-Series draws on the brand's earliest designs: the M2/05, at USD 1,905 the most expensive model in the range supplied for this listing, has a 47 mm stainless steel case, a Miyota 8215 automatic with a 40-hour power reserve, a seven-layer dial, a bronze PVD animation ring with a bouchonné finish, a sapphire crystal with a blue anti-reflective coating and a green oiled calfskin strap, with 3 ATM water resistance. The PS-Series is more nautical: the PS1/04, at USD 1,895, combines a stainless steel case with a sandblasted gunmetal PVD finish, a double antique-brass PVD bezel, a skeletonised five-layer dial with 21 applied markers and a Miyota 82S5 automatic, with 3 ATM water resistance.

The V-Series arrived in 2015 and is named for the V that the case's twin ears and crown form on the side. SJX's review of the V1/01 and V2/01 describes a 49.7 by 44.3 mm case with no protruding lugs, a Miyota 8S27 automatic with a 40-hour power reserve, and an unusual display of hours on a central disc that must be added to numerals on a fan-shaped scale from 11 to 3 o'clock, with a day and night window and an aperture at seven o'clock showing part of the balance. It cost SFr 1,055 before taxes, and the reviewer, who found the time-telling confusing at first, thought the dial made it look more expensive than its price and singled out the Fast Strap Changer as its most impressive feature. The Q-Series followed in September 2016 in the same asymmetrical NFC case, with a regulator-style layout: hours on an off-centre 24-hour subdial, minutes on a centre hand, running seconds at five o'clock and, for the first time for the brand, a date at three, driven by a Miyota 8219 automatic. The Q1/01 in silver, the Q2/01 in copper and the Q3/01 in black were priced at US$1,160, US$1,190 and US$1,220 at launch.

The W-Series, represented by the W1/01 "The Blade" at USD 1,250, takes its "Squround" case from a circular saw: a polished stainless steel case 44.3 by 49.7 mm, a Miyota 82S7 automatic with a 40-hour power reserve, hardened K1 mineral glass, 3 ATM water resistance and a multi-level dial in which a central 12-hour disc recalls the saw blade, a 60-second disc sits at five o'clock and a night and day indicator at nine. The R-Series, introduced as a new shape with a retro soul, has a 40 mm case that the brand's Indian store calls its smallest. The ME-Series, called M-EVO by the brand, is more futuristic: the ME3/01 "Stone Blue", at USD 1,795, is a 44.5 mm stainless steel watch with blue PVD, a five-layer dial and three sapphire crystals, powered by a self-winding skeleton TMI NH70 with a 41-hour power reserve and 5 ATM water resistance. The PG1/01 M "GMT" at USD 1,805 is described as the brand's first GMT watch, with a Miyota 9075 GMT and a Milanese bracelet. The T-Series, represented by the T2/08 "Riley II" at USD 1,300, and the C-Series, represented by the C2/01 "PDP" at USD 1,560, were not documented in detail, and no information on the S-Series could be read for this listing.

Most Seven Friday watches have a Fast Strap Changer, a quick-release pin that lets the strap be swapped without tools, and an NFC chip in the case back that works with the brand's app to authenticate and register the watch and to extend its warranty by a year; the Time and Watches interview says each product has had a "digital twin" since 2016 and reports about 120,000 registrations over two to three years. The movements are Japanese Miyota and Taiwanese TMI calibres rather than in-house ones, and design and branding are central to the appeal, so the brand sits at the accessible, design-led end of this catalogue. Production volumes and ownership details beyond the founder-CEO are not published in the sources used. Where the brand's pages give a case size as 47 mm in a header and different measurements in the specification, this listing uses the specification. Prices are in US dollars as shown on the brand's site, with import duties included; the lowest price across the series, about USD 898, comes from the brand. This listing draws on the brand's website, SJX (2015 and 2016) and a 2021 Time and Watches interview with Dan Niederer.
