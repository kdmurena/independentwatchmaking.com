Marloe Watch Company

Marloe Watch Company began in 2015 when its co-founder Oliver, inquisitive by nature, opened up his own fairly expensive branded watch to replace a dead battery — and found, staring back at him, a hollow case wrapped around an underwhelming plastic quartz movement. The watch felt heartless, and Oliver became convinced he could make something better. He found a partner in Gordon, an industrial designer who shared his conviction that things should be done properly, and together they set out to build a well-designed, beautifully engineered mechanical watch for the price of a high-street fashion piece. Within twelve months the first mechanical Marloe watch had sold, and Marloe Watch Company was born — funded initially through crowdfunding, a community-first beginning that still shapes the company's culture today; Marloe describes itself as the only genuinely customer-owned watch brand, with employees, shareholders, and customers together forming what it calls the wider 'MWC community.' The brand's name comes from Marlow, Oliver's hometown on the banks of the River Thames, just around the corner from the company's headquarters in Sonning Common, Oxfordshire — where, after a decade navigating the watch industry, Marloe brought assembly fully in-house, allowing it to call itself a genuine British watchmaker in the fullest sense: every current-generation watch is hand-assembled by Marloe's own skilled team at its Oxfordshire HQ.

Marloe's guiding philosophy is summed up in the phrase 'Time Well Spent': a conviction that in an age of instant, disposable things, a mechanical watch stands as something enduring, worth waiting for, and worth doing properly rather than quickly. That philosophy translates into a distinctive design process: every Marloe watch begins with a story of genuine human endeavour — adventurers, engineers, pioneers, and visionaries whose achievements the brand finds instructive and worth honouring — and the design team immerses itself in that story for months, unearthing the evocative period details that inform every decision on the finished watch, from case shape to dial texture to colour palette. Past design inspirations documented on Marloe's own site include Donald Campbell's Bluebird land-speed and water-speed record attempts, Scott's Antarctic expeditions, Alcock and Brown's first transatlantic flight, the R34 airship, submarine history, and BOAC's early commercial aviation. Each watch is produced in batches of up to 1,000 pieces, with every individual example carrying its own unique serial number and passing hand quality-control inspection before it ships; the brand has been recognised by GQ, Esquire, and Metro for combining genuine craftsmanship with accessible pricing.

The current British Built collection, hand-assembled at the Oxfordshire HQ, spans several distinct model families. The SkySplitter (Jetstream and Airway references, £599) is Marloe's aviation-inspired line. The Morar 310 is the broadest family, available across numerous dial variants — Port, Ord, Starboard, Bumblebee, Destroyer, Gold King MkII, a USA Edition, and a Standard Dress reference — all starting from £499. The Sceptre S2 (AC75, Reactor, Regatta, Sonar, and Vault references, £399) draws on marine and racing themes, its name and AC75 reference nodding to modern America's Cup yacht design. The Haggis Works Pinky, currently in pre-order for its second production batch at £449, reflects the brand's willingness to explore more overtly characterful, story-driven designs. The Aerodyne (Miami and Heart references, from £349) rounds out the current line. Earlier Marloe watches, including the hand-wound, ETA-movement Haskell that first built the brand's reputation among enthusiasts and reviewers, remain part of the brand's design lineage and are referenced in its archive. All watches ship with free worldwide shipping and a 30-day return policy, and the brand offers dedicated repair and servicing support through its Oxfordshire workshop for the lifetime of every watch it has made.
