Stefan Kudoke

Germany
Founded2005
LocationGlashütte, Germany
Price$4,800–$18,800
Volume~30/yr
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Stefan Kudoke discovered watchmaking as a boy, passing a watch repair shop across from his childhood home in Frankfurt (Oder), east of Berlin, every day on his way to school; as a teenager he also ran a small airbrush and graphic-design venture with friends, an early taste of working for himself. He began his apprenticeship at 17, trained in Glashütte's watchmaking school, and went on to work at Glashütte Original's studio for complications and prototypes, followed by service-department postings for Breguet, Blancpain, and Omega in New York. He earned his Meisterbrief, or Master Craftsman certificate, at just 22, and in 2005 — bolstered by parallel studies in economics and finance — he and his wife, Ev Kudoke, founded their own watchmaking house together.

His first principle is individuality — each KUDOKE is the product of a genuine dialogue between watchmaker and client — and his earliest watches, gathered under the Kunstwerk (art-work) banner, grew out of skills he taught himself: hand skeletonisation and engraving applied to modified Unitas 6498 base movements. The line includes the sculptural KudOktopus and Free KudOktopus, their bridges hand-carved and engraved into an octopus wrapped around the balance; Real Skeleton, with bone-shaped hands and a diamond-eyed skull inspired by historical Memento Mori clocks; the rhodium-plated HS-1 and rose-gold-plated HR-1 skeleton variants; Black Beauty, Skull, White Flower, Classic, and Mysticum, the last hiding its hour indication beneath a triangular pointer under an ornately engraved dial. Prices across the Kunstwerk range run from around 4,800 USD to roughly 15,000 USD.

In 2018, Kudoke moved beyond modified base movements with his first true in-house calibre, Kaliber 1, developed together with Austrian independent watchmaker Habring² — the two movements share some components — and inspired by the design of an old English pocket watch movement Kudoke had owned for years. Hand-wound, 30mm across and just 4.3mm thick, it runs 18 jewels at 28,800vph with 46 hours of reserve, its balance cock hand-engraved with Kudoke's infinity motif. The debut Kudoke 1 kept things simple with central hours, minutes, and a small seconds at nine o'clock; the Kudoke 2 added a 24-hour indication and won the GPHG's Petite Aiguille prize in 2019, the first time a German independent watchmaker had won a Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève award. The Kudoke 3 went further still, tracking the hour across a three-tiered plate with a single hand that shifts between levels as it moves — a design whose salmon-dial edition won Kudoke a second Petite Aiguille in 2024, making him the only watchmaker to date to win the category twice.

Kudoke ∞ (Infinity) sets a hand-engraved Kaliber 1 beneath an aventurine dial whose flecked surface mimics a night sky, its three-quarter bridge hand-guilloché into a radiating star pattern; limited to twelve pieces a year, it is priced from roughly 15,000 to 18,800 USD depending on case and dial. The newest addition, the 2025 Kudoke 5, rebuilds the format around a dedicated movement, Kaliber 1-24H_L, and a rotating 24-hour celestial disc that becomes the watch's entire focus rather than a supporting feature, in a 38mm steel case; priced at €17,900 excluding VAT, first deliveries began in autumn 2025.

Kudoke became a full AHCI member in 2021. His small team, based in the Saxon village of Weifa a short drive from Glashütte, still hand-finishes every bridge, plate, hand, and balance cock before assembly, work that extends to outside collaborations such as his engraving and finishing on Louis Erard's regulator collection. Across the full range — from the more accessible Kunstwerk skeleton pieces to the rare gold and aventurine Infinity editions — Kudoke's watches span roughly 4,800 to just under 19,000 USD, remarkable value, reviewers have consistently noted, for a level of handwork more often associated with houses charging several times as much.
