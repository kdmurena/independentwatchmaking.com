Charles Girardier

Switzerland
Founded2018 (revival of an 18th-century house)
LocationBuchs, Switzerland
PriceCHF ~17,800–53,900+
VolumeUnder 500/yr
Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Charles Girardier is the modern revival of an 18th-century Geneva watchmaking name, brought back to life in 2018 by Patrick Alexandre Ulm after he began collecting the original master's vintage pieces at auction. The historical Charles Girardier, nicknamed 'Girardier L'aîné' (1759–1839), was renowned for Geneva enameling — hand-painted miniatures on dials — and for his art-mechanical automata, especially jaquemarts, the small animated figures once reserved for steeple clocks and grand campaniles; he was commissioned by European royalty and political leaders of his day before his name fell silent for nearly two centuries. Ulm's revival deliberately keeps that DNA alive rather than simply borrowing the name: the house caps its own production at under 500 watches a year, designs and develops its movements, cases, and dials in-house at its atelier in Buchs, and certifies every watch against 38 internal quality criteria under what it calls the Charles Girardier hallmark — including a running tolerance of –1 to +2 seconds a day, tighter than typical Swiss chronometer standards. Even the hallmark's own 'CG' monogram is drawn from the initials engraved on the house's first in-house caliber, echoing the fluid signature of the original 18th-century master.

The house's signature complication, and the clearest link to Girardier's automaton heritage, is the Signature Mystérieuse®: an interlaced 'C&G' monogram at 12 o'clock whose two elements rotate in opposite directions purely from the kinetic energy of the wearer's wrist, powered by a dedicated hidden oscillating weight, before settling back into place when the watch is read. Built into the self-winding CG SM-1809 caliber (32 jewels, 46-hour reserve, 308 components, hand-polished bridges) alongside a 60-second flying tourbillon and Grand Feu champlevé enamel dials, the Signature Mystérieuse® line reached its high point in 2020, when the ladies' 'Fleur de Sel' reference (CG-1809 37/1WG Ice Blue) won the Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève in the Ladies' Complication category — the moment that put the revived house on the map. Alongside it sits the Synchronaut® collection (CG SYNC-1863), a GMT line named for Henry Royce — born in 1863 — and developed in partnership with the Rolls-Royce Enthusiasts' Club for the marque's 120th anniversary, its guilloché dials hand-engraved on a 60-year-old machine and its date sub-dial modeled on an antique Girardier L'aîné pocket watch.

The newest and most technically significant reference is Plénitude®, launched in 2026 around the house's first fully self-developed manufacture caliber, the CG PL1/PL2 (188 components, 29 jewels, hand-bevelled bridges, Cotes de Geneve). Rather than chase complexity, Plénitude® channels the house's philosophy of restraint into two features: the Volcano® Power Reserve, a three-dimensional mechanical indicator directly inspired by the antique power-reserve displays found on historic Girardier pocket watches, and, on select references, a moon phase — each limited to 25 pieces at CHF 17,800–19,800. Beyond its numbered collections, Charles Girardier maintains an active Antique Collection of restored original 18th- and 19th-century Girardier pocket watches and offers a full sur-mesure bespoke program, letting clients commission personalized dials, complications, and finishes — a direct continuation of the original L'aîné's own practice of crafting singular pieces for the eminent clients of his time.
