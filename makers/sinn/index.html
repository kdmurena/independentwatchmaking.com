Founded in Frankfurt in 1961 by former pilot and blind-flying instructor Helmut Sinn, Sinn Spezialuhren has been owned and led by engineer Lothar Schmidt since 1994. Under Schmidt's leadership the brand developed its proprietary technologies, the Frankfurt Financial District Watches, and co-initiated the DIN 8330 standard for pilot watches. Six watch ranges: Instrument Chronographs, Instrument Watches, Frankfurt Financial District Watches (launched 1999), Diving Watches, Pilot Watches, and Classic Timepieces. Key proprietary technologies: Tegiment hardened steel (exceptionally scratch-resistant); Ar-dehumidification (argon-filled cases); anti-magnetic copper sulphate solution; HYDRO Technology (oil-filled cases, 5,000m depth rated); and patented Goldbronze 125 (CuSn7Au12.5 — improved skin compatibility and seawater resistance).

Notable firsts: first watch brand to use German Submarine Steel for cases (2005); co-developed the TESTAF pilot watch standard (2012); SZ01 in-house chronograph with centre-mounted jump 60-minute hand (2011). An 857 UTC TESTAF accompanied stratosphere record-breaker Robert Alan Eustace in 2014; astronaut Reinhard Furrer wore the 140 S on the Spacelab D1 mission. Supplied to GSG 9, KSK special forces, and KSM commando frogmen. Multiple Red Dot, iF, and German Design Award winners. Exceptional value for genuine technical innovation.

Schmidt's route to Sinn ran through an unlikely detour: as an IWC engineer he was recruited by Sinn intern Bruno Belamich to help launch a new French-German venture that became Bell & Ross, whose earliest watches were built so closely from Sinn's own designs that they carried a 'by Sinn' mark on the dial. That collaboration brought Schmidt to Helmut Sinn's attention directly, and in 1994 the 78-year-old founder sold him the company outright, though a falling-out meant Sinn himself soon departed the firm bearing his own name. Schmidt has spent three decades since proving the acquisition was no accident: in 2025, marking twenty years of the U-Series diving watches, Sinn released the U15, U16, and U18 special editions, their cases forged directly from steel salvaged from the decommissioned German Navy submarines U 15, U 16, and U 18 — a literal, traceable link between military hardware and the watch on the wrist. That same year Schmidt began formally sharing leadership, appointing long-serving department heads Simone Richter and Andreas Hölzel to the executive team, part of a broader succession plan that includes his 2024 announcement that his shares in the company will eventually pass to a foundation rather than a private buyer. 2026 marked Sinn's first-ever appearance at Watches and Wonders Geneva, where the brand unveiled the 544 and 544 RS — a compact, bead-blasted revival of the 1994 titanium 244, Schmidt's own first model under the company — alongside the limited 936 S chronograph and the unconventional 308 Hunting Watch, proof that even after sixty-five years, Germany's most understated tool-watch manufacture still has new instruments to build.
