Bangalore Watch Company (BWC) is an Indian watch brand founded in 2018 by the husband-and-wife team of Nirupesh Joshi and Mercy Amalraj, who left careers in technology and consulting and describe themselves as outsiders to the watch industry. Based in Bangalore, which the brand calls India's watchmaking ground zero, it works with a small team and says its watches are designed, engineered and assembled in the city. Its stated aim is to give people a story to wear, and each collection tells its own, offering a 21st-century view of India built around space exploration, aviation and advanced technology instead of the usual clichés. The movements are Swiss, mostly the Sellita SW200 automatic and, for the Epoch, a Sellita hand-wound calibre, and the watches use sapphire crystals, 316L steel and Super-LumiNova.

The first collection, the Renaissance Automatic of 2018, was followed by themed lines named for subjects the founders care about. Apogee, billed as the first Indian watch tested and qualified for outer space, though its product pages give no test details, is a space-themed line honouring ISRO, with dual-time models, two crowns, an Aryabhata satellite caseback and 100 m of water resistance. The Visitor, which commemorates the interstellar object ʻOumuamua, has a Sellita SW200-1 in a 40 mm Cerasteel case (a steel core with a ceramic outer coating) and a dial cut from the Muonionalusta meteorite, while the Manzinus honours the Chandrayaan-3 Moon landing with a meteorite disc at six o'clock. Mach 1 is a pilot-watch line inspired by Indian aviation and the MiG-21, in 40 mm cases of steel or matte black PVD with 100 m of water resistance; the Avalanche recalls the 1978 Chetak helicopter airlift to the Siachen Glacier, and the limited Admiral uses steel recovered from the INS Vikrant.

Peninsula is a 44 mm outdoor line with a pebble-shaped case, a domed sapphire crystal, 200 m of water resistance and dials drawn from India's coasts, mountains and rainforests, such as the Mannar with a mother-of-pearl dial inspired by Gulf of Mannar oysters. The Carbon, limited to 100 watches, uses a dial made of ReforgeCarbon, a composite of chopped carbon fibre and recovered carbon black, with an air-quality scale in place of the minute track. Cover Drive, a cricket-themed line with a 40 mm steel case, a sandwich dial, a 120-click bezel that tracks overs in 50-over or T20 matches and a caseback showing a batsman, is now retired, and the brand says a second generation is expected in early 2027. The Forty-Seven is a Ronda-powered quartz pocket watch in rose-gold PVD steel, a tribute to India's independence in 1947.

The newest line is the Epoch, a return to the dress watch eight years after the Renaissance, shown in November 2026 as the Executive, in steel with a champagne dial, and the Statesman, in rose-gold PVD with an anthracite dial. Both are 39 mm by 11.6 mm with 47 mm lug-to-lug, a Swiss Made Sellita SW261 hand-wound movement with Geneva stripes and blued screws, a sapphire exhibition caseback in a locket style that holds a personal message, and 50 m of water resistance. The movement is lettered as regulated in Bangalore. Monochrome, which reported the launch, gave the price as USD 3,100, and the brand site lists INR 297,600 with pre-orders shipping on 6 January 2027. Epoch has no story of its own, the brand says, which leaves room for the owner's.

All watches carry a 24-month global warranty, excluding straps and accessories, and ship free worldwide from Bangalore, with the buyer paying import duties. Some models are sold on an enquiry basis, and the brand says it only makes watches it would wear itself. Teddy Baldassarre's review reported a 1,800 sq ft flagship boutique opened in Bangalore in early 2026, and the brand's site offers boutique visits. Prices have risen over time: older reviews quote the Cover Drive at about USD 760, against INR 134,000 on the site today. The brand's own pages give few edition sizes, so the listing states only the Peninsula Carbon's 100 pieces.
