Golay Spierer is Geneva's purest tailor: a house that has never made a catalogue watch, only bespoke ones, each built from a first conversation rather than a sketch already on file. The Golay name carries genuine four-generations-back watchmaking memory — Jacques David Golay, born in the Vallée de Joux in 1782, established himself as a Geneva watchmaker in 1829; his son Auguste founded Golay-Leresche in 1837; by the late 19th century, as Golay Fils & Stahl, the family firm ranked alongside Patek Philippe and Vacheron Constantin among Geneva's flagship houses. That lineage was renewed on 1 January 2001, when a descendant of the Golay watchmaking line partnered with Emile Spierer — who shared the same values and the same dream — to found Golay Spierer SA, settling into workshops in Carouge that September. The first watch, chronograph N° 2001-1001, was delivered on 23 November 2001, marking the symbolic start of an atelier that has since produced more than 160 original, unique creations — no two ever alike.

Every commission follows the same three-act process. 'The path to creation' begins with a conversation about taste, history, and dreams, developed over weeks of computer-rendered sketches until a design is confirmed; 'the manufacture' turns that design into full plans and specifications distributed among roughly twenty specialist craftsmen — case-makers, engravers, dial-makers, movement finishers — whose dialogue the atelier orchestrates piece by piece, and during which the client chooses the watch's name; and final assembly happens in-house in Carouge, where, as Golay Spierer puts it, technical precision meets a genuinely emotional act of bringing the watch to life. The result is a portfolio of unrepeatable pieces built around personal stories, family history, and singular obsessions — among them La Libellule, Peter Pan, Gibeon, Esteban P, Hibben, London, Sympathie, La Quénographe, La Zodiac, La Seguin, La Salles, and commissions like the Famille Probst piece and the WTOFF and Classic Spirit models — alongside dedicated collector projects such as the Chronos Embarqués automotive chronographs, a 70th-anniversary trio commissioned for Facel Vega, and Instruments de Mesure du Temps, the atelier's Scuderia Ventidue-linked exploration of motorsport-inflected horology.

The atelier's most extraordinary works are its 'Opus' pieces, which fuse watchmaking with literal historical relics. Opus 1, marking the 250th anniversary of Napoleon Bonaparte's birth, houses an ultra-thin automatic micro-rotor movement in a hand-engraved rose gold case; its back is set with a genuine 20-franc Napoleon gold coin struck in 1807, the same year as an authentic letter signed by the Emperor supplied with the watch, from which a hand-cut bee — the imperial symbol — taken from beneath his own signature is inlaid into the piece (CHF 150,000). Opus 2, honoring the 100th anniversary of Albert Einstein's Nobel Prize, sets a meteorite plaque engraved with his name into a sandblasted grade 5 titanium case, its dial bearing a hand-carved relief symbolizing spacetime's gravitational curvature layered over a genuine document signed by Einstein himself (CHF 120,000). Even outside the Opus series the engineering runs deep: the Mysterious Lock achieves what the atelier calls a world first in case construction, its sapphire crystal held shut by four screwed horns alone, wrapped around a Vaucher VMF 5401 micro-rotor movement beneath a white gold guilloché dial. In an industry increasingly built on repeated references, Golay Spierer remains something rarer: a house where every watch is, quite literally, the only one of its kind.
