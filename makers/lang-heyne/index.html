Lang & Heyne

Germany
Founded2001

Founded in 2001 in Dresden by Marco Lang — a fifth-generation watchmaker whose father was head restorer at Dresden's Mathematisch-Physikalischen Salon (the Royal Saxon Watch Cabinet, one of the world's greatest historical timepiece collections) and later head of apprentices at A. Lange & Söhne — and Mirko Heyne, a fellow watchmaker friend. Heyne departed in 2002 to join Nomos Glashütte, but the name remained. Lang became an AHCI member in 2005. Based near Dresden in Radeberg, Lang & Heyne is fiercely Saxon and proud of it — not in Glashütte, but in the Saxon capital, seat of August the Strong, patron of the arts and watchmaking since the 18th century. Unlike Glashütte's three-quarter plates, Lang & Heyne uses open bridge architecture to maximise the visibility of hand finishing — no machine-finished surfaces anywhere.

Watch names are drawn from the Fürstenzug, the great 19th-century Dresden mural depicting 800 years of Saxon rulers — Georg, Anton, Friedrich II, Friedrich III, Friedrich August I, Johann — which Marco Lang personally visits on completing each new design. Nine in-house calibres developed. The flagship Anton features a flying tourbillon in a rectangular case, with cutouts showing the tourbillon from both front and back; the Inhorgenta Award 2024 'Luxury Watch of the Year' was awarded to the Anton Manufaktur Edition (5 pieces, black ceramic dial). Friedrich II features a remontoir constant-force mechanism. All movements: hand-bevelled and black-polished steel components, swan-neck spring regulators, screwed gold chatons, Glashütte ribbing, engraved balance cocks. Single-pusher chronographs and flying tourbillons available. 'Made in Saxony.'

The manufacture's ownership has changed hands more than its unbroken Saxon identity might suggest. In 2013 Lang sold a majority stake to Prof. Dr. Ulrich L. Rohde — son of a co-founder of the electronics group Rohde & Schwarz — and the two, with two further shareholders, folded the business into the Tempus Arte Group, with Lang contributing the Lang & Heyne manufacture while the group established Uhren-Werke-Dresden as a dedicated movement supplier. Lang headed the company until 2018 and departed for good in 2019 to found his own eponymous atelier, but the transition was unusually smooth: Jens Schneider, previously a Lang & Heyne consultant and formerly head of construction at the nascent Moritz Grossmann, stepped in as director of development and has carried the house's aesthetic and technical DNA forward without interruption. Tempus Arte today also owns STOWA, and since July 2025 the group has been led by CEO Dr.-Ing. Daniel T. Roy alongside Alexander Gutierrez-Diaz. Roughly 97% of every component is still produced in-house, annual output has grown modestly to around 150–200 pieces, and the brand's distinctive third centralised lug at the top and bottom of each case remains a signature detail. Collector demand has followed the watches into the auction rooms — a 2014 Friedrich III in steel with a black dial made CHF 21,420 at Phillips Geneva in 2020 — proof that Lang & Heyne's reputation for hand-finished, historically-rooted Saxon watchmaking has outlasted the departure of the founder whose name still leads it.
