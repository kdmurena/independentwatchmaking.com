Linde Werdelin

Linde Werdelin was born from a near-fatal accident: in 1996, keen mountaineer and off-piste skier Jørn Werdelin was caught off guard by sudden bad weather, got lost, skied off a cliff, and broke his back. In 2002 he partnered with his childhood friend Morten Linde — the two had grown up together north of Copenhagen and had, in Jørn's own recollection, been inseparable since boyhood — around an idea born directly from that accident: a high-end sports watch that could, when required, be combined with a detachable digital instrument providing real-time information for skiing or diving. 'The instrument was our reason d'être,' Jørn has said. 'Without the instrument, there would have been no Linde Werdelin watch company.' The work began with Morten and a single assistant designer in Morten's Copenhagen studio, soon growing into a six-person team working alongside the Danish Technical University's IPU to develop the instruments' software, hardware, and the essential locking mechanism connecting instrument to watch — a genuinely unusual bi-national development process, with instruments engineered in Denmark and the watch itself made in Switzerland. Five years of research and development later, the first Linde Werdelin collection — the Biformeter, accompanied by 200 Founders Watches offered as an invitation to the friends and partners who had supported the original concept — reached the market in 2006.

In 2007, the Biformeter and its Land Instrument helped guide climbers Conrad Anker and Leo Houlding to become the first to free-climb Mount Everest's North East Ridge; when the expedition team returned, they reported having carried only the instrument to the summit, leaving the watch itself at base camp to save weight — a moment that pushed Linde Werdelin toward building dramatically lighter watches. The Dive Instrument that followed in 2009 ran on some 300,000 lines of professional-grade code to protect divers against the dangers of excessive depth or overly rapid ascent. By 2012, with smartphone software rapidly overtaking dedicated hardware, Linde Werdelin made the difficult decision to cease producing the physical instruments altogether — though the case architecture the instruments demanded, including the distinctive side 'griffs' originally built for instrument attachment, remains central to the brand's design identity to this day.

Linde Werdelin's watches are organised around two enduring universes: Oktopus, inspired by the sea and deep diving, and Spido, inspired by mountaineering and skiing. Both share a defining structural signature — true three-dimensional case skeletonisation, drilled and shaped to dramatically lighten the watch rather than merely exposing the movement through a display back, with direct design inspiration drawn from Formula 1 engineering. That racing influence surfaces most explicitly in the SpidoSpeed, whose dial material references 1940s racing-car dashboards, whose sub-dials carry small holes recalling brake discs, and whose strap is detailed with round cut-outs echoing driving gloves. Linde Werdelin has always taken a transparent, deliberately non-manufacture approach to movements, working instead with some of the finest independent names in watchmaking: the SpidoLite launched at Baselworld 2009 used a vintage movement modified by Svend Andersen, founder of the AHCI; the Oktopus Moonphase (2010, limited to 29 pieces) paired a movement built by Andersen with a moonphase complication designed by Frédéric Piguet, the complications specialist whose work also appears in Breguet; and the LW03 calibre powering the SpidoSpeed Chronograph, launched in 2011, was built exclusively for Linde Werdelin by independent Swiss manufacturer Concepto, under watchmaker Valérien Jaquet. In 2015, the Danish Frogman Corps — the elite combat-diver unit of the Danish Naval Special Operations Force — commissioned the Oktopus Frogman, built specifically for night diving with enhanced luminosity and still in active use by the unit today. Engraving work by renowned artist Johnny Dowell ('Kingnerd') produced the Oktopus Reef and Oktopus Crazy Universe in 2017, followed by the Oktopus Volcano in 2019.

In 2015, Linde Werdelin became the first luxury watch brand to take ownership responsibility for every watch it had ever produced, formally linking every seller with a buyer for its full back catalogue — a policy the brand frames as a natural extension of knowing more about its own watches than anyone else ever could. Across two decades the company has produced 43 distinct watch models and 10 instrument variants, selling roughly 5,000 watches in total, each model limited to between 22 and 222 individually numbered pieces. The brand celebrated its 20th anniversary in 2022 with the Oktopus Moon 3DTP Black Ink, and continues to develop and expand its interpretation of the skiing and diving universes that gave rise to the company in the first place.
