Alexandre Meerson

Alexandre Meerson is a third-generation watchmaker whose design house exists, in its own words, as an act of deliberate 'elegant disruption' against what it calls the dull world of grey men in grey suits selling icons that are decades old. His father, Emerich Meerson, was part of the post-war design renaissance in Europe, creating one of the first fashion watches, designing for Tiffany, H.Stern, and Mikimoto, and founding his own watch brand — but the relationship between father and son was a difficult one, and Alexandre left home at fifteen in search of his own destiny, determined to make watches that meant something rather than cash in on the fashion of the moment. Pierre Cardin, the designer who would become his mentor, once described the extraordinary creative freedom of the post-war period this way: 'I was very lucky, I was part of the post-war period when everything had to be re-done' — a sentiment Alexandre carried directly into his own approach to watch design, using the pain of his family history to challenge, rather than inherit, the accepted codes of the craft. He went on to work for LVMH, migrating luxury brands into the new online universe of the early internet era, before founding his own design studio, Art in Detail, where he pioneered the early use of technology in watches and wearable devices. It wasn't until he launched his own eponymous brand, however, that his true creative voice fully emerged — a purity of line and form that stood in startling contrast to haute horlogerie's often over-complicated design language, and one for which Meerson was widely lauded as a genuine disruptor within the industry.

Meerson watches are created in the House's French design studio and manufactured in Switzerland, with production deliberately restricted to just 150 to 200 timepieces per year — production levels decided at the end of each year for the one to follow, keeping the brand genuinely scarce by design rather than by marketing claim. Alexandre works directly with clients to create watches that tell their individual stories, describing his ownership community as like-minded individuals who recognise the genius hidden within the deceptively simple lines of his work; 'otherness,' the brand states plainly, is at the very core of why Meerson is special, because Alexandre himself remains fundamentally an outsider who refuses to follow what the House calls the 'copy economy' of watchmaking. That refusal extends to two distinct tiers of ownership. Collection Pieces are available in signature colours and finishes through Official Retailers and directly from the House, still produced in low annual series to preserve exclusivity. Unique Pieces represent the House's most exceptional offering: clients invite Alexandre into their own story, passions, and dreams, and he either explores and reinterprets the silhouettes of an existing collection or begins from a completely blank canvas — a process that can take years to reach completion, and that Alexandre describes not as making watches to sell, but as crafting purely for pleasure.

The current collection is built around four core lines, each with a distinct character: Altitude, described by the House as 'classic, reinvented'; the D15 MK-1 GMT, 'muscular and refined'; the Palatine, framed as 'the cocktail watch, French style'; and the Mutiny, 'the modern-classic all-rounder.' A further Solent reference explores its own distinct silhouette within the wider House language. Meerson also offers an Augmented Memories smartphone app, allowing prospective clients to explore the full collection in augmented reality from anywhere in the world. The House maintains retail presence across the USA, France, England, and Switzerland, alongside direct online ordering, and continues to expand into complementary categories including a House clock and dedicated women's collections — all while holding to the same founding ethos: to design, in true partnership with the client, the best watch they will ever wear.
