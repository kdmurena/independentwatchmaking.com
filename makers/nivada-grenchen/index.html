Nivada Grenchen was founded in 1926 in Grenchen, Switzerland, by Otto Wüllimann, Hermann Schindler, and Jakob Schneider, initially trading as Wüllimann, Schneider, Nivada SA. From the outset the house was among the pioneers of automatic watch manufacturing in Switzerland, building instrument watches valued for water resistance, chronograph function, and high-precision movements rather than dress-watch prestige; by 1939 it had earned enough standing in the industry to participate in national Swiss exhibitions. That same period brought U.S. expansion through a partnership with Croton Watch Co. Inc., founded in 1878, distributing under names including Croton Nivada and Croton Nivada Grenchen. In 1939 Movado challenged the similarity of the Nivada name, and after years of legal pressure the brand formally adopted Nivada Grenchen in 1958; by the 1960s it was distributed in more than 84 countries.

The watch that would define the brand began life as the Aquamatic, a rugged, waterproof automatic. Between 1955 and 1956, the U.S. Navy's Operation Deep Freeze I — a scientific mission led by Rear Admiral Richard E. Byrd, credited with the 1926 North Pole flight and a Medal of Honor recipient — used it to help establish a permanent American presence in Antarctica. Exposed to sub-zero cold and constant ice and water, the watch kept accurate time throughout, and Nivada renamed it Antarctic on the dial in tribute, making it one of the first wristwatches ever to reach and perform on the continent. The rugged evolution that followed, the 1958 Super Antarctic, added enhanced shock resistance and distinctive arrow hands and likewise accompanied later Deep Freeze expedition members.

In 1961 came the Chronomaster Aviator Sea Diver, nicknamed 'The World's Busiest Watch' and still regarded as the most iconic model in Nivada's history: a genuine multi-purpose 'Super Chronograph' combining a chronograph function, bidirectional rotating bezel, tachymeter scale, second time-zone indication, and regatta countdown, all while maintaining real water resistance for its era. The brand's deep-sea ambitions followed close behind: the 1964 Depthomatic was among the first automatic dive watches rated to 200 metres, and the 1965 Depthmaster pushed to 1,000 metres, among the earliest watches anywhere built for such depths, alongside the rotating-bezel Antarctic Diver the same year. Nivada was equally active in travel and complication watches through the decade: the Electric (mid-1960s) took its stylised, lightning-bolt hour markers from the era's fascination with electricity; the Wanderer GMT (1967) and Travelmaster (1967), later joined by the Travelmaster WT (1970) running the Vulcain Cricket Calibre 120, paired GMT and world-time functions with the renowned Vulcain Cricket mechanical alarm movement; the Alertamatic (1968) offered its own dedicated alarm function; and the Fiber Star, cased in the lightweight Space Age resin Noryl with interchangeable bezels and straps, targeted a younger, more accessible market in the early 1970s.

Introduced in 1976 as the Mustang 76 before its name settled as the F77, Nivada's answer to the era's integrated-bracelet sports watch combined an angular case, octagonal bezel, and stainless-steel bracelet with a sporty day-date display, produced through 1980 and later revived as a modern collection in its own right. The wider Quartz Crisis of the 1970s, compounded by the 1973 oil crisis, hit Nivada hard: its Solothurn production facilities closed in the late 1970s, and over the following decades the brand changed hands repeatedly and largely disappeared from the market, even as its vintage pieces remained actively sought by collectors.

Nivada Grenchen returned in 2018, relaunched by Guillaume Laidet and Rémi Chabrat with faithful reissues of the Chronomaster, Antarctic, and Depthmaster, amplified by social media to a new generation of collectors. Consistent with a century-old philosophy of never manufacturing its own movements but instead 'selecting the right calibre for every purpose,' Nivada has historically partnered with ETA, A. Schild, Phénix, Venus, and the Valjoux chronograph calibres, and today works with Soprod, Sellita, Landeron, and La Joux-Perret. The 2020s brought a steady stream of expansion: the Super Antarctic (2021) alongside a returning Depthmaster line, the Chronoking collection and F77 comeback (2023), and the revived Aquamar diver (2025), ahead of the brand's 100th anniversary in 2026. The relaunch has drawn coverage from Hodinkee, Fratello, Worn & Wound, Monochrome, Time and Tide, and Hypebeast, with current pieces priced from roughly 945 USD for the core Antarctic and Super Antarctic lines up to 2,500 USD and beyond for limited vintage-finish and complicated references.
