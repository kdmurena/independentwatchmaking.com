M.A. Meyer & Söhne Zeitgeneration, also written Zeitgeneration or Michael Albert Meyer Time Generation, is a small Austrian watch brand run by the master watchmaker Michael Albert Meyer from his atelier and sales point at Rathausplatz 53 in Weitra, in the Waldviertel region of Austria. Meyer says that his grandfather Albert Meyer's mechanical wristwatch inspired him to make modern mechanical watches for the next generation, and the brand's tagline is 'Time with Generations'. It is registered in the EU as M.A. MEYER & SÖHNE – ZEITGENERATION.

Meyer trained at the watchmaking school in Karlstein im Waldviertel. He then worked for many years for IWC and Patek Philippe, including more than four years in Schaffhausen designing and developing complicated watches with perpetual calendars, and spent eleven years as a watch specialist at the family jewellery business Juwelier Paul Koppenwallner in Salzburg. He also completed a TGP degree at the University of Education in Vienna, which qualifies him to teach at the Karlstein watchmaking school. According to the brand he began conceiving and planning his own brand in 2014 and developed it over four years in his atelier in Weitra, and he began selling his collection of limited-edition wristwatches in the Austrian market in 2018.

The brand's philosophy is that in an age of constant connectivity the mechanical watch still matters, both as everyday jewellery and as a store of value, and that its watches should stand apart from trends. It lists longevity, craftsmanship, exclusivity and quality among its values, and presents itself as a family business in which the master watchmaker works alongside family members and the next generation. The watches are mechanical, hand-wound unless stated otherwise, and produced in limited numbers, and each dial is handmade in the atelier so that no two are alike.

The collection uses a base caliber 6497 that the brand refines in-house with Geneva stripes, a beryllium-bronze balance wheel, large ruby bearings and hand-blued screws, visible through a sapphire display back. The models include the Hunter, Black Hunter and Green Hunter and the Klassik, in 42 mm or 43 mm stainless steel cases with hands in 18-carat white or rose gold. The Sixties and Sixties Champagne have blued hands, with a 42 mm case listed for the Champagne, and the Sixties Automatic is a 42 mm self-winding version described as a women's model. The Laguna has a deep blue dial. The Skyrock is the largest at 45 mm with luminous hands, and the Fliegeruhr aviator's watches in 42 mm have luminous hands for legibility. The Full Day, Full Day Klassik and the 24 Hours Worldtimer use a 24-hour movement based on the caliber 6498, with a two-part handmade 24-hour dial developed by Meyer that distinguishes day from night at a glance, and white gold-plated hands on the Worldtimer. The brand describes the Worldtimer as showing the world at a glance, around the clock, but its page does not describe time zones or city names. The Skeleton has a proprietary skeletonised movement based on the 6497, with DLC-coated bridges and plates and hands in blued steel or optionally 18-carat gold.

The brand's website lists no prices, water resistance, strap details or edition sizes, and invites buyers to enquire by email or phone about availability. The pages we read do not describe any servicing or repair offering, and no founding date is given beyond the 2014 and 2018 milestones above.
