Born in Valdagno, Italy, on 20 August 1974, Luca Soprana is the fourth generation of a family steeped in horological tradition. His grandfather Roberto was his first teacher, though as a child he was not allowed to touch the tools. He studied economics and business at the University of Bologna before training at WOSTEP at twenty-six, alongside Kari Voutilainen.

In 2002 he became WOSTEP's first Italian instructor. His career took in Vulcain, Vianney Halter (on the TRIO and Janvier N°1), BNB Concept (head of prototyping, including on the Concord Tourbillon Gravity), Patek Philippe and Rolex. Finding corporate hierarchies unsuitable, he then went alone, founding Atelier SMA (Soprana Micromécanique Appliquée) at Le Château 3 in Vaumarcus, in the shadow of Vaumarcus Castle above Lake Neuchâtel; visits are by appointment only. He has been described as 'one of those known-unknown watchmakers at the very apex of their craft' — the invisible backbone behind the Jacob & Co. Astronomia and the Massena LAB Old School, work that made him known in Japan through a manga series about the Astronomia collaboration. He is fluent in Italian, French and English.

His philosophy is 'a refusal of the superfluous, a culture of detail.' The atelier says every watch starts with the movement and the architecture of timekeeping, not marketing or surface decoration: 'Real human relationships are more important than marketing.' Most components are made in-house by traditional methods, with wheels, pinions, hands and hairsprings shaped, counted and finished by hand, along with pivots, balance staffs and chatons. Plates and bridges are ARCAP (non-ferrous, non-magnetic, thermally stable) with straight Geneva stripes and black-polished steel, and each movement is engraved directly with Luca Soprana's name, Vaumarcus and the serial number, never plated.

The first watch signed under his own name is the Soprana Time Only (Ref. LSTO), a 38.8 mm, 9.9 mm-thick three-hand dress watch drawn from early 20th-century tourbillon architecture, with sloping polished lugs and sapphire crystals front and back. Its sector dial has an outer minute track interrupted at nine and three by a polished centre field, with small seconds at six o'clock engraved 'Vaumarcus' and in-house flamed-blued lance steel hands. The hand-wound Calibre LSTO, designed and made in-house, beats at 18,000 vibrations per hour with a 12 mm free-sprung balance under a mirror-polished steel bridge, an in-house Breguet overcoil hairspring and a 42-hour power reserve from a single barrel. The latest releases are a steel case with a ruthenium dial (listed as sold out), a rose gold case with salmon, blue or silver dials, and a platinum case with ruthenium, salmon or ice blue dials. 2026 prices before taxes are CHF 68,000 in steel and CHF 82,000 in rose gold or platinum, on an alligator strap; about ten pieces are made a year, numbered from N° 01, with a waiting list.

The Nagoma, a 2026 collaboration with the knifemaker Emmanuel Esposito, is presented as a single vision in two handmade mechanical objects rather than a watch paired with a knife. It is built on the Time Only, in a 38.8 mm steel case under 10 mm thick, with a revised hand-wound LSTO calibre with a large balance wheel and a mirror-polished steel balance bridge, behind a black-lip mother-of-pearl mosaic of individually hand-sorted fragments, accompanied by a handmade Esposito knife with mother-of-pearl elements. It is extremely limited, each set is unique and bespoke, and the price is on enquiry.

The defining reference is the Derek Pratt Remontoir d'Égalité. Pratt, a contemporary of George Daniels known for his obsession with mechanical precision, died in 2009 without ever producing a wristwatch under his own name; years after first encountering his prototype, Soprana rebuilt it from the ground up with the blessing of Pratt's family. The hand-wound DP07 calibre brings Pratt's Reuleaux-triangle constant-force remontoir, coaxial with the escape wheel, from pocket watch to wrist for the first time: twin suspended barrels feed a one-second remontoir that drives deadbeat seconds at seven o'clock, with a large free-sprung balance and about 36 hours of power reserve. The case is 41.6 mm by 12.6 mm in 316L steel or platinum, with the crown engraved 'DP' and sapphire front and back, and three dial configurations (grey, silvered Roman and salmon Arabic), all signed 'Derek Pratt' on the dial itself. It is priced from CHF 135,000 plus taxes in steel, platinum on enquiry. GPHG 2025 finalist. A handful of pieces a year — each by hand, each after a conversation, with a waiting list.
