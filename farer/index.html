Farer is an independent British watch company founded in 2015 and driven by what it describes as a passion for detail, design, and most importantly, difference — a philosophy that runs through every decision the brand makes, from the palette of colours it uses on its dials to the movement supplier it chooses to work with. The brand is rooted in the conviction that it is not enough to simply make high-quality luxury watches: what the world needs is watches that bring something genuinely new to horology while remaining accessible. Every Farer is designed meticulously in London and then handmade, without compromise, in Switzerland, the combination that the brand summarises as 'British Design × Swiss Made.' To back this up, Farer offers a 60-month movement guarantee — five years, a commitment that is rare in any price category — alongside free worldwide shipping, free 30-day returns, complimentary engraving, and a direct-to-consumer distribution model that allows Farer to state, without exaggeration, that it has zero room to discount: the price on the website is always the best possible price.

The movement question is one that Farer has addressed with more transparency than most brands at its price level. After touring the La Joux-Perret factory in La Chaux-de-Fonds and speaking directly with the LJP technical team, Farer published a detailed account of what distinguishes the G100 calibre from the Miyota 9015 on which its development was based. The G100 features a completely re-engineered gear train, barrel, and balance, yielding a 68-hour power reserve — nearly three full days — versus 42 hours in the Miyota equivalent; a single-piece tungsten rotor for superior automatic winding compared to the Miyota's two-piece construction; a KIF anti-shock system (a trusted Swiss supplier that predates Rolex's in-house Paraflex) rather than the Miyota's Parashock; an ETACHRON-style fine adjustment regulator with a regulation screw; vertical rather than horizontal dial foot fixing; and a monobloc crown stem. Finishing options include Côtes de Genève, blued screws, and polished chamfers. The G100 is manufactured entirely in Switzerland, with La Joux-Perret producing its own mainplates, bridges, and rotors from raw metal — the same supplier that makes movements for Arnold & Son, Frédérique Constant, and Angelus. Farer uses the Soigné grade of the G101 (a variation of the G100 family), regulated to ±7 seconds per day, which it introduced in its Three Hand Series and has since expanded across more of the range.

The collection spans more than a dozen distinct styles across over 40 models, making Farer one of the most prolific independent British watch brands in terms of range breadth. The Lander is the brand's signature GMT, its flagship icon, currently in its fourth generation (Lander IV, 39.5mm, £1,375); it anchors the GMT and GMT Bezel collections. The Endeavour is Farer's Aqua Compressor dive watch, offered in a Titanium Series III variant at £1,250 — a tool watch built for performance under pressure. The Venture is the Three Hand Series III entry (£1,095), representing the accessible heart of the collection. The Roché II is Farer's World Timer, currently sold out, at £1,550. Beyond these signature models, the full collection encompasses: Racing Chronographs (a new addition); Pilot Series II watches; 35mm Cushion Case models for smaller wrists; regular GMT variants; Moonphase watches; the Integra collection; Lissom watches; Three Hand Series II; World Timers; Tonneau-case models; and the Aquamatic. The breadth of colour combinations across the dial and strap offerings is a particularly distinctive Farer characteristic, the brand committing to vivid, unconventional palettes in a market that often defaults to black, white, and navy.
