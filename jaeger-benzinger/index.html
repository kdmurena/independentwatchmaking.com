Jaeger & Benzinger

Germany

Official Site ↗ Instagram ↗

Founded in 2012 in Baden-Baden — one of Germany's most beautiful spa resort cities in the Black Forest — by Kay Jaeger and master craftsman Jochen Benzinger. Kay Jaeger brings decades of experience in fashion and design, is founder of Manufakturmesse (Germany's prestigious annual fine hand-made crafts fair), and spent 30 years as a passionate watch collector before co-founding this brand. Jochen Benzinger is Germany's foremost specialist in traditional decorative watchmaking — mastering hand guilloché, skeletonization, hand engraving, and enamelling; he also operates his own higher-end solo atelier 'Benzinger' in Pforzheim and creates limited editions for IWC, Glashütte Original, Chronoswiss, and others.

Jaeger & Benzinger represents the more accessible expression of Benzinger's extraordinary craft, starting from around $4,500. The collection combines classical aesthetics (Breguet numerals, historical touch-points) with 21st-century sensibility, using genuine engine-turned guilloché dials and hand-engraved movement components throughout. Every watch is built to order; because hand guilloché and engraving are never exact, every piece is genuinely unique. Even the packaging is hand-made. A rare example of German decorative watchmaking made attainable to a wider audience.

The engineering behind the decoration is more serious than the accessible pricing suggests. The Edition 1, for instance, runs on a heavily reworked ETA/Unitas 6498-1 stretched to fill a 36.6 mm case, beating at 18,800 vph with 53 hours of reserve, its dial carrying rose-engine sunray guilloché cut with a 48-bump rosette wheel across a rhodium-plated three-quarter plate, a free-hand-engraved half-bridge finished in rose gold with snail patterns, double snailing on the crown and ratchet wheels, perlage throughout, and a hand-engraved caseback border recording the maker and place of origin — with flame-blued spade, leaf, and counterweighted hands completing a watch collectors have called a feast for the senses. Benzinger, who has devoted some 45 years entirely to guilloché and engraving, uses ETA-based movements across both his mainline solo atelier work and the more accessible Jaeger & Benzinger line by design, reserving his rarer proprietary-movement projects — such as the 50-piece GAP 1 built in collaboration with Austria's Habring2, which sold through almost immediately and has already prompted talk of a sequel — for the Benzinger name alone. Owners describe a buying experience as personal as the watches themselves: hand-written guarantee cards in German, notes penned with a fountain pen, and roadshow demonstrations where Glashütte watchmakers from A. Lange & Söhne and Nomos have examined the finishing and offered their own admiration. For a fraction of what comparable hand-guilloché and hand-engraved work commands from Switzerland's grander houses, Jaeger & Benzinger delivers a genuinely artisanal German dress watch — proof that Baden-Baden's spa-town elegance and Pforzheim's engraving tradition can still be had without a six-figure price tag.
