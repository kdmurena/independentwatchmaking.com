Zero West

Zero West Watches is a British brand founded in early 2016 by two friends and watch enthusiasts, Graham Collins and Andrew Brabyn, in the coastal town of Emsworth, Hampshire, with a clear founding vision: to build a classic British watch brand celebrating great moments in history through innovative design and engineering. The brand's name is itself a piece of navigational history — derived from the zero meridian established at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich in 1884, the fixed reference point from which longitude is measured east and west across the globe; Greenwich's own coordinate, 0.00° W, gives the brand its name. That fascination with coordinates, dates, and precise historical reference points runs through every Zero West watch: dials frequently carry the specific latitude and longitude of a historic event, alongside its exact date, transforming each timepiece into what the brand calls a marker of a specific 'time and place.' After extensive evaluation of movement options, the founders chose to base their designs on the Swiss ETA range, and spent roughly a year developing a family of highly engineered, ergonomically optimised watches built to survive harsh military environments while remaining genuinely well styled — a brief drawn directly from the founders' backgrounds in aerospace and luxury product design and manufacturing.

Every Zero West case is fully machined from aerospace-grade materials on state-of-the-art multi-axis CNC machines for repeatable precision, then entirely hand-finished by time-served craftsmen to a flawless polish; a distinctive design signature runs through the collection in the way the case appears to bolt directly onto its lugs, rather than being machined as a single continuous piece, giving every Zero West watch an unmistakably industrial character. Sapphire crystals are custom-made to the brand's own specification with a double-matched convex curve to minimise optical distortion, with anti-reflective coating applied to the inner surface. Dials and hands are designed in-house, and all luminous elements use genuine Swiss Tritec X1 Super-LumiNova for maximum afterglow performance. After evaluating hundreds of commercially available straps and finding none that matched the ambition of the watches themselves, the team took the decision to design and make their own in-house, sourcing Horween and English bridle leathers and spending over a year perfecting cutting, skiving, saddle-stitching, and edge-finishing techniques. Every finished watch incorporates a robust sealing mechanism rated to a minimum of 10 ATM and is individually inspected and pressure-tested before leaving the Emsworth workshop.

The collection is organised into four lines, each built around a different strand of British engineering heritage. The Longitude Series pays homage to the history of navigation and marine chronometry: the L1 Longitude references John Harrison's H4 marine chronometer — six years in the making, and trialled from Portsmouth to Kingston, Jamaica in 1761 with an accuracy deviation of just five seconds — while the L2 Longitude Marine commemorates Hubert Scott-Paines' 1933 world-record 100mph hydroplane run on Southampton Water, its case sweeping into a vintage leather strap handmade in the Emsworth workshop. AirSpeed is the aviation-focused line, including the M1 Mosquito — its dial marking the coordinates of Amiens Prison, site of the Mosquito bomber raid of Operation Jericho that freed 120 French Resistance prisoners scheduled for execution, a mission later revealed to be part of the deception plan preceding the D-Day landings — and the ZW-S3, which recasts a disc of metal salvaged from the actual Rolls-Royce Merlin engine of Spitfire P9427, shot down over Poole Bay in 1940, into the watch's own caseback. hiOctane channels British motoring heritage, exemplified by the CR-1 Chronograph, inspired by the 1960s café racer motorcycle culture centred on London's Ace Café and the M1 motorway, complete with an Ace of Clubs symbol on its hour totaliser subdial and a Valjoux 7750 movement. The Z-Ops Editions form the brand's special projects division, acquiring rare historic material and transforming it into limited mechanical watches that preserve engineering achievement and human endeavour in wearable form.

Beyond its production collections, Zero West operates Q-Branch, a self-described research and development wing led by a self-taught engineer with twenty years' experience as a survival equipment fitter in the Royal Air Force. Q-Branch produces one-off engineering curiosities alongside the watch business: a Time Machine gearbox sculpture built over two years from aluminium, steel, and brass, its gear ratios so extreme that a full cycle of its mechanism would theoretically take billions of years to complete; a scale reproduction of an F-35 stealth fighter ejector seat, built after an invitation to the Martin-Baker Aircraft Company, the pioneering British firm behind ejector seat technology; and a spy-themed display case built around a vintage Samsonite case, a replica Walther PP pistol, and a miniature breathing apparatus concealed inside a cigar tube. Q-Branch is also developing in-house pad printing for watch dials and multiple-axis temperature testing to COSC (Contrôle officiel suisse des Chronomètres) limits, bringing an increasing share of traditional and contemporary horological skill directly in-house. Zero West watches are built entirely in England and sold direct from the maker, with visits to the Emsworth studio available by appointment.
