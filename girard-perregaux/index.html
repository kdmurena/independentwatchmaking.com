Girard-Perregaux of La Chaux-de-Fonds is one of the oldest fine watchmaking houses on earth — and, since a management buyout returned it to independent hands, once again a true independent. Its origins trace to 1791, when Jean-François Bautte signed his first timepiece in Geneva. Apprenticed at twelve, Bautte was a master engraver, watchmaker, jeweler, and goldsmith before his twentieth birthday, and when he opened his own company he gathered every métier under one roof — in effect inventing the watchmaking manufacture itself. His ultra-thin watches, perfume pistols concealing miniature timepieces, and watches shaped like flowers and butterflies captivated clients from Paris and Florence to China. The modern name arrived in 1856, when watchmaker Constant Girard married Marie Perregaux and united their names over the door. It was Girard who created the house's eternal signature: in 1867 he presented the first Three Bridges chronometer tourbillon at the Neuchâtel Observatory and the Paris Universal Exhibition, setting an Observatory precision record that stood unrivalled for seventeen years — and, more radically, structuring the movement around three visible parallel bridges, revealing what watchmaking had always concealed. He patented the architecture in the United States in 1884 and perfected it in 1889 as La Esmeralda, the legendary pocket watch with arrow-shaped bridges of solid gold that took the gold medal at the Paris Exposition Universelle. 'Making the invisible visible' has been the house creed ever since.

Few manufactures can match the string of firsts that followed. In 1965–66 Girard-Perregaux developed high frequency — 36,000 vibrations per hour, considered by many experts the last useful improvement to the mechanical watch — winning the Neuchâtel Observatory's Centenary Prize in 1966 (a date the dressy '1966' collection commemorates). In 1971 its quartz caliber established 32,768 Hz as the universal frequency standard every quartz watch on the planet still uses. In 1975 came the Laureato, the octagonal-bezel, integrated-bracelet sports watch designed entirely in-house, now in its fifth generation and the pillar of the modern catalogue. And after Luigi Macaluso revived the brand from 1992 — the era that produced the Three Bridges wristwatch (1994), the WW.TC world timer, and the Vintage 1945 collection of curved Art Deco rectangles — the house achieved perhaps its greatest modern invention: the Constant Escapement, which delivers unwavering energy to the regulating organ via a buckling silicon blade. Refined over twenty years (against the 250 it took to develop the Swiss lever escapement, the brand likes to note), it won the Aiguille d'Or — the GPHG's supreme prize — in 2013, and evolved in 2023 into the COSC-certified Neo Constant Escapement (from $105,000) carrying thirteen patents. The maison's exotic side flourished too: the Planetarium Tri-Axial (2017), the astronomical Cosmos (2019), and the 230th-anniversary La Esmeralda Tourbillon 'A Secret' Eternity Edition ($469,000), its case engraved in homage to 1889.

Owned by luxury conglomerate Kering from 2011 until 2022, Girard-Perregaux regained its freedom when CEO Patrick Pruniaux led a management buyout — the first of its kind in Swiss haute horlogerie history — exiting Kering together with sister manufacture Ulysse Nardin to become a privately owned, independent entity under the Sowind Group. Today it stands as one of the largest genuinely independent haute horlogerie manufactures in Switzerland, with roughly two centuries of uninterrupted production, in-house movement manufacture from mainplate to escapement, and a museum's worth of treasures behind its doors. Today's collections span the Laureato (including 2025's Laureato Three Gold Bridges, uniting the two icons in a 50-piece edition), the skeletal Bridges line where the 1867 architecture goes modern (Neo-Tourbillon, Neo Constant Escapement), the elegant 1966, the Art Deco Vintage 1945, the oval ladies' Cat's Eye, Grand Complications, and playful Legacy Editions like the revived 1970s Casquette 2.0 in ceramic and titanium. From Bautte's perfume pistols to buckling silicon blades, the through-line is intact: over 230 years of revealing the beauty of function.
