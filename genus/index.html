GENUS was founded in 2019 by Sébastien Billières and Catherine Henry, though Billières' watchmaking journey began decades earlier and reads like a tour of independent horology's inner circle. The son of a watchmaker, he earned his horology degree in 1999 and began his career working directly under Roger Dubuis, whose team then numbered barely ten people. In the earliest days of Urwerk, he became the first watchmaker to join Felix Baumgartner on prototyping, testing, and parts manufacturing for the Harry Winston Opus V — a model he remains personally responsible for servicing to this day. Eager to sharpen his craft further, he rented a workbench from Svend Andersen (himself already a fixture of this catalogue); that same bench had previously belonged to Baumgartner, and before him, to Franck Muller — a single piece of furniture connecting four generations of independent watchmaking's biggest names. Billières went on to specialize in tourbillons, automatons, and Geneva Seal (Poinçon de Genève) production; in 2006 the Geneva Foundation for Adult Education (IFAGE) gave him carte blanche to design two entirely new curricula, Modular Training in Watchmaking and Training in Industrial Quality, which he still teaches today. From 2007 his contract-manufacturing company grew to over twenty specialists, becoming the region's leading Poinçon de Genève assembly house, before he joined forces with Catherine Henry — a management sciences graduate and entrepreneur, now GENUS's Chief Operating Officer — to found GENUS in 2019, a brand whose watchword is simply 'Freedom.'

GENUS is dedicated to rethinking how time is experienced, abandoning conventional hands for dynamic, fluid, mechanical displays of extraordinary poetry. The GNS1.2 — ten years in development and protected by two patents — was a genuine world first: mechanical elements travel seamlessly from one orbit to another while still conveying the time, with a lead traveling indicator nicknamed 'the Genus' moving freely along a figure-8 (infinity, or analemma) center path to mark the tens of minutes, the precise minute shown on a rotating dial in the traditional 3 o'clock position, and hour indices revolving around the periphery, pivoting four times by a quarter-turn each to keep themselves oriented for reading. Case, movement mainplate, bridges, and complication module are all cast in ethical 18K gold certified by the Responsible Jewellery Council, hand-decorated and hand-finished entirely within the brand's own Geneva workshop. The GNS2 (2024) simplified the architecture to two trains and a closed dial, golden arrows gliding along a peripheral track and a central infinity loop; the in-house Calibre 260Rh-2 powering it is hand-wound, beats at 18,000vph, holds a 50-hour reserve across 278 parts and 26 jewels.

The collection has since branched into a genuine thematic universe, each execution offered across cases in grade 5 titanium, Damascene titanium, rose gold 4N, or white gold. The Nocturne (2025) reimagines the display in a mysterious nocturnal palette; the Infinity Blue and Launch Edition explore the arrow motif in titanium; the Dragon series (including a striking Dragon Blue in Damascene titanium) turns the peripheral indices into coiling mythical beasts; Time2Race channels motorsport energy into the same orbital architecture; and the Eternal Gallop — offered in both a standard titanium execution and a Masterpiece rose-gold edition — transforms the wandering hour display into a herd of galloping horses. A dedicated Gems line pushes furthest into pure jewelry territory, with the Rainbow, Rivière, and Diamonds & Sapphires references setting the orbital mechanism within fields of precious stones. From a single rented workbench in the shadow of watchmaking's greatest names, GENUS has built, in barely half a decade, one of Geneva's most visually distinctive independent houses.
